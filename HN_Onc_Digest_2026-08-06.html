1. Who gets a medical oncology consult before you book the OR

4 studies
Practice-changing

A same-day cost-effectiveness analysis gives the CPS≥10 argument real teeth — perioperative pembrolizumab is a reasonable value proposition only above that cutoff. This builds directly on last cycle's CPS≥10 discussion.

  • University of Michigan modeled the KEYNOTE-689 cohort: for CPS>10, perioperative pembrolizumab produced ~1.35 additional QALYs at an incremental cost of $181,900 (ICER $134,700). For CPS 1-10, the gain was ~0.08 QALYs at an ICER of $2,179,400.▸

    Key conclusion — First formal economic argument translating the CPS-stratified clinical signal into a hard value threshold: it converts "marginal benefit" into "not worth the price" for CPS 1-10, and "reasonable value" for CPS>10.

    Counterpoint — QALY inputs were borrowed from CheckMate 141 health-state utilities, not measured directly in KEYNOTE-689 patients, and even the "good" CPS>10 subgroup only had a 57% probability of being cost-effective at a $150,000 willingness-to-pay threshold (11% for CPS 1-10) — suggestive, not definitive, economics.

    Read the paper →

  • A new JCO commentary, "Notably Off Key," from a multi-institutional radiation oncology/surgery/med onc group, directly questions the clinical benefit of perioperative IO in resectable HNSCC after KEYNOTE-689.▸

    Key conclusion — A group spanning rad onc, surgery, and med onc across three institutions (Medical College of Wisconsin, Moffitt, UT Southwestern) argues the EFS benefit may not translate into meaningful clinical benefit once treatment-related delay and toxicity are weighed against it.

    Counterpoint — This is an interpretive commentary, not new trial data — it's an argument about how to read KEYNOTE-689's existing results, and it's part of an active back-and-forth (see the section counterpoint below) rather than a settled critique.

    Read the paper →

  • A companion JAMA Otolaryngology editorial discusses logistics of subcutaneous pembrolizumab and how it changes coordination of neoadjuvant scheduling with surgery.▸

    Key conclusion — A purely logistical shift worth knowing about: a subcutaneous formulation changes infusion-chair scheduling and could shorten the runway needed to coordinate neoadjuvant dosing with a surgical date.

    Counterpoint — Perspective piece with no efficacy or safety data of its own — an operations note, not a clinical-benefit claim.

    Read the paper →

  • A multidisciplinary expert consensus roadmap (15 international experts) ranked "personalized chemoradiation following perioperative immunotherapy" and "risk-adapted prognostic evaluation at diagnosis" as the top unmet needs in locally-advanced HNSCC.▸

    Key conclusion — Confirms the specialty itself — not just critics — sees the post-perioperative-IO treatment pathway as unresolved; this was a formal, weighted-ranking survey of international rad onc/med onc leaders, not an offhand opinion.

    Counterpoint — A priority-setting exercise (ranked expert survey), not a data readout — it tells you what trials should be designed next, not what the answer is.

    Read the paper →

Section counterpoint

A systematic review of 25 RCTs in resectable LA-HNSCC (industry-funded, Merck) found EFS improvement with perioperative pembrolizumab, adjuvant nivolumab, and several other regimens was broadly comparable to SoC on OS, with pathologic response used as an early surrogate — a reminder that "no OS signal yet" cuts both ways. 2. What's replacing your post-op surveillance imaging

4 studies
Practice-changing within trials

ctDNA-gated de-escalation has moved from "coming" to an actual open protocol, and the specialty society just weighed in formally. See also last cycle's ctDNA/MRD coverage.

  • MUSC's OPERATION trial (NCT07513389, recruiting) omits immediate postoperative radiation in patients with intermediate pathologic risk features and a negative two-week post-op ctHPVDNA (NavDx) result.▸

    Key conclusion — First trial found that actually withholds postop RT based on a negative ctDNA result rather than just proposing to someday — a real test of whether liquid biopsy can safely replace part of the current risk-stratification pathway.

    Counterpoint — Single-institution, phase II, only 36 patients planned — a pilot feasibility signal, not practice-changing evidence yet.

    View on ClinicalTrials.gov →

  • The American Head and Neck Society published its first formal review of ctDNA in JAMA Otolaryngology, addressing test variability and the implications of an inaccurate result.▸

    Key conclusion — A sign the technology has crossed from "interesting research tool" to "something the specialty society believes warrants a formal position" — useful as the single best current overview of where the field stands.

    Counterpoint — A review/position paper summarizing existing trials, not new primary data of its own.

    Read the paper →

  • A new customized 11-gene NGS tumor-informed ctDNA assay in HPV-negative locally-advanced HNSCC (n=40) found 74.3% pre-op / 37.1% post-op positivity using tumor-specific variants (vs. 20%/2.5% using pathogenic-variant-only interpretation); pretreatment positivity predicted worse progression-free survival.▸

    Key conclusion — How you define a "positive" result (tumor-specific variants vs. pathogenic-variant-only calling) changes detection rates several-fold — assay design choices, not just the underlying technology, will determine clinical utility.

    Counterpoint — n=40, single-center (Rouen, France); the PFS association was specifically tied to the lower-sensitivity pathogenic-variant definition, not the tumor-specific-variant definition used for the headline detection-rate numbers — the two metrics shouldn't be conflated.

    Read the paper →

  • SCREEN-HPV (NCT06528353, not yet recruiting, n=10,000) will test blood-based HPV16-E6 serology plus ctDNA for population-level screening of HPV-driven OPC.▸

    Key conclusion — A fundamentally different use case from the rest of this section — early detection in an asymptomatic population, not post-treatment surveillance — and notable simply for its scale (n=10,000).

    Counterpoint — Not yet recruiting; screening in HPV+ OPC has no current standard-of-care role, so this remains exploratory.

    View on ClinicalTrials.gov →

Section counterpoint

A broader liquid-biopsy systematic review flags persistent heterogeneity in isolation protocols and detection platforms (NGS vs. digital PCR) as the main barrier to clinical implementation. DOI

Go deeper

The AHNS review above is the best single starting point; for methodology depth, the NGS tumor-informed assay paper is the most rigorous primary dataset this cycle.

3. Where dissection extent is actually being questioned

4 studies
Hypothesis-generating

The level IIB omission data from last cycle just got directly contested in print, and a new systematic review offers a cleaner answer for a different subsite.

  • A letter, "Omission of Level IIB Dissection in cN0 HNSCC: Some Caveats!," directly challenges the meta-analysis on selective neck dissection cited last cycle; the original authors published a response defending their conclusions.▸

    Key conclusion — A direct, published disagreement about whether the pooled occult-metastasis rate in the meta-analysis we cited last cycle is genuinely low enough to justify omitting level IIB — read both sides before changing practice.

    Counterpoint — This is a letter-and-reply exchange, not new patient data — it's a methodological argument about interpreting the original meta-analysis, not an independent dataset.

    Read the letter →  ·  Read the response →

  • A cleaner signal in early-stage (cT1-2N0) supraglottic SCC: a 16-study review found occult nodal metastasis in 13-37.5% of patients, concentrated in level II (especially IIA, 75-85%), with levels I, IIB, IV, V rarely involved (<5%).▸

    Key conclusion — The cleanest data point this cycle: occult mets are concentrated almost entirely in level II/IIA for supraglottic primaries, so limiting dissection to levels II-III (omitting IV and IIB) preserved regional control in lateralized disease.

    Counterpoint — A systematic review of heterogeneous retrospective/population-based studies spanning 25 years (2000-2025); overall survival benefit from elective dissection itself was inconsistent across studies even though the nodal-yield pattern was consistent.

    Read the paper →

  • A prospective feasibility study of sentinel lymph node biopsy in gingivo-buccal SCC (n=39, India) reported 100% sensitivity/specificity for occult nodal disease.▸

    Key conclusion — A genuine less-morbid alternative signal in a population where elective SOND is currently the default despite low nodal positivity rates — if validated at scale, this could meaningfully reduce shoulder/spinal-accessory morbidity.

    Counterpoint — n=39, single-center; the authors themselves caution the small sample size limits how much weight to put on "100%" — a single false negative would meaningfully move that number.

    Read the paper →

  • A large NCDB analysis (n=5,569) used ML risk stratification after transoral surgery in HPV+ OPSCC to identify a low-risk group where adjuvant therapy conferred no measurable OS benefit.▸

    Key conclusion — Identifies a data-driven "truly low-risk" subgroup after transoral surgery — could eventually let more patients skip adjuvant treatment altogether, not just narrow the neck dissection itself.

    Counterpoint — Retrospective NCDB registry data; risk-group assignment and treatment-benefit estimates varied somewhat depending on which ML model (DeepSurv vs. NMTLR vs. RSF) was used — the authors call for prospective validation before withholding treatment based on this.

    Read the paper →

Section counterpoint

The SLNB gingivo-buccal series and the ML risk-stratification study (both above) are each individually underpowered or retrospective — encouraging directional signals, not yet grounds to change default management.

Go deeper

A systematic review/meta-analysis of TORS vs. definitive chemoradiation for OPSCC (15 studies, 6,767 patients) found better OS with TORS overall (HR 0.51) but no OS advantage specifically in HPV+ disease, and comparable dysphagia outcomes. DOI

4. Radiation de-escalation — a necessary reality check

3 studies
Background, tempering prior optimism

The umbrella review this cycle is a genuine counterweight to the ORATOR-driven enthusiasm from last digest.

  • An umbrella review of systematic reviews/meta-analyses (correcting for study overlap) found that across 55 studies (n=38,929), broad de-intensification strategies in HPV+ OPSCC were associated with worse OS (HR 1.33), PFS (HR 2.11), locoregional control (HR 2.51), and distant control (HR 1.90) vs. SoC.▸

    Key conclusion — The most rigorous synthesis yet on HPV+ de-escalation, and it's a genuine yellow flag: broad de-intensification (not just cetuximab substitution) trends toward worse survival and disease control across the pooled literature, after correcting for double-counting of overlapping meta-analyses. Cetuximab substitution specifically showed inferior OS (HR 2.83) and LRC (HR 2.78) without a significant late-toxicity benefit — reinforcing the RTOG 1016 story from last cycle.

    Counterpoint — "De-intensification" pools together very different strategies (dose reduction, drug substitution, volume reduction, surgery-alone) with different risk profiles — the pooled hazard ratios shouldn't be read as "all de-escalation is bad," just that the blanket approach lacks a selection biomarker.

    Read the paper →

  • The same review notes trial-status updates: ADEPT (post-TORS adjuvant de-intensification) closed early, ECOG-E3311 long-term follow-up published in 2025, PATHOS phase III enrollment was completing as of 2025.▸

    Key conclusion — The three trials actually designed to answer the post-TORS de-escalation question are all now mature or maturing — real Level 1 evidence on this exact question should start reading out over the next digest cycles.

    Counterpoint — Status note, not a data point yet — worth tracking, not yet actionable.

  • A Lancet letter exchange debates the evidence base for proton beam therapy in oropharyngeal cancer.▸

    Key conclusion — A live, published disagreement about whether current comparative evidence justifies proton therapy for oropharyngeal cancer outside of trials — worth knowing both sides before a patient asks about it.

    Counterpoint — Correspondence, not a trial — reflects an ongoing evidence gap rather than new data on either side.

    Read the letter →  ·  Read the reply →

Section counterpoint

The umbrella review itself cautions that ctDNA-gated RT omission (Section 2) wasn't powered in this pooled analysis and may behave differently than blanket dose reduction or drug substitution. Read this as "de-escalation needs a selection biomarker," not "de-escalation is dead."

Go deeper

The umbrella review is the single best current synthesis — it explicitly re-analyzes overlap across prior meta-analyses rather than double-counting the same trials. DOI

5. Cutaneous SCC: guidelines catch up, dosing questions open up

4 studies
Practice-changing

The European cSCC guidelines got a formal two-part 2026 update, and new real-world data suggest less immunotherapy may be enough for responders. See also last cycle's cSCC coverage.

  • EADO/ESTRO/EORTC published an updated two-part consensus guideline (diagnostics/prevention, and treatment) for invasive cSCC.▸

    Key conclusion — The first formal guideline refresh since 2023 — codifies a new "difficult-to-treat CSCC" 5-group classification and confirms adjuvant cemiplimab after surgery+RT in high-risk disease now belongs in the tumor-board conversation.

    Counterpoint — Consensus guideline (expert panel), not a new trial — largely formalizes evidence already covered in our prior cycle (C-POST, etc.) into an actual practice document rather than adding new data.

    Read Part 1 →  ·  Read Part 2 →

  • A Bayesian causal analysis from Mass Eye and Ear/MGH (the FIRST study, n=189) found nearly half of patients received ≤2 doses of checkpoint inhibition, and among 50 complete responders managed without surgery, only 1 recurrence occurred over a median 25.4-month follow-up.▸

    Key conclusion — Real-world evidence that durable disease control is achievable with far less immunotherapy exposure than typical fixed-duration neoadjuvant protocols, and without routine surgical consolidation. Benefit was concentrated early (2 vs. 1 dose), with uncertain incremental value beyond 2 doses.

    Counterpoint — Retrospective, Bayesian-modeled, single-institution cohort — treatment decisions were response-guided by treating physicians, not randomized, so this describes what happened, not what's proven optimal.

    Read the paper →

  • A second international case series (n=112) benchmarks craniofacial resection for advanced anterior skull base cSCC: 5-year OS 52.3%, intracranial extension the strongest independent predictor of worse survival (HR 6.54).▸

    Key conclusion — A modern surgical benchmark for the most advanced cSCC — useful for judging whether newer immunotherapy-first sequencing is actually beating historical surgical results in your own patients.

    Counterpoint — Historical cohort (1995-2015) predating routine immunotherapy access — not a fair head-to-head comparator for current IO-based approaches, only a baseline.

    Read the paper →

  • HLX07, a novel anti-EGFR antibody, showed a 60% ORR at the 1000mg dose level (vs. 19% at the higher 1500mg dose) in a phase II trial of advanced cSCC.▸

    Key conclusion — A new anti-EGFR antibody shows a real signal in advanced cSCC — an emerging non-IO option worth knowing about for IO-ineligible or IO-refractory patients.

    Counterpoint — The response rate was paradoxically higher at the lower dose (60% at 1000mg vs. 19% at 1500mg) in small, non-randomized cohorts (21 vs. 10 patients) — this inverse dose-response is unexplained and could easily be a small-sample artifact rather than a true biological effect. A larger trial is needed before drawing conclusions.

    Read the paper →

Section counterpoint

The FIRST study and HLX07 trial above are both real-world/small-sample signals, not confirmatory trials — treat both as hypothesis-generating rather than protocol-ready.

Go deeper

The EADO/ESTRO/EORTC Part 2 guideline is the most current, broadly-endorsed treatment algorithm; last cycle's Kreft et al. review remains the best discussion of neoadjuvant-vs-surgery-first sequencing.

6. Systemic pipeline for recurrent/metastatic disease

5 items
Background / pipeline

Several new trials are testing next-generation ADCs and bispecifics in combination with checkpoint inhibition, largely in the neoadjuvant/1L R-M space.

  • AbbVie's telisotuzumab adizutecan (a c-Met-directed ADC) + pembrolizumab is entering randomized phase II as first-line therapy for recurrent/metastatic HNSCC (NCT07744126, not yet recruiting, n=150).▸

    Key conclusion — A new target class (c-Met-directed ADC) entering the HNSCC combination space for the first time in our tracking.

    Counterpoint — Not yet recruiting; phase II with no efficacy data yet.

    View on ClinicalTrials.gov →

  • Becotatug vedotin (MRG003, an EGFR-directed ADC) combined with PD-1 inhibitors (toripalimab or pucotenlimab) is being tested in three separate Chinese trials spanning neoadjuvant PD-L1+ resectable disease, locally recurrent resectable disease, and hypopharyngeal SCC specifically (NCT07677475, NCT07632339, NCT07665190).▸

    Key conclusion — An EGFR-ADC-plus-IO class worth watching alongside ficerafusp alfa and petosemtamab — a broad, fast-moving development program testing the same drug across three distinct clinical scenarios simultaneously.

    Counterpoint — All early-phase (phase II), Chinese single/limited-site trials; cross-trial comparison to Western EGFR-bispecific programs isn't possible yet.

    NCT07677475 →  ·  NCT07632339 →  ·  NCT07665190 →

  • University of Chicago is testing induction cemiplimab + chemotherapy with or without fianlimab (a LAG-3 inhibitor) in locoregionally advanced HNSCC (NCT07346196).▸

    Key conclusion — The first LAG-3 inhibitor combination flagged in this space — tests whether dual checkpoint blockade (PD-1 + LAG-3) improves induction responses ahead of definitive local therapy.

    Counterpoint — Not yet recruiting (start date pushed to 2027); LAG-3 combinations have had mixed results in other tumor types (e.g., melanoma), so extrapolation to HNSCC is speculative.

    View on ClinicalTrials.gov →

  • A review from an Italian/European group synthesizes current immunotherapeutic strategies across HPV+/HPV- recurrent/metastatic OPSCC, covering PD-1 blockade, EGFR-directed bispecifics, and therapeutic HPV vaccines.▸

    Key conclusion — The best current single-source synthesis of where EGFR-bispecifics (ficerafusp alfa, petosemtamab, amivantamab), PD-1 blockade, and therapeutic HPV vaccines stand for recurrent/metastatic OPSCC, split cleanly by HPV status.

    Counterpoint — Narrative review, not a data source in itself — useful for orientation, not for citing specific efficacy numbers.

    Read the paper →

  • A systematic review of HPV therapeutic vaccine preclinical data notes there are still no FDA-approved therapeutic vaccines for HPV-associated cancers, despite two decades of prophylactic vaccine availability.▸

    Key conclusion — Useful for setting patient expectations when this comes up in clinic: prophylactic vaccines (which prevent infection) are not the same as therapeutic vaccines (which treat existing disease), and the latter remain entirely investigational.

    Counterpoint — Preclinical models only; no late-phase human efficacy data yet.

Bottom line for tumor board

The CPS≥10 argument that closed out last cycle's digest just picked up hard economic backing — a same-day cost-effectiveness analysis shows perioperative pembrolizumab is a reasonable value proposition above that cutoff and a poor one below it, while yet another commentary questions the whole KEYNOTE-689 premise. At the same time, this cycle is a genuine correction on the de-escalation side: a large umbrella review shows blanket HPV+ de-intensification underperforms standard of care, and the level IIB neck dissection omission data got directly contested in print. The throughline is the same as last time — the field is converging on "de-escalate/intensify by biomarker, not by disease category" — but ctDNA is now closer to doing that job for real, with an actual trial (OPERATION) omitting postop RT based on a negative post-op liquid biopsy rather than just proposing to. Nothing here should change what you do in the OR this month, but the CPS-stratified consult conversation and the "which de-escalation trial is my patient eligible for" conversation both got more concrete.
