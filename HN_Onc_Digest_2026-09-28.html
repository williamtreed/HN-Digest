1. Early oral cancer and response-adapted local therapy move toward comparative testing

3 findings
Hypothesis-generating

A new phase III protocol compares neoadjuvant sintilimab plus chemotherapy with upfront surgery in cT2N0 oral SCC. Two retrospective cohorts suggest response can influence local-treatment selection, but none validates off-protocol surgical de-escalation. This advances the prior response-adapted-surgery question.

  • A 300-patient phase III will compare neoadjuvant sintilimab plus chemotherapy with upfront surgery in cT2N0 oral SCC.▸

    Key conclusion — The protocol asks the correct randomized sequencing question; both arms retain radical surgery.

    Counterpoint — Not yet recruiting, no outcomes, and no validated response-based reduction in primary or neck surgery.

    Evidence briefClinicalTrials.gov · first posted Sep 18 · no results

    A superiority trial against the actual surgical standard

    Protocol: NCT07827872 randomizes resectable cT2N0M0 oral SCC to sintilimab 200 mg, nab-paclitaxel 260 mg/m², and carboplatin AUC 5 every three weeks before radical resection and neck dissection, or upfront radical surgery.

    300Planned enrollment
    Phase IIIRandomized open label
    3-year EFSPrimary endpoint

    Outcomes: pCR, MPR, OS, radiographic response, adverse events, and pathologic nodal positivity are secondary; estimated primary completion is October 2032.

    Surgical interpretation: The design can test whether adding systemic therapy before an otherwise standard operation improves outcome.

    Limitations/do not overread: No efficacy, safety, delay, margin, or functional results. Do not alter cT2N0 management on registration alone.

    View the registry record →

  • Neoadjuvant ICI in 23 temporal-bone SCC patients produced heterogeneous response and changed local therapy in four.▸

    Key conclusion — Six complete and four partial responses were observed, but ten patients progressed.

    Counterpoint — Retrospective, mixed primary/cutaneous tumors, and no comparator for de-escalation.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · two-center retrospective cohort

    Response can change the operation; progression remains common

    Methods/scope: Twenty-three patients with temporal-bone-involving SCC received neoadjuvant immune-checkpoint inhibition and response assessment before local treatment.

    6Complete responses
    4Partial responses
    10Progressive disease
    4Local plans reduced

    Results: RECIST CR was 26.1%, PR 17.4%, SD 13.0%, and PD 43.5%. Two resectable patients moved to nonsurgical management and two moved from lateral temporal-bone resection to mastoidectomy. Three-year OS was 100% among CR/PR/SD versus 78% with PD (p=.029; reported HR 13.2, 95% CI 1.3–134).

    Surgical interpretation: Preserve a prompt operation for nonresponders and document the baseline resection before therapy.

    Limitations/do not overread: Twenty-three selected patients, treatment heterogeneity, and a very wide survival interval. This does not prove surgery or RT can be omitted.

Response is influencing local-treatment selection, but none of these sources validates an operation-reduction algorithm outside a trial.

2. Neck and reconstruction: count nodes carefully, protect function, and avoid fluid excess

4 findings
Practice-relevant

Strict imaging criteria can identify positive HPV+ nodes without reproducing pathologic burden; nerve preservation does not prevent early accessory-nerve dysfunction; higher fluid volume tracks with complications; and multidomain geriatric assessment predicts morbidity better than age alone.

  • Blinded imaging review in 292 HPV-positive TORS patients separated strictly positive from merely suspicious nodes.▸

    Key conclusion — Strictly positive nodes had 0.94 sensitivity and 0.98 PPV; adding suspicious nodes lowered specificity to 0.57.

    Counterpoint — A selected single-center TORS cohort cannot validate imaging as a pathology substitute.

    Evidence briefPubMed abstract · blinded retrospective imaging-pathology cohort

    “Suspicious” is not a pathologic node count

    Methods/scope: Head-and-neck radiologists blinded to pathology re-read preoperative imaging in 292 HPV-positive OPSCC patients treated with TORS from 2016–2024.

    Results: Clinical-pathologic count correlation was 0.70. Strictly positive nodes yielded 0.94 sensitivity and 0.98 PPV; adding suspicious nodes raised sensitivity to 0.96 but lowered specificity to 0.57. With three radiographic positive nodes, 5% had five or more pathologic nodes (OR 1.24; p=.58). Accuracy was 0.91 in level II and 0.74 in level III.

    Surgical interpretation: Strict imaging can support candidacy discussion, but suspected-node inflation should not dictate adjuvant assignment.

    Limitations/do not overread: Retrospective, selected for TORS, and no external validation. Imaging does not replace neck pathology.

    Read the paper →

  • Accessory-nerve conduction fell substantially after nerve-preserving neck dissection and only partly recovered by three months.▸

    Key conclusion — CMAP amplitude fell 45.2% at three weeks despite nerve preservation.

    Counterpoint — Small cohort, six-patient modified-radical subgroup, and no validated shoulder endpoint.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · prospective physiologic cohort

    An intact nerve can still be a dysfunctional nerve

    Methods/scope: Forty-three oral-cavity SCC patients underwent nerve-preserving selective (n=35) or modified-radical (n=8) neck dissection.

    Results: In 41 unilateral cases, CMAP fell from 9.15±2.04 mV to 5.01±2.47 mV at three weeks (−45.2%; p<.0001), with partial recovery to 6.26±2.23 mV at three months (p<.001). Modified-radical asymmetry remained numerically worse but was not significant.

    Surgical interpretation: Counsel and rehabilitate for shoulder dysfunction even when the nerve is preserved.

    Limitations/do not overread: Short follow-up, adjuvant therapy not modeled, and physiology is not a validated functional outcome. Do not use it against an oncologically indicated dissection.

    Read the paper →

  • In 826 oral-cavity free flaps, higher fluid volume—not vasopressor use—tracked with medical and flap complications.▸

    Key conclusion — Each additional liter was associated with OR 1.33 for medical and 1.30 for flap complications.

    Counterpoint — Retrospective practice evolution and broad flap-event confidence intervals limit causal inference.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · retrospective weighted cohort

    Avoid reflexive fluid loading solely to avoid vasopressors

    Methods/scope: Eight hundred twenty-six oral-cavity free-flap cases from 2008–2019 were analyzed with inverse-probability weighting.

    Results: Vasopressors were not associated with medical complications (OR 1.36; 95% CI 0.76–2.43) or flap complications (OR 2.24; 0.66–7.57). Each additional liter of fluid was associated with medical complications (OR 1.33; 1.17–1.52) and flap complications (OR 1.30; 1.06–1.59).

    Surgical interpretation: Favor a hemodynamic strategy that limits unnecessary volume rather than presuming vasopressor avoidance protects the flap.

    Limitations/do not overread: Confounding by indication, time trends, and low event rates. The data do not prove every vasopressor regimen is safe.

    Read the paper →

  • A prospective geriatric assessment predicted major morbidity after curative oral-cancer surgery better than age alone.▸

    Key conclusion — Multidomain high-risk status carried OR 6.03 for major morbidity; chronological age did not.

    Counterpoint — Seventy-four patients, wide confidence interval, and no evidence that assessment-guided intervention improves outcomes.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · prospective risk-stratification cohort

    Assess domains; do not use age as the gatekeeper

    Methods/scope: Seventy-four patients aged ≥70 underwent an eight-domain assessment before curative oral SCC surgery; deficits in at least two domains defined high risk.

    Results: Thirty-four (46%) were high risk. Major 30-day morbidity occurred in 17 (23%), including three deaths (4.1%). High-risk status predicted major morbidity (OR 6.03; 95% CI 1.10–33.04; p=.039); age did not (OR 0.70; 0.16–3.08; p=.64). No single domain remained independently predictive.

    Surgical interpretation: Use multidomain assessment for prehabilitation, consent, and postoperative support—not to deny curative surgery by age.

    Limitations/do not overread: Single center, imprecision, and no external validation. High risk is not a contraindication.

    Read the paper →

Section counterpoint

These studies refine counseling and workflow but are not randomized technique comparisons. Apply them as risk signals, not absolute eligibility or reconstruction rules.

Go deeper

The 826-patient free-flap cohort is the most operationally useful source; the prospective accessory-nerve study is the clearest reminder that anatomic preservation and functional preservation are different endpoints.

3. Postoperative radiotherapy delay is the modifiable hazard this cycle

2 findings
Workflow-changing

The six-week PORT target was missed in most laryngectomy and oral-cavity pathways. The laryngectomy study links increasing delay with mortality; the oral-cavity study identifies length of stay and readmission as practical coordination targets.

  • Only 21.2% of 6,079 pT4 laryngectomy patients began PORT within 42 days.▸

    Key conclusion — Each extra week of delay was associated with a 3.7% higher adjusted mortality risk.

    Counterpoint — Registry associations cannot separate delay from complications, access, and disease severity.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · National Cancer Database cohort

    Make the adjuvant pathway part of the operation

    Methods/scope: Adults with nonmetastatic pT4 laryngeal SCC underwent total laryngectomy from 2010–2022; delay was 43–180 days and omission meant no PORT within 180 days.

    Observed PORT timing

    ≤42 days21.2%
    43–180 days66.2%
    Omitted12.6%

    Digest-generated chart from the cited abstract; not a publisher figure.

    Results: Radiation at a facility different from the operating institution was associated with delay; older age, comorbidity, and lower neighborhood income were associated with omission.

    Surgical interpretation: Pre-book referral, dental/pathology readiness, and early wound review before discharge.

    Limitations/do not overread: Residual confounding and selection effects. The association does not prove every week directly causes mortality.

    Read the paper →

  • In an integrated system, 89.3% of 252 oral-cavity patients started PORT later than six weeks.▸

    Key conclusion — Median time was 8.57 weeks; length of stay was the only significant univariate predictor.

    Counterpoint — Prediction in one system does not establish causation or survival effect.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · integrated-system retrospective cohort

    Discharge and readmission are visible leverage points

    Methods/scope: Two hundred fifty-two resected oral-cavity cancer patients received PORT in Kaiser Permanente Southern California from 2015–2025.

    Results: 225/252 (89.3%) started after six weeks; median time was 8.57 weeks (IQR 2.86). Random-forest modeling ranked length of stay, margin status, concurrent systemic therapy, and 60-day readmission highest; only length of stay was significant in univariate analysis.

    Surgical interpretation: Close the loop on margin finalization, discharge barriers, readmission prevention, and radiation handoff before the patient leaves surgery.

    Limitations/do not overread: One system, no external validation, and no survival model. Variable importance is not a causal intervention effect.

    Read the paper →

Section counterpoint

PORT delay is partly a marker of complications and access barriers. Redesign the pathway while avoiding causal overstatement.

Go deeper

The 6,079-patient laryngectomy analysis provides the clearest risk signal; the oral-cavity cohort is more useful for locating modifiable workflow bottlenecks.

4. Cutaneous SCC: parotid restraint and more precise perineural-spread imaging

3 findings
Selective application

A small cN0 cohort argues against routine prophylactic parotidectomy when morbidity is substantial; MRI neurography closely tracked nerve involvement but was less reliable for detailed skull-base zoning; anti-PD-1 therapy produced durable imaging control in a selected perineural-spread series.

  • Prophylactic parotidectomy found one occult parotid metastasis among 26 cN0 face/scalp cSCC patients and added substantial morbidity.▸

    Key conclusion — Complications were 65.4% versus 5.0%; five-year RFS and DSS did not differ.

    Counterpoint — Forty-six patients, one occult parotid event, and strong selection uncertainty.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · retrospective cohort

    Parotid-sparing surveillance is reasonable to discuss in cN0 disease

    Methods/scope: Twenty-six patients underwent prophylactic parotidectomy and 20 were observed.

    3.8%Occult parotid disease
    19.2%Occult neck disease
    65.4%PP complications
    42.3%Facial-nerve paralysis

    Results: Complications were 65.4% versus 5.0% (p<.001); five-year RFS (p=.971) and DSS (p=.911) did not differ.

    Surgical interpretation: Avoid treating prophylactic parotidectomy as routine in a clinically node-negative face/scalp primary.

    Limitations/do not overread: Small, retrospective, two decades, and no randomized risk balance. Do not omit parotid treatment when clinical or pathologic risk is high.

    Read the paper →

  • 3T MRI neurography closely tracked histologic nerve involvement in 70 cSCC patients but was less reliable for fine zone-2 mapping.▸

    Key conclusion — Reported PPV was 98%; zonal agreement fell to 50% for 2a and 40% for 2b.

    Counterpoint — A surgically enriched cohort means PPV is not population screening performance.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · retrospective imaging-pathology correlation

    Trust detection more than fine skull-base zoning

    Methods/scope: Seventy patients contributed 90 suspected involved nerves for MRI-histology comparison.

    Results: Two MRI-positive nerves were histology-negative (PPV 98%); three MRI-negative nerves were histology-positive, with reported 97% positive agreement. Zonal agreement was 83% by Williams and 69% by the modified system; modified-zone agreement was 50% for 2a and 40% for 2b.

    Surgical interpretation: Use MRI confidently to identify involved nerves but preserve uncertainty when resection extent depends on fine zone-2 subdivision.

    Limitations/do not overread: Enriched sample, limited zonal subgroups, and positive agreement is not sensitivity.

    Read the paper →

  • Nine cSCC patients with perineural spread had durable MRI control after anti-PD-1 therapy, including one pseudoprogression.▸

    Key conclusion — Three complete and five partial responses were observed; none progressed at 37 months' median follow-up.

    Counterpoint — Nine selected patients plus a narrative, not systematic, literature review.

    Evidence briefPubMed abstract · Sep 14 paper indexed this cycle

    Early MRI worsening may not mean failure

    Methods/scope: Nine patients with cSCC perineural spread underwent serial MRI after anti-PD-1; seven had zone 3 intracranial involvement.

    Results: Best response was CR in three, PR in five, and SD in one; no perineural progression occurred at median 37 months. One patient had pseudoprogression. The narrative review of 101 reported patients found CR 35%, PR 43%, SD 9%, and PD 10%.

    Surgical interpretation: Review early MRI worsening with symptoms and interval imaging before declaring failure or abandoning an organ-preserving strategy.

    Limitations/do not overread: Retrospective selection, heterogeneous regimens, and no surgical comparator. Imaging response does not prove eradication along the nerve or define surgery omission.

    Read the paper →

Section counterpoint

The parotid and perineural-spread data point toward selective treatment, not blanket omission. Burden, named-nerve anatomy, salvageability, and surveillance reliability remain decisive.

Go deeper

The MRI-histology correlation is the strongest anatomy study; use the anti-PD-1 imaging series primarily to recognize response lag and pseudoprogression.

5. Systemic therapy: CPS <1 caution, setting-specific vaccine activity, and phase III accrual complete

5 findings
Practice-informing

The CPS <1 meta-analysis raises a credible harm signal but remains subgroup-level evidence. PDS0101 activity was confined to ICI-naïve disease; amivantamab plus pembrolizumab produced a high first-line response rate in a small uncontrolled cohort. Both LiGeR trials now show completed accrual and no results.

  • A seven-trial Bayesian meta-analysis found a high probability that ICI-based therapy worsened outcomes in PD-L1 CPS <1 HNSCC.▸

    Key conclusion — In recurrent/metastatic disease, Bayesian OS HR was 1.42 with 95.6% probability that HR exceeded 1.0.

    Counterpoint — Aggregate trial subgroups, mixed settings/endpoints, and no patient-level interaction analysis.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · Bayesian/frequentist meta-analysis

    “Eligible for ICI” is not the same as “likely to benefit”

    Methods/scope: Seven phase III randomized trials contributed 630 CPS <1 patients.

    Reported Bayesian results
    Setting/outcomeHR (95% CrI)P(HR>1)

    Locally advanced DFS/PFS/EFS1.19 (0.72–2.06)76.8%

    Results: The frequentist R/M OS HR was 1.46 (95% CI 1.17–1.83; p<.001).

    Surgical interpretation: Make CPS <1 a deliberate multidisciplinary risk-benefit discussion and document non-ICI alternatives.

    Limitations/do not overread: Subgroup-level aggregation, few trials, mixed endpoints, and possible reporting/interaction limitations. This does not prove every CPS <1 patient is harmed or replace setting-specific guidance.

    Read the paper →

  • PDS0101 plus pembrolizumab produced a 34% ORR in ICI-naïve HPV16-positive disease and no responses after ICI resistance.▸

    Key conclusion — Activity was setting-specific; the ICI-resistant primary endpoint was not met.

    Counterpoint — Nonrandomized cohorts with different eligibility and no pembrolizumab control.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · multicenter phase II nonrandomized trial

    Do not extrapolate the naïve signal into salvage

    Methods/scope: Eighty-eight enrolled, 87 treated, and 75 entered mITT efficacy analysis: 53 ICI-naïve CPS ≥1 and 22 ICI-resistant.

    Results: ICI-naïve ORR was 34.0%, median PFS 5.3 months (95% CI 2.10–9.00), and median OS 39.3 months (23.90–NE). The resistant cohort had zero objective responses and median OS 14.8 months (8.50–26.00). Grade ≥3 treatment-related events occurred in 13.8%.

    Surgical interpretation: Consider this a first-line vaccine research strategy, not a post-ICI rescue option.

    Limitations/do not overread: No randomized control, smaller efficacy set, and demographically narrow cohort. OS cannot be attributed to PDS0101.

    Read the paper →

Section counterpoint

The strongest completed evidence is a subgroup meta-analysis that raises caution, while the response cohorts are uncontrolled and the phase III registries have no outcomes. Setting and comparator matter more than headline response rate.

Go deeper

The CPS <1 meta-analysis is the most practice-informing source; read its credible intervals and subgroup design before treating the harm probability as an individual-patient rule.

Bottom line for tumor board

This cycle changes workflow more than treatment. Build the adjuvant plan before laryngectomy or oral-cavity resection so wound, dental, pathology, and referral bottlenecks do not consume the six-week PORT window. For cN0 face/scalp cSCC, individualize prophylactic parotidectomy against its observed morbidity; for named-nerve spread, use MRI confidently for detection but cautiously for fine zone-2 mapping and early post-ICI worsening. In HPV-positive OPSCC, do not convert radiographic “suspicious” nodes into a pathologic count. In recurrent/metastatic disease, make CPS <1 a deliberate risk-benefit discussion, recognize that PDS0101 activity did not extend to ICI-resistant disease, and wait for randomized confirmation of amivantamab and petosemtamab strategies. The new cT2N0 oral-cancer phase III is a trial opportunity—not a reason to change upfront surgery today.
