## Highlights — key takeaways

- **Neoadjuvant cemiplimab has durable phase II follow-up, but still no randomized surgery-omission evidence.** At 54 months, no new recurrences had appeared; none of 40 patients with pCR had recurred, while response-group comparisons remain post-treatment and nonrandomized.
- **Two pT1–2 pN1 oral-cancer cohorts disagree about PORT.** One 85-patient analysis found no adjusted OS or regional-control association; a 58-patient cohort found improved RFS only after propensity matching reduced the analysis to 30 patients.
- **Margins remain the clearest salvage and orbital-invasion signal.** PATH outperformed rpTNM in one salvage cohort but failed to reproduce an ordered four-stage gradient; an exenteration meta-analysis lacked a direct orbit-preservation comparator.
- **Imaging and nodal metrics refine risk without dictating an operation.** CT and MRI had similar pooled performance for jaw invasion, while weighted lymph-node ratio was the strongest of three quantitative nodal measures in one retrospective series.
- **Care delivery changed timelines more convincingly than tumor control.** Navigation shortened median surgery-to-RT time by 11 days without a DFS difference; ERAS shortened length of stay and opioid use, and proton therapy reduced selected acute toxicities without an efficacy advantage.
- **The trial landscape moved, but produced no new registry efficacy data.** TRACE will randomize 78 resectable HNSCC patients, C-PRE is suspended with no interpretable reason posted, and a 25-patient cemiplimab/Quad Shot feasibility study is recruiting.

**Quiet watchlist:** No new KEYNOTE-689, NIVOPOST, petosemtamab, ficerafusp alfa, therapeutic HPV-vaccine, prospective ctDNA-triggered intervention, or validated surgical-omission result appeared in the interval. The cycle is active but does not justify a new omission rule.

## 1. Perioperative therapy: pathologic response is prognostic; a randomized comparator is coming

**Bottom line:** A retrospective PCE cohort links broader pathologic response—not pCR alone—to RFS, while TRACE will compare a new antibody with pembrolizumab before surgery. Neither supports response-adapted reduction of a planned operation today.

### Pathologic response after neoadjuvant paclitaxel, carboplatin, and cetuximab

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; single-institution retrospective cohort.

**Methods/results:** Fifty-nine HNSCC patients received neoadjuvant paclitaxel, carboplatin, and cetuximab followed by surgery. Favorable pathologic response was defined as grade ≥2, with less than one-third viable cancer. It occurred in 42.4% and was associated with better 2-year RFS (80.1% vs 62.7%; p=.032). Neither pCR nor radiologic complete/partial response was significantly associated with prognosis. Younger age and female sex were associated with favorable pathologic response in multivariable logistic regression.

**Surgical interpretation:** If PCE is used in a protocol or selected multidisciplinary setting, capture residual viability beyond a binary pCR label and keep the original operative plan visible.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Retrospective, 59 patients, single institution, no untreated or alternative-regimen control, and response groups defined after therapy. Association with RFS does not prove PCE improves survival or identify who can safely receive less surgery.

**Source:** [Oral Oncology; DOI 10.1016/j.oraloncology.2026.108175](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oraloncology.2026.108175)

### TRACE: INCA33890 versus pembrolizumab before surgery

**Provenance/access:** ClinicalTrials.gov protocol first posted October 15 and updated October 22; phase II; not yet recruiting; **no results**.

**Protocol:** NCT07876414 plans to randomize 78 patients with resectable locally advanced HNSCC to INCA33890 900 mg IV every two weeks for three cycles or pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every three weeks for two cycles. The primary endpoint is major pathologic response, defined as ≤10% residual viable tumor across the resected primary and cervical nodes, assessed at nine weeks for INCA33890 and six weeks for pembrolizumab. Estimated start is February 2027; no study locations were posted at review.

**Surgical interpretation:** TRACE is relevant because it preserves surgery and uses whole-specimen primary-plus-node pathology as the comparison endpoint. It is not yet a referral option.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Estimated enrollment and dates, no recruiting sites, no outcomes, and an MPR endpoint rather than demonstrated EFS, OS, function, or safe surgical de-escalation.

**Source:** [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07876414](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07876414)

**Section counterpoint:** Pathologic response can stratify prognosis and make a useful trial endpoint. It does not by itself establish that an operation, neck dissection, or adjuvant treatment can be reduced.

**Go deeper:** The PCE paper is a signal about how to describe the resection specimen; TRACE is the more consequential future test because it randomizes the neoadjuvant comparator.

## 2. Molecular and conventional surveillance: feasibility is ahead of clinical utility

**Bottom line:** Pretreatment ctHPV-DNA was detectable in most—but not all—rare HPV-positive sinonasal/nasopharyngeal cancers, while a retrospective-only review found no survival advantage for routine radiologic follow-up. Neither result validates a surveillance action rule.

### Pretreatment ctHPV-DNA in HPV-positive sinonasal and nasopharyngeal carcinoma

**Provenance/access:** Open-access full text in Acta Oncologica/PMC; single-center feasibility cohort.

**Methods/results:** Fifteen patients treated from 2017–2025 had pretreatment blood collected in Streck tubes and analyzed by droplet digital PCR. Thirteen tumors were squamous cell carcinomas and two were HPV-related multiphenotypic carcinomas. HPV16 accounted for 6/15 (40%); HPV18 for two, HPV33 for one, HPV35 for three, HPV45 for one, and HPV56 for two. Pretreatment ctHPV-DNA sensitivity was 80%, and level increased with T category.

**Surgical interpretation:** A positive baseline assay may make longitudinal research feasible in rare HPV-associated sinonasal/nasopharyngeal disease, but the 20% baseline-negative fraction matters when counseling about future surveillance.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Fifteen patients, mixed subsites and histologies, baseline detection only, no specificity estimate, no recurrence-detection performance, and no evidence that acting on the assay improves outcome.

**Source:** [Acta Oncologica; DOI 10.2340/1651-226X.2026.46393](https://doi.org/10.2340/1651-226X.2026.46393)

### Symptom-based versus radiological follow-up after curative HNSCC treatment

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; systematic review of retrospective studies.

**Methods/results:** Searches of PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, and CENTRAL through April 2025 identified 18 retrospective studies. Across pooled descriptive data, 61.5% of recurrences were symptom-detected, 24% clinically detected, and 14.5% imaging-detected. Reported salvageable recurrences were 60%, 45%, and 30%, respectively. Eight studies with survival data found no significant OS or DFS difference between radiological and non-radiological strategies.

**Surgical interpretation:** Build follow-up around rapid symptom access, examination, and site-specific imaging rather than assuming scheduled scans improve survival.

**Limitations/do not overread:** All included studies were retrospective, with heterogeneous schedules, sites, stages, treatment eras, and definitions. Detection-mode proportions are vulnerable to selection, lead-time, and ascertainment bias; they do not prove symptom-based follow-up is superior or that imaging should be abandoned.

**Source:** [Tumori; DOI 10.1177/03008916261491111](https://doi.org/10.1177/03008916261491111)

**Section counterpoint:** Detectability and earlier detection are surrogate properties. The missing evidence is a prospectively tested action pathway that improves salvage, survival, or quality of life.

**Go deeper:** The ctHPV-DNA full text is useful for assay feasibility and genotype breadth; the surveillance review is most useful for showing how weak the comparative evidence remains.

## 3. Surgery, margins, neck pathology, imaging, and recovery

**Bottom line:** Margin status dominated one salvage-surgery validation; quantitative nodal burden added retrospective prognostic information; CT and MRI remained the practical jaw-invasion modalities; and ERAS improved efficiency outcomes with low-certainty evidence.

### External validation of PATH after radical salvage surgery

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; retrospective observational validation cohort.

**Methods/results:** Eighty-seven patients with recurrent HNSCC underwent radical-intent salvage surgery from 2014–2024. Five-year disease-specific survival was 65.9% for PATH I, 92.3% for II, 37.4% for III, and 13.5% for IV (p=.003), so the proposed four-stage gradient was not ordered. rpTNM did not discriminate stages (p=.674). PATH performed better by C-index (0.670 vs 0.572) and AIC (291.0 vs 304.1). Margin status was the only independent prognostic variable (70.0% vs 17.8% five-year disease-specific survival; p<.001). A post hoc PATH I–II versus III–IV grouping yielded 74.4% versus 31.7% and adjusted HR 3.57.

**Surgical interpretation:** PATH may communicate prognosis better than recurrent-stage TNM after salvage, but the operative priority reinforced here is still achieving a negative margin when anatomically and functionally reasonable.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Eighty-seven patients, retrospective selection for radical surgery, non-ordered stage gradient, and post hoc dichotomization. Do not use PATH as a stand-alone eligibility rule or infer that a negative margin is achievable in every candidate.

**Source:** [Acta Otorrinolaringológica Española; DOI 10.1016/j.otoeng.2026.512414](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.otoeng.2026.512414)

### Quantitative pathologic lymph-node parameters after HNSCC surgery

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; single-center retrospective cohort.

**Methods/results:** Investigators analyzed 410 surgically treated patients from 2010–2020, separating 343 HPV-unrelated cancers from 67 HPV-associated oropharyngeal cancers. In the HPV-unrelated group, data-derived cutoffs for lymph-node yield, lymph-node ratio, and weighted lymph-node ratio were independently associated with OS: HR 1.58 (p=.012), 1.77 (p=.026), and 2.30 (p<.001), with C-indices of 0.55, 0.59, and 0.61. Associations were driven by the ipsilateral neck. In patients without an adjuvant-treatment recommendation, lymph-node yield HR was 1.92 (p=.044) and weighted ratio HR 2.31 (p=.038); in pN0 disease, lymph-node yield HR was 1.88 (p=.027). The measures were not prognostic in HPV-associated OPSCC, where ENE dominated.

**Surgical interpretation:** Adequate retrieval and side-specific reporting remain valuable; weighted ratio may be a research stratifier in HPV-unrelated disease, but it should not replace anatomic pathology or multidisciplinary risk assessment.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Retrospective single-center analysis, data-derived cutoffs, modest discrimination, multiple subgroup tests, and no external validation. Low yield may reflect anatomy, specimen handling, or case complexity rather than surgical quality alone.

**Source:** [European Archives of Oto-Rhino-Laryngology; DOI 10.1007/s00405-026-10684-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00405-026-10684-0)

### Imaging for jaw-bone invasion in oral SCC

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; systematic review and meta-analysis of 38 studies/78 datasets.

**Methods/results:** Pooled sensitivity/specificity were 78.7%/84.2% for CT, 84.3%/81.6% for MRI, 96.7%/57.5% for SPECT, 88.6%/77.1% for cone-beam CT, 80.2%/81.5% for PET/CT, and 65.7%/90.0% for panoramic radiography. In OSCC studies using histopathology as the reference, CT was 78.9%/81.7% and MRI 87.2%/75.4%.

**Surgical interpretation:** CT and MRI remain complementary workhorses for mandibular/maxillary planning. A high-sensitivity modality such as SPECT may create many false positives and should not independently determine segmental resection.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Heterogeneous studies, thresholds, anatomy, prevalence, and reference standards; pooled indirect comparisons do not prove one modality superior or define a safe marginal-versus-segmental mandibulectomy rule.

**Source:** [Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery; DOI 10.1007/s10266-026-01592-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10266-026-01592-0)

### Enhanced-recovery pathways after head-and-neck free-flap reconstruction

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; updated comparative-study meta-analysis using random effects and GRADE.

**Methods/results:** Fifteen studies with 2,787 patients were included after searches through July 22, 2026. ERAS reduced length of stay by a mean 2.75 days (13 studies; 95% CI 1.35–4.16 days shorter) and 72-hour opioid exposure by 55.98 morphine-milligram equivalents (three studies; 95% CI 41.07–70.89 lower). No clear differences emerged for 30-day readmission, total flap failure, reoperation, or 30-day mortality. Certainty was low for length of stay and opioid use and very low for most safety outcomes. The abstract reports earlier enteral feeding but presents an internally inconsistent estimate/interval direction, so that numeric claim is not reproduced here.

**Surgical interpretation:** ERAS is reasonable for standardizing mobilization, analgesia, and discharge planning, with the strongest signal in efficiency rather than proof of equivalent rare-event safety.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Mostly nonrandomized pathway studies, clinical heterogeneity, few studies for opioid outcomes, and low/very-low GRADE certainty. Shorter stay does not itself prove better recovery or unchanged flap safety.

**Source:** [Annals of Surgical Oncology; DOI 10.1245/s10434-026-20786-y](https://doi.org/10.1245/s10434-026-20786-y)

**Section counterpoint:** Retrospective prognostic models and pooled diagnostic accuracy refine probabilities. They do not replace resectability judgment, pathology review, or patient-specific functional tradeoffs.

**Go deeper:** PATH is the clearest caution about relying on a staging label; the jaw-imaging meta-analysis is the most directly useful for operative planning; the ERAS paper supports pathway work while keeping certainty visible.

## 4. Postoperative radiation and treatment delivery: discordant selection evidence, clearer process signals

**Bottom line:** Two small pN1 oral-cancer cohorts point in different directions, so PORT remains an individualized decision. Navigation improved time to radiation, and protons reduced selected acute toxicities without improving disease control or survival.

### PORT versus observation in pT1–2 pN1 oral SCC: no adjusted association

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; retrospective cohort.

**Methods/results:** Eighty-five patients had pT1–2 OSCC with one ipsilateral node ≤3 cm and no extranodal extension; 43 received PORT. Poor differentiation, high-risk margins, and bilateral neck dissection predicted PORT use. At 60 months, estimated OS was 63% with surgery alone and 65% with PORT; regional-recurrence probability was 30% and 25%. Adjusted PORT-versus-surgery HR was 0.94 for OS (95% CI 0.46–1.92; p=.9) and 0.81 for regional RFS (0.30–2.17; p=.7). Apparent benefit in pT2 disease, age under 70, or neck yield under 18 came from exploratory subgroups.

**Surgical interpretation:** Observation remains defensible for carefully selected pT1–2 pN1 patients with adequate neck management and no other adverse feature, but the wide intervals require shared decision-making.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Retrospective treatment selection, 85 patients, broad confidence intervals, and non-prespecified subgroup signals. The null estimate does not prove PORT has no benefit in every pN1 patient.

**Source:** [Journal of Cranio-Maxillo-Facial Surgery; DOI 10.1016/j.jcms.2026.109925](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcms.2026.109925)

### PORT versus observation in pT1–2 pN1 oral SCC: matched RFS signal

**Provenance/access:** Open-access full text in Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery/PMC; retrospective single-center cohort.

**Methods/results:** Fifty-eight pT1–2 pN1 OSCC patients without extracapsular spread were included. In the full cohort, PORT was not associated with OS (p=.628) or RFS (p=.244). Propensity matching retained only 30 patients—14 surgery alone and 16 PORT—and excluded 28 without a match. In that reduced cohort, PORT was associated with RFS (multivariable HR 0.21; 95% CI 0.02–0.85; p=.029) but not OS (p=.893).

**Surgical interpretation:** The result is a reason to discuss PORT when other risk features cluster, not a reason to make pN1 alone an automatic indication.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Retrospective data back to 2009, incomplete capture of adverse pathology, residual treatment-selection confounding, only 30 matched patients, wide CI, and overfitting risk. The matched association does not overturn the null full-cohort result or the companion 85-patient analysis.

**Source:** [Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery; DOI 10.1007/s10006-026-01654-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10006-026-01654-1)

### Care navigation after free-flap surgery

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; retrospective before/after cohort.

**Methods/results:** A standardized navigation pathway was evaluated in 404 patients undergoing head-and-neck free-flap reconstruction. Median surgery-to-radiation interval fell from 56 days (IQR 39–83) before navigation to 45 days (IQR 31–64) after implementation (p=.004). DFS did not differ (p=.7695).

**Surgical interpretation:** Assigning ownership of dental clearance, pathology review, referrals, simulation, and wound readiness is a practical quality intervention. This extends the September 28 signal that delayed PORT is common in high-risk postoperative care.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Nonrandomized before/after design, possible secular and case-mix changes, and no demonstrated DFS improvement. An 11-day median reduction does not prove every patient met the ≤42-day target or that navigation caused the change.

**Cross-cycle context:** [September 28 radiation-timing section](HN_Onc_Digest_2026-09-28.html#rt-deescalation)

**Source:** [Head & Neck; DOI 10.1002/hed.70513](https://doi.org/10.1002/hed.70513)

### Intensity-modulated proton versus photon therapy for oropharyngeal cancer

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; synthesis of two phase III randomized trials and one matched cohort.

**Methods/results:** No significant efficacy difference was found for locoregional recurrence (HR 1.37; 95% CI 0.56–3.36; I²=0%), PFS (HR 0.90; 0.61–1.33; I²=0%), or OS (HR 0.79; 0.32–1.98; I²=47%). Grade 3 acute dysphagia occurred in 24.0% with IMPT versus 40.9% with IMRT (RR 0.65; 0.51–0.84; I²=0%); grade 3 xerostomia/oral-pharyngeal dryness occurred in 19.9% versus 32.5% (RR 0.72; 0.54–0.95; I²=0%). Nutritional endpoints were inconclusive: 12-month gastrostomy dependence RR 0.32 (0.08–1.28), ≥20% weight loss RR 1.05 (0.12–9.38; I²=85.7%), and composite nutritional outcome RR 0.92 (0.12–7.07; I²=87.7%).

**Surgical interpretation:** Proton referral can be framed around reducing selected acute toxicities and integral dose, not superior control or a proven reduction in late feeding-tube dependence.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Only three comparative sources, one nonrandomized, broad efficacy intervals, heterogeneous nutritional outcomes, and limited late-toxicity evidence.

**Source:** [ESMO Open; DOI 10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108602](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108602)

**Section counterpoint:** PORT selection remains confounded in small observational cohorts. Process improvement and toxicity reduction are more credible signals this cycle than any new oncologic indication.

**Go deeper:** Read the two pN1 studies together—the 30-patient matched analysis should not be isolated from its null full cohort or from the separate adjusted null result.

## 5. Cutaneous SCC: durable neoadjuvant follow-up, expert ART boundaries, and a changing trial landscape

**Bottom line:** Final phase II cemiplimab follow-up is reassuring for patients with deep pathologic response, but it remains single-arm. ART consensus is expert opinion, exenteration evidence lacks an eye-preservation comparator, C-PRE is suspended, and the Quad Shot study is feasibility-only.

### Final phase II follow-up of neoadjuvant cemiplimab

**Provenance/access:** PubMed/JCO abstract; final analysis of multicenter phase II NCT04154943.

**Methods/results:** The original resectable stage II–IV cSCC trial reported a 51% pCR rate and 89% estimated 12-month EFS at 19 months. At 54 months' median follow-up, 16 EFS events had occurred; there were no new recurrences, and all five new events were comorbidity-related deaths without cSCC recurrence. Estimated 54-month EFS was 76.2% overall, 81.5% after pCR, 88.9% after major pathologic response, 100% after partial pathologic response, and 45.7% in nonresponders/not evaluable patients. None of 40 patients with pCR had recurred. Estimated 54-month OS was 79.4%, with no new grade ≥3 treatment-emergent adverse event.

**Surgical interpretation:** Durable control after pCR strengthens pathologic response as a trial-stratification endpoint and supports long-term counseling after neoadjuvant therapy plus surgery.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Single-arm phase II study, response groups defined after treatment, few later events, and competing comorbidity deaths. Do not compare response-category EFS causally or omit surgery/RT solely because a clinical response appears complete.

**Source:** [Journal of Clinical Oncology; DOI 10.1200/JCO-26-02166](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO-26-02166)

### Delphi consensus on ART for node-negative cSCC

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; US multidisciplinary Delphi panel.

**Methods/results:** The 35-member panel included 15 radiation oncologists, nine dermatologic/Mohs surgeons, seven medical oncologists, three head-and-neck surgeons, and one surgical oncologist; consensus required ≥80%. All 35 recommended ART when further surgery is impossible and gross or microscopic margins remain positive. After negative-margin surgery, consensus supported ART for gross cortical/marrow invasion (32/35), skull-base invasion (32/35), four risk factors (35/35), three risk factors (30–33/35), PNI ≥0.1 mm plus another risk factor (30/35), or clinical nerve invasion (31/35). Thirty-four of 35 favored omitting ART after pCR to neoadjuvant immunotherapy. Radiation oncologists favored a four-to-eight-week window (15/15), 60–66 Gy in 2-Gy fractions (15/15), or 50–55 Gy in 2.5-Gy fractions (13/15); no consensus emerged for elective nodal-basin irradiation.

**Surgical interpretation:** The clearest consensus remains re-excision when feasible and ART for an unresectable positive margin. The negative-margin scenarios provide a tumor-board framework where trial-level evidence is absent.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Expert consensus, not comparative outcomes evidence; only three head-and-neck surgeons; risk-factor definitions may overlap; and pCR-based ART omission has not been proven safe in a randomized trial.

**Source:** [International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics; DOI 10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.09.022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.09.022)

### Orbital exenteration for advanced head-and-neck cSCC

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; random-effects systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Methods/results:** Ten studies included 217 patients undergoing orbital exenteration. Pooled OS was 73.7% at two years (95% CI 63.4–81.9) and 70.8% at five years (56.0–82.2); DFS was 66.1% (42.2–83.9) and 51.6% (37.8–65.2). Local recurrence was 23.7% (14.0–37.1), regional recurrence 14.0% (3.9–39.2), and complications 39.2% (24.1–56.7). In study-level meta-regression, margin-positive proportion was the only covariate associated with local recurrence (β 0.076 per percentage point; 95% CI 0.027–0.124; p=.015; R²=100%).

**Surgical interpretation:** The data quantify outcomes for consent and reinforce margin planning, but exenteration decisions still require orbital-apex extent, cranial-nerve involvement, expected clearance, function, and systemic options.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Retrospective heterogeneous series, wide intervals, ecological meta-regression, selection of operable patients, and no direct exenteration-versus-orbit-preservation comparison. The pooled survival cannot establish superiority of exenteration.

**Source:** [Head & Neck; DOI 10.1002/hed.70517](https://doi.org/10.1002/hed.70517)

### C-PRE phase III status changed to suspended

**Provenance/access:** ClinicalTrials.gov registry last updated October 20; randomized phase III; suspended; **no results**.

**Protocol/status:** NCT06568172 plans 420 patients with resectable stage III/IV cSCC. The control arm undergoes surgery within six weeks of randomization with postoperative IMRT when indicated. The experimental arm receives cemiplimab every 21 days for up to four cycles, then response-adaptive surgery 21 days after the last dose, postoperative RT when indicated, and four every-six-week adjuvant cemiplimab cycles for patients without pCR. Primary EFS is followed up to six years. The official record changed to suspended on October 20; the posted reason is only “Other - Other,” which is not clinically interpretable.

**Surgical interpretation:** Pause referral assumptions and verify site-level availability directly. If the study resumes, it is the randomized design most capable of testing whether perioperative cemiplimab plus response-adapted surgery improves EFS over surgery-first care.

**Limitations/do not overread:** No results and no informative suspension explanation. Suspension does not imply toxicity, futility, efficacy, or permanent closure.

**Source:** [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06568172](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06568172)

### Cemiplimab plus Quad Shot radiation in frail or older head-and-neck cSCC

**Provenance/access:** ClinicalTrials.gov protocol first posted October 13; early phase I; recruiting at Washington University in St Louis; **no results**.

**Protocol:** NCT07869823 plans 25 older, frail, or highly comorbid patients with locally advanced head-and-neck cSCC. Cemiplimab 350 mg IV is given every three weeks for eight doses, with discretionary continuation to one year for responding, tolerating patients. Quad Shot radiation delivers two fractions per day for two days, repeated every three weeks for up to four cycles: 59.2 Gy planned, or 44.4 Gy if complete clinical and radiographic response permits stopping after cycle three. Feasibility requires more than 80% of responders to complete at least six cemiplimab doses and more than 75% to complete at least three Quad Shots.

**Surgical interpretation:** This is a nonoperative feasibility option for a deliberately vulnerable population, not a comparison with curative surgery in fit resectable patients.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Single arm, 25 planned patients, feasibility defined among responders, no efficacy results, and selection for age/frailty/comorbidity. The protocol cannot establish equivalence to surgery or conventional definitive treatment.

**Source:** [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07869823](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07869823)

**Section counterpoint:** The strongest result is durable follow-up from a completed single-arm trial. The other four items are expert synthesis, retrospective pooling, or registry state—not new comparative efficacy.

**Go deeper:** Use the final cemiplimab analysis for long-term counseling, the Delphi paper to structure—but not settle—ART discussion, and the two registries only for current trial logistics.

## Bottom line for tumor board

This cycle changes confidence more than standards. Neoadjuvant pathologic response is increasingly durable and prognostic, but no finding permits surgery omission. For pT1–2 pN1 oral SCC, preserve equipoise: two new cohorts disagree, and the positive estimate rests on 30 matched patients. In salvage surgery and orbital-invasion cSCC, margin status remains central, while new staging and imaging summaries should refine—not replace—operative judgment. Invest in delivery systems: navigation shortened surgery-to-RT time, ERAS reduced stay and opioid exposure, and protons reduced selected acute toxicities without an efficacy advantage. For cSCC trials, distinguish the mature single-arm cemiplimab follow-up from expert ART consensus and result-free registry events; C-PRE suspension has no posted clinical explanation, and the Quad Shot study is a small feasibility protocol.
