1. Who gets a medical oncology consult before you book the OR

4 findings
Practice-changing, with caveats

For CPS ≥1 resectable stage III-IV disease, perioperative pembrolizumab is FDA-approved and NCCN-endorsed — but the benefit concentrates at CPS ≥10, and editorials are pushing back on blanket use.

  • KEYNOTE-689 (Uppaluri et al., NEJM): 36-month EFS 57.6% vs 46.4% overall (HR 0.73), with the largest prespecified effect at CPS ≥10; surgery was completed in about 88% of both groups.▸

    Key conclusion —Adding neoadjuvant and adjuvant pembrolizumab to surgery-based curative therapy improved EFS across the trial hierarchy, with the clearest absolute separation at CPS ≥10.

    Counterpoint —The trial tests an entire perioperative package, not the neoadjuvant dose in isolation; overall survival was immature, major pathologic response was modest, and the study was sponsored by Merck.

    Evidence briefStructured abstractPhase III · NCT03765918

    The pivotal perioperative regimen, separated into what the trial actually proved

    This open-label phase III trial randomized 714 patients with resectable, locally advanced HNSCC to two preoperative pembrolizumab cycles, surgery, postoperative pembrolizumab plus risk-adapted RT with or without cisplatin, then pembrolizumab maintenance—or to surgery followed by standard postoperative therapy. The primary endpoint was EFS in a prespecified CPS hierarchy.

    The trial supports an early multidisciplinary consult because systemic therapy begins before the operation and continues through postoperative treatment. CPS informs the expected magnitude of benefit, but surgery remains the curative-pathway anchor.

    Do not overread

    Grade ≥3 treatment-related events were common in both groups (44.6% vs 42.9%). The design cannot tell whether benefit came from the preoperative doses, postoperative therapy, or both, and it does not establish that response should shrink the planned operation.

  • NIVOPOST-OP / GORTEC 2018-01 (Bourhis et al., Lancet): postoperative nivolumab plus chemoradiotherapy improved 3-year DFS to 63.1% vs 52.5% (HR 0.76) after resection of pathologically high-risk HNSCC.▸

    Key conclusion —This is a postoperative intensification strategy: the operation and high-risk pathology come first, then nivolumab is layered onto and after cisplatin-based chemoradiotherapy.

    Counterpoint —The open-label regimen increased grade 4 treatment-related toxicity (10% vs 5%), overall survival remains immature, and it does not answer any presurgical selection or delay question.

    Evidence briefStructured abstractPhase III · NCT03576417

    A high-risk pathology trial—not a competitor for the preoperative slot

    Across 82 sites in six countries, 680 patients were enrolled after complete macroscopic resection; 666 entered the efficacy analysis. Eligible high-risk findings included extranodal extension, a positive margin, at least four involved cervical nodes without extranodal extension, or multiple foci of perineural invasion.

  • KEYNOTE-689 surgical-window signal: 45 clinical progressions were recorded by surgery in the pembrolizumab group vs 9 with surgery first, while roughly 88% in each arm still completed surgery.▸

    Key conclusion —The presurgical interval needs active ownership and a fixed operative plan; the numerical imbalance is important for implementation but does not, by itself, prove pembrolizumab caused progression or prevented surgery.

    Counterpoint —This is a supplemental, exploratory safety signal from the same 714-patient trial—not an independent cohort—and granular data on altered surgical extent and morbidity were not the primary report's focus.

    Supplement digestPresurgical safety signalSame KEYNOTE-689 population

    How to interpret the progression count without collapsing distinct endpoints

    Clinical progression recorded before or at surgery is not identical to failure to undergo surgery. The protocol preserved the planned surgical footprint rather than allowing apparent response to de-escalate the operation, and the aggregate surgical-completion rate remained about 88% in both randomized groups.

    Operational read

    Set the operation date before treatment, define who evaluates interval symptoms or imaging, and agree on a rapid route back to the surgeon. Response is not a license to reduce oncologic margins outside a study.

    Evidence boundary

    The imbalance warrants scrutiny, but causal attribution requires patient-level reasons, timing, and adjudication. It should be read beside—not instead of—the randomized EFS result and surgical-completion denominator.

    Read the primary trial and supplement →

  • Ben Kridis & Khanfir pooled the two phase III perioperative trials (1,394 patients): DFS HR 0.75 and OS HR 0.74, with grade ≥3 treatment-related events around 43%–45% and discontinuation in 12%–18%.▸

    Key conclusion —The pooled direction favors checkpoint inhibition, but its greatest practical value is placing efficacy and treatment burden side by side.

    Counterpoint —This meta-analysis contains only two heterogeneous trials—one perioperative and one postoperative—so pooling cannot identify the best timing, isolate the neoadjuvant contribution, or replace direct trial interpretation.

    Meta-analysis digestStructured abstract2 phase III RCTs

    A compact synthesis with a very small evidence base

    The authors combined KEYNOTE-689 and NIVOPOST-OP—1,394 high-risk patients in total—to estimate efficacy and toxicity for perioperative checkpoint inhibition. Because the component regimens occupy different points in the surgical pathway, the pooled effect is a program-level signal rather than a timing comparison.

    Two studies are too few to explore heterogeneity robustly. A pooled OS estimate does not resolve immature OS in each report, and it cannot tell a surgeon whether preoperative therapy is preferable to postoperative-only intensification.

2. Cutaneous SCC: the same argument, one step behind

2 findings
Hypothesis-generating, moving fast

Cemiplimab is moving earlier into the sequence for high-risk cutaneous SCC — expect referrals mid-treatment with no comparative data yet on when surgery-first vs. neoadjuvant is right.

  • C-POST (adjuvant cemiplimab after surgery and postoperative RT): 24-month DFS was 87.1% vs 64.1% with placebo (HR 0.32) in 415 patients with high-risk cutaneous SCC.▸

    Key conclusion —For rigorously defined high-risk cSCC that has already been resected and irradiated, one year of adjuvant cemiplimab produced a large early DFS benefit.

    Counterpoint —Overall survival was not different at the interim analysis, follow-up was 24 months, and the result does not address neoadjuvant therapy, surgery avoidance, or patients who did not receive postoperative RT.

    Evidence briefStructured abstractDouble-blind phase III · NCT03969004

    A narrow postoperative population with a large disease-free-survival separation

    C-POST randomized 415 patients after complete surgery and postoperative radiation to cemiplimab or placebo for up to 48 weeks. High-risk eligibility was pathologically stringent: nodal extranodal extension with a node at least 2 cm, at least three involved nodes, or selected nonnodal features such as in-transit metastasis, T4 disease, perineural invasion, or locally recurrent disease with another risk factor.

    Pathology now determines who should return to the multidisciplinary table after RT. The trial supports adjuvant systemic therapy for the selected C-POST risk phenotype, not a broad “high-risk” label applied by intuition alone.

    The HR 0.32 is striking but early. It should not be used to justify omitting surgery or RT, and comparisons with perioperative mucosal-HNSCC trials are indirect.

  • The updated European cSCC treatment guideline places adjuvant cemiplimab after surgery and RT into formal multidisciplinary discussion while retaining surgery with complete margin assessment as the foundation for resectable disease.▸

    Key conclusion —The update integrates checkpoint inhibition into a stage- and resectability-based pathway without displacing surgery for common resectable cSCC.

    Counterpoint —This is a European multidisciplinary consensus document, not a new comparative cohort; recommendations depend on local regulation, access, comorbidity, immunosuppression, and patient preference.

    Guideline digestPeer-reviewed consensus updateNo new patient cohort

    The algorithm by clinical state

    Part 2 of the European interdisciplinary guideline updates treatment recommendations from the 2023 edition. It emphasizes margin-controlled surgery for common cSCC, individualized multidisciplinary treatment for macroscopic nodal disease, anti-PD-1 therapy for advanced disease not amenable to curative surgery or radiation, and MDT discussion of adjuvant cemiplimab after surgery plus RT in high-risk disease.

3. What's going to replace your 3-month post-op CT/PET

1 study · 5 trials
Practice-changing within trials

ctDNA/MRD assays are maturing into a surgical/adjuvant-therapy selection tool built directly into trial designs — the thing most likely to change your surveillance in 2-3 years.

  • A tissue-agnostic methylome MRD assay was validated in 325 head and neck cancer patients: MRD positivity strongly tracked recurrence (RFS HR 35.7), with 91% sensitivity, 88% specificity, and a mean 4.1-month lead time.▸

    Key conclusion —A blood-only, tissue-agnostic methylation classifier produced a strong prognostic signal across HPV-positive and HPV-negative disease and often detected recurrence before standard clinical recognition.

    Counterpoint —This was observational validation. Prognostic accuracy and lead time do not prove that acting on a positive assay improves survival, preserves function, or safely replaces imaging.

    Evidence briefStructured abstractBlinded validation cohort

    A recurrence detector with a large risk signal—and an unresolved action problem

    The study analyzed 1,155 serial plasma samples from 325 patients using genome-wide methylome enrichment and a tissue-agnostic classifier. Training and validation sets were distinct, the validation analysis was blinded, and median clinical follow-up was 60 months.

    A positive result outside a protocol creates uncertainty about confirmatory imaging, biopsy, treatment timing, and false-positive harm. It is not yet a substitute for standard surveillance.

    Read the paper →

  • SAVAL (University of Maryland; NCT06088381) — a recruiting, single-arm phase II trial testing whether selected HPV-positive oropharynx patients who clear postoperative ctDNA can omit adjuvant radiation.▸

    Protocol conclusion —SAVAL turns an early postoperative blood draw into a proposed off-ramp from RT for an intermediate-risk subset after transoral surgery.

    Counterpoint —There are no outcomes yet. The estimated 61-patient, single-arm design and a 2-year PFS endpoint that includes salvage will require careful interpretation of recurrence burden as well as salvage success.

    Live registryRecruitingPhase II · estimated n=61

    The ctDNA result changes postoperative intensity only after pathology agrees

    Patients enter with preoperative ctDNA positivity and undergo transoral robotic surgery. A second blood sample is collected 2–14 days postoperatively. The experimental observation path requires both intermediate-risk pathology—pT1–3, N1–2, negative margins, and absent or microscopic extranodal extension up to 1 mm—and ctDNA clearance.

4. Where the extent of your dissection is actually being questioned

2 findings
Hypothesis-generating

Two independent signals support pulling back on neck dissection extent in selected patients — neither is a guideline change yet.

  • Level IIB in cN0 necks: a 35-study meta-analysis estimated occult metastasis at 1.2%–2.6% across oral cavity, larynx, oropharynx, and hypopharynx primaries, supporting selective—not routine—omission.▸

    Key conclusion —Level IIB involvement was uncommon across subsites, making omission a plausible morbidity-reduction strategy in carefully selected clinically node-negative necks.

    Counterpoint —The evidence is a heterogeneous, largely retrospective set of level-specific pathology studies; no randomized omission trial proves equivalent regional control, and shoulder morbidity was inconsistently captured.

    Meta-analysis digestStructured abstractPRISMA 2020 · 35 studies

    The pooled risk by primary subsite

    The authors searched Embase, PubMed, Scopus, and Cochrane through April 30, 2026. Adults with cN0 laryngeal, oral cavity, oropharyngeal, or hypopharyngeal SCC had to undergo elective neck dissection with level-specific pathology. Random-effects models pooled the proportion of level IIB metastasis.

    Clinical read

    Use subsite, nodal mapping, and intraoperative findings to select—not automatically omit—level IIB when the expected oncologic yield is very low and SAN morbidity matters.

    Do not overread

    SAN morbidity was reported inconsistently (up to 4%) and shoulder impairment up to 2%. Sparse functional reporting limits any direct calculation of benefit versus missed disease.

    Read the paper →

  • Omitting neck dissection after an imaging-negative neoadjuvant response in cT3–4N0M0 oral SCC: a 58-patient retrospective series found no statistically significant difference in 36-month neck control, DFS, or OS.▸

    Key conclusion —The series supplies an early response-adapted omission signal, but it is far short of demonstrating noninferiority.

    Counterpoint —Selection was retrospective, confidence intervals were very wide, imaging-negative nodes were not pathologically proven negative in the omission group, and every patient received postoperative chemoradiotherapy.

    Evidence briefStructured abstractSingle-center retrospective cohort

    A provocative comparison heavily protected by postoperative treatment

    At Sun Yat-sen University, patients treated from October 2019 through July 2023 had cT3–4N0M0 oral SCC, received three to four cycles of PD-1 blockade plus paclitaxel and platinum, then underwent extended primary-tumor resection. Imaging-negative necks were managed with dissection in 30 patients and without dissection in 28; all received concurrent postoperative chemoradiotherapy.

5. Radiation de-escalation — changes your referral conversations

2 findings
Useful for counseling now

Dose de-escalation in HPV+ oropharynx cancer keeps showing better patient-reported swallowing/QOL without a confirmed efficacy penalty.

  • ORATOR/ORATOR2 post hoc comparison: 59 patients with T1–2 p16-positive oropharynx cancer treated on the RT arms had better 1-year swallowing scores and less grade 2–3 dysphagia/mucositis with 60 Gy than with 70 Gy.▸

    Key conclusion —The lower-dose cohort had a clinically coherent functional advantage across swallowing, symptom, and toxicity measures.

    Counterpoint —This was not a randomized dose comparison: it pooled RT arms from two different trials, used different cisplatin schedules, and was too small to establish oncologic noninferiority.

    Evidence briefStructured abstractPost hoc cross-trial comparison · n=59

    A quality-of-life signal that still needs a direct efficacy comparison

    Investigators compared the RT arms of ORATOR2 (60 Gy; n=29) and ORATOR (70 Gy; n=30) in early p16-positive oropharynx cancer. The analysis centered on 1-year MD Anderson Dysphagia Inventory change, EORTC quality-of-life domains, and clinician-graded toxicity.

  • RTOG 1016 quality-of-life substudy: replacing cisplatin with cetuximab did not improve any 6-month QOL domain in 375 analyzable patients—and the parent randomized trial showed inferior tumor control and survival with cetuximab.▸

    Key conclusion —Cetuximab did not deliver the hoped-for quality-of-life advantage that might offset its established efficacy disadvantage in HPV-positive oropharynx cancer.

    Counterpoint —The QOL analysis used a prespecified subset of the parent trial, with attrition by 12 months; it does not define the best alternative for a truly cisplatin-ineligible patient.

    Evidence briefStructured abstractPhase III QOL substudy · 375 analyzable

    No patient-reported tradeoff to rescue an inferior substitution

    The first 400 RTOG 1016 participants completed EORTC QLQ-C30 and H&N35 instruments at baseline, treatment completion, and 3, 6, and 12 months; 375 were analyzable. A change greater than 10 points was prespecified as clinically meaningful. The randomized parent trial compared 70 Gy accelerated RT with cisplatin versus cetuximab.

6. Systemic options once someone's out of surgical/RT salvage

2 findings
Background / pipeline

Two next-gen EGFR-directed agents are showing responses in patients who've failed both anti-PD-1 and cetuximab.

  • Petosemtamab (EGFR × LGR5 bispecific): two recruiting open-label phase III trials are testing it with pembrolizumab first-line (LiGeR-HN1) and against investigator's choice after platinum plus anti-PD-1 (LiGeR-HN2).▸

    Pipeline conclusion —The development program now spans both the first-line PD-L1-positive setting and the post-platinum/post-checkpoint setting, with survival as a phase III endpoint.

    Counterpoint —This archive item is a pipeline review plus live protocols, not a phase III efficacy result. Both pivotal studies are open-label and remain recruiting.

    Pipeline digestReview + live registriesNo phase III results

    Two pivotal questions for an EGFR × LGR5 bispecific

    Petosemtamab is designed to engage EGFR and LGR5 while activating immune effector function. The review summarizes the biologic rationale and early program; the registries show how the sponsor is testing that signal in two distinct recurrent/metastatic treatment lines.

  • Ficerafusp alfa (EGFR/TGF-β fusion): in a small phase I pembrolizumab-combination cohort across advanced solid tumors, 4 partial responses were observed (2 HNSCC), with an overall response rate of 31%.▸

    Key conclusion —The combination produced durable responses in a heavily pretreated phase I population, including HNSCC activity after prior checkpoint-directed therapy.

    Counterpoint —This was a heterogeneous, uncontrolled, very small solid-tumor cohort with wide confidence intervals; it cannot supply an HNSCC-specific response rate or establish sequencing after cetuximab.

    A durable combination signal, with denominators that matter

    The first-in-human study evaluated pharmacokinetics in 46 monotherapy and 15 combination-treated patients with advanced solid tumors. In the pembrolizumab combination efficacy set, four partial responses occurred—two in HNSCC and two in squamous anal cancer—and five additional patients had stable disease.

Bottom line for tumor board

The live question in resectable HNSCC has shifted from "add immunotherapy or not" to "who's actually CPS ≥10 / high-risk enough to justify the neoadjuvant delay and toxicity" — your JCO argument is now showing up independently in half a dozen other editorials, alongside genuine defenders of the CPS ≥1 label. Watch ctDNA closely: it's the mechanism several active trials are using to make that selection concrete, and it's plausible it reshapes your surveillance protocol before it reshapes NCCN guidelines.
