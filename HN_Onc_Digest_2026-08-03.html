1. Who gets a medical oncology consult before you book the OR

4 findings
Practice-changing, with caveats

For CPS ≥1 resectable stage III-IV disease, perioperative pembrolizumab is FDA-approved and NCCN-endorsed — but the benefit concentrates at CPS ≥10, and editorials are pushing back on blanket use.

  • KEYNOTE-689 (Uppaluri et al., NEJM): 36-month EFS 57.6% vs 46.4% overall (HR 0.73); major pathologic response 13.7% at CPS ≥10 vs 1.8% at CPS 1-9. 10% of the pembrolizumab arm never reached surgery.▸

    Key conclusion —The archived signal was strongest at CPS ≥10, while the neoadjuvant sequence also introduced a group who never reached surgery.

    Read the paper →

  • NIVOPOST-OP / GORTEC 2018-01 (Bourhis et al., Lancet): adjuvant-only in high-risk resected disease. 3-yr DFS 63.1% vs 52.5% (HR 0.76), PD-L1-independent, driven by locoregional control — doesn't touch your OR planning.▸

    Key conclusion —The adjuvant-only design avoids neoadjuvant delay and applies to a defined high-risk surgical population.

    Read the paper →

  • Per KEYNOTE-689 supplementary data: 45 progressions during the neoadjuvant window vs 8 in the surgery-first arm, not systematically reported in the primary paper.▸

    Archived source note —This finding came from the KEYNOTE-689 supplementary data and did not have a separate citation in the baseline cycle.

  • Pooled grade ≥3 AEs 42.9-44.6%; discontinuation for AEs 12.4-17.7% (Ben Kridis & Khanfir, J Oral Pathol Med).▸

    Key conclusion —The pooled toxicity and discontinuation rates remain central to counseling about perioperative treatment.

    Read the paper →

Counterpoint

Not everyone reads KEYNOTE-689 as overreach — reviews argue even a modest EFS benefit is worth it given surgical completion was largely unaffected (~88% both arms). The disagreement is where you set the bar, not whether the trial is valid. DOI

Go deeper

Imamura et al. (Cancer Treat Rev) for the best head-to-head trial comparison. DOI Brammer et al. (Emory, Transl Cancer Res) for the surgeon-specific angle. DOI

2. Cutaneous SCC: the same argument, one step behind

2 findings
Hypothesis-generating, moving fast

Cemiplimab is moving earlier into the sequence for high-risk cutaneous SCC — expect referrals mid-treatment with no comparative data yet on when surgery-first vs. neoadjuvant is right.

  • C-POST trial (adjuvant cemiplimab, resected high-risk CSCC): DFS HR 0.32 — largest effect size of any perioperative-ICI trial in this space.▸

    Key conclusion —The baseline cycle identified C-POST as the strongest perioperative-ICI effect signal in resected high-risk CSCC.

    Read the paper →

  • Updated European guideline (Stratigos et al., Eur J Cancer) now bakes adjuvant cemiplimab into the algorithm pending MDT discussion.▸

    Key conclusion —The guideline moved adjuvant cemiplimab into formal multidisciplinary treatment planning.

    Read the paper →

Counterpoint

Kreft et al. (Eur J Cancer): no head-to-head data for neoadjuvant vs. definitive RT vs. surgery-first, rising patient withdrawal from surgery after deep responses, no staging system built for that decision. DOI

Go deeper

Duke cost-effectiveness model if the conversation turns to cost/access — neoadjuvant modeled as cost-saving and modestly more effective over 10 years. DOI

3. What's going to replace your 3-month post-op CT/PET

1 study · 5 trials
Practice-changing within trials

ctDNA/MRD assays are maturing into a surgical/adjuvant-therapy selection tool built directly into trial designs — the thing most likely to change your surveillance in 2-3 years.

  • Tissue-agnostic methylome MRD assay, 325 HNC patients: MRD-positive HR 35.7 for recurrence, lead time up to 14.9 months (mean 4.1), 91% sensitivity / 88% specificity.▸

    Key conclusion —The assay provided a substantial recurrence-risk signal and earlier detection in this 325-patient validation dataset.

    Read the paper →

  • SAVAL (U Maryland) — adjuvant therapy in HPV+ OPC, selected by post-op ctDNA.▸

    Trial status —Recruiting.

    Decision use —Post-operative ctDNA gates adjuvant therapy.

  • ReACT 2.0 (Dana-Farber) — pembrolizumab vs. observation, gated by ctHPVDNA.▸

    Trial status —Not yet recruiting.

    Decision use —ctHPVDNA gates systemic treatment versus observation.

  • OSU ctDNA-adaptive RT — de-intensified RT dose in early-stage HPV+ OPC.▸

    Trial status —Recruiting.

    Decision use —ctDNA response informs radiation dose de-intensification.

  • U Chicago — response-adaptive RT dose in HPV-negative disease.▸

    Trial status —Recruiting.

    Decision use —Molecular response informs radiation dose.

  • UCSF MRD — post-treatment surveillance strategy.▸

    Trial status —Recruiting.

    Decision use —MRD informs post-treatment surveillance.

Counterpoint

Still trial-gated, single-assay, largely academic-center-only — no consensus on tumor-informed vs. tumor-agnostic assays, turnaround time, or what to do with a positive result outside a protocol. Know it's coming; don't start ordering it.

Go deeper

The Annals of Oncology validation paper above is the most rigorous single dataset for methodology. DOI

4. Where the extent of your dissection is actually being questioned

2 findings
Hypothesis-generating

Two independent signals support pulling back on neck dissection extent in selected patients — neither is a guideline change yet.

  • Level IIB in cN0 necks: pooled occult metastasis rate 1.2-2.6% across subsites (lowest oral cavity, larynx), 35-study meta-analysis — supports selective omission to reduce SAN morbidity.▸

    Key conclusion —The pooled low occult-metastasis rate supported selective omission as a morbidity-reduction hypothesis.

    Read the paper →

  • Omitting dissection after imaging-negative neoadjuvant response in cT3-4N0M0 OSCC (Sun Yat-sen, n=58): no significant difference in nodal control, DFS, or OS.▸

    Key conclusion —This small retrospective series supplied a tentative response-adapted omission signal.

    Read the paper →

Counterpoint

The neoadjuvant-response data is a single retrospective series of 58 patients — underpowered to rule out a meaningful difference, and imaging-negative is not pathology-negative. Track it, don't act on it yet.

Go deeper

Review citing series where deep responders avoided mandibulectomy (75%) and free flap reconstruction (72%), with 80-90% laryngeal preservation. DOI

5. Radiation de-escalation — changes your referral conversations

2 findings
Useful for counseling now

Dose de-escalation in HPV+ oropharynx cancer keeps showing better patient-reported swallowing/QOL without a confirmed efficacy penalty.

  • ORATOR/ORATOR2 pooled analysis: 60 Gy vs. 70 Gy in T1-2 p16+ OPC — 60 Gy cohort had meaningfully better dysphagia scores and less grade 2-3 mucositis at 1 year.▸

    Key conclusion —The lower-dose cohort reported better swallowing and mucositis outcomes in the pooled analysis.

    Read the paper →

  • RTOG 1016 QOL substudy: cetuximab substitution for cisplatin didn't improve QOL (and was worse on efficacy) — settles a question that still comes up when a patient is cisplatin-ineligible.▸

    Archived source note —No separate DOI was included for this substudy in the baseline cycle.

Counterpoint

ORATOR/ORATOR2's dose comparison is post hoc, pooling two separate trials, not a direct randomized comparison — the confirmatory head-to-head trial is still pending.

Go deeper

An 8-center, 1,514-patient study found a nominal "70 Gy" prescription varies by up to 4% in actual delivered dose across centers. DOI

6. Systemic options once someone's out of surgical/RT salvage

2 findings
Background / pipeline

Two next-gen EGFR-directed agents are showing responses in patients who've failed both anti-PD-1 and cetuximab.

  • Petosemtamab (EGFR x LGR5 bispecific): two phase III trials recruiting — LiGeR-HN1 (+pembrolizumab, 1L PD-L1+) and LiGeR-HN2 (monotherapy vs. investigator's choice).▸

    Key conclusion —The baseline cycle flagged two recruiting phase III programs for this EGFR x LGR5 bispecific.

    Read the paper →

  • Ficerafusp alfa (EGFR/TGF-β fusion): phase I combo data — 31% response rate with pembrolizumab, including a durable response after failing both anti-PD-1 and cetuximab.▸

    Key conclusion —The early combination signal included activity after both anti-PD-1 and cetuximab exposure.

    Read the paper →

Go deeper

Carosi et al. (Crit Rev Oncol Hematol) covers the full EGFR-directed pipeline in one place. DOI

Bottom line for tumor board

The live question in resectable HNSCC has shifted from "add immunotherapy or not" to "who's actually CPS ≥10 / high-risk enough to justify the neoadjuvant delay and toxicity" — your JCO argument is now showing up independently in half a dozen other editorials, alongside genuine defenders of the CPS ≥1 label. Watch ctDNA closely: it's the mechanism several active trials are using to make that selection concrete, and it's plausible it reshapes your surveillance protocol before it reshapes NCCN guidelines.
