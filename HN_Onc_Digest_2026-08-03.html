Highlights — key takeaways

  • Perioperative pembrolizumab (KEYNOTE-689) is FDA-/NCCN-endorsed for CPS ≥1, but the data only really support CPS ≥10 — major pathologic response was 13.7% vs. 1.8% across that cutoff.
  • NIVOPOST-OP is the easier trial to operationalize — adjuvant-only, no neoadjuvant delay, improves locoregional (not distant) control in a defined high-risk surgical population.
  • ctDNA/MRD is becoming a surgical decision tool, not just a research endpoint — multiple recruiting trials use it to gate adjuvant therapy or de-escalated RT, with lead times up to ~15 months over prior detection.
  • Two signals support pulling back on neck dissection extent — selective omission of level IIB in cN0 necks, and (more tentatively) omitting dissection after a strong neoadjuvant response.
  • Cutaneous SCC is one step behind HNSCC on the same neoadjuvant-vs-surgery-first question — expect more patients referred mid-treatment asking to wait and see.

1. Who gets a medical oncology consult before you book the OR

Practice-changing, with caveats

For CPS ≥1 resectable stage III-IV disease, perioperative pembrolizumab is FDA-approved and NCCN-endorsed — but the benefit concentrates at CPS ≥10, and editorials are pushing back on blanket use.

  • KEYNOTE-689 (Uppaluri et al., NEJM): 36-month EFS 57.6% vs 46.4% overall (HR 0.73); major pathologic response 13.7% at CPS ≥10 vs 1.8% at CPS 1-9. 10% of the pembrolizumab arm never reached surgery. DOI
  • NIVOPOST-OP / GORTEC 2018-01 (Bourhis et al., Lancet): adjuvant-only in high-risk resected disease. 3-yr DFS 63.1% vs 52.5% (HR 0.76), PD-L1-independent, driven by locoregional control — doesn't touch your OR planning. DOI
  • Per KEYNOTE-689 supplementary data: 45 progressions during the neoadjuvant window vs 8 in the surgery-first arm, not systematically reported in the primary paper.
  • Pooled grade ≥3 AEs 42.9-44.6%; discontinuation for AEs 12.4-17.7% (Ben Kridis & Khanfir, J Oral Pathol Med). DOI

Counterpoint

Not everyone reads KEYNOTE-689 as overreach — reviews argue even a modest EFS benefit is worth it given surgical completion was largely unaffected (~88% both arms). The disagreement is where you set the bar, not whether the trial is valid. DOI

Go deeper

Imamura et al. (Cancer Treat Rev) for the best head-to-head trial comparison. DOI Brammer et al. (Emory, Transl Cancer Res) for the surgeon-specific angle. DOI

2. Cutaneous SCC: the same argument, one step behind

Hypothesis-generating, moving fast

Cemiplimab is moving earlier into the sequence for high-risk cutaneous SCC — expect referrals mid-treatment with no comparative data yet on when surgery-first vs. neoadjuvant is right.

  • C-POST trial (adjuvant cemiplimab, resected high-risk CSCC): DFS HR 0.32 — largest effect size of any perioperative-ICI trial in this space. DOI
  • Updated European guideline (Stratigos et al., Eur J Cancer) now bakes adjuvant cemiplimab into the algorithm pending MDT discussion. DOI

Counterpoint

Kreft et al. (Eur J Cancer): no head-to-head data for neoadjuvant vs. definitive RT vs. surgery-first, rising patient withdrawal from surgery after deep responses, no staging system built for that decision. DOI

Go deeper

Duke cost-effectiveness model if the conversation turns to cost/access — neoadjuvant modeled as cost-saving and modestly more effective over 10 years. DOI

3. What's going to replace your 3-month post-op CT/PET

Practice-changing within trials

ctDNA/MRD assays are maturing into a surgical/adjuvant-therapy selection tool built directly into trial designs — the thing most likely to change your surveillance in 2-3 years.

  • Tissue-agnostic methylome MRD assay, 325 HNC patients: MRD-positive HR 35.7 for recurrence, lead time up to 14.9 months (mean 4.1), 91% sensitivity / 88% specificity. DOI

4. Where the extent of your dissection is actually being questioned

Hypothesis-generating

Two independent signals support pulling back on neck dissection extent in selected patients — neither is a guideline change yet.

  • Level IIB in cN0 necks: pooled occult metastasis rate 1.2-2.6% across subsites (lowest oral cavity, larynx), 35-study meta-analysis — supports selective omission to reduce SAN morbidity. DOI
  • Omitting dissection after imaging-negative neoadjuvant response in cT3-4N0M0 OSCC (Sun Yat-sen, n=58): no significant difference in nodal control, DFS, or OS. DOI

Counterpoint

The neoadjuvant-response data is a single retrospective series of 58 patients — underpowered to rule out a meaningful difference, and imaging-negative is not pathology-negative. Track it, don't act on it yet.

Go deeper

Review citing series where deep responders avoided mandibulectomy (75%) and free flap reconstruction (72%), with 80-90% laryngeal preservation. DOI

5. Radiation de-escalation — changes your referral conversations

Useful for counseling now

Dose de-escalation in HPV+ oropharynx cancer keeps showing better patient-reported swallowing/QOL without a confirmed efficacy penalty.

  • ORATOR/ORATOR2 pooled analysis: 60 Gy vs. 70 Gy in T1-2 p16+ OPC — 60 Gy cohort had meaningfully better dysphagia scores and less grade 2-3 mucositis at 1 year. DOI
  • RTOG 1016 QOL substudy: cetuximab substitution for cisplatin didn't improve QOL (and was worse on efficacy) — settles a question that still comes up when a patient is cisplatin-ineligible.

Counterpoint

ORATOR/ORATOR2's dose comparison is post hoc, pooling two separate trials, not a direct randomized comparison — the confirmatory head-to-head trial is still pending.

Go deeper

An 8-center, 1,514-patient study found a nominal "70 Gy" prescription varies by up to 4% in actual delivered dose across centers. DOI

6. Systemic options once someone's out of surgical/RT salvage

Background / pipeline

Two next-gen EGFR-directed agents are showing responses in patients who've failed both anti-PD-1 and cetuximab.

  • Petosemtamab (EGFR x LGR5 bispecific): two phase III trials recruiting — LiGeR-HN1 (+pembrolizumab, 1L PD-L1+) and LiGeR-HN2 (monotherapy vs. investigator's choice). DOI
  • Ficerafusp alfa (EGFR/TGF-β fusion): phase I combo data — 31% response rate with pembrolizumab, including a durable response after failing both anti-PD-1 and cetuximab. DOI

Go deeper

Carosi et al. (Crit Rev Oncol Hematol) covers the full EGFR-directed pipeline in one place. DOI

Bottom line for tumor board

The live question in resectable HNSCC has shifted from "add immunotherapy or not" to "who's actually CPS ≥10 / high-risk enough to justify the neoadjuvant delay and toxicity" — your JCO argument is now showing up independently in half a dozen other editorials, alongside genuine defenders of the CPS ≥1 label. Watch ctDNA closely: it's the mechanism several active trials are using to make that selection concrete, and it's plausible it reshapes your surveillance protocol before it reshapes NCCN guidelines.
