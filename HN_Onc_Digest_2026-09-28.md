## Highlights — key takeaways

- **The most actionable signal is operational:** in 6,079 pT4 laryngectomy patients, only 21.2% started postoperative radiotherapy within 42 days, and each additional week of delay was associated with a 3.7% higher adjusted mortality risk.
- **A phase III trial will test neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy against upfront surgery in cT2N0 oral cancer.** It is the right randomized question, but it is not yet recruiting and has no results; standard surgery should not change.
- **CPS <1 deserves a more skeptical systemic-therapy discussion.** A Bayesian meta-analysis of seven randomized trials found a high probability that ICI-based treatment was associated with worse outcomes in this subgroup, but the evidence is aggregated from trial subgroups and mixed settings.
- **cN0 facial/scalp cSCC did not gain from prophylactic parotidectomy in a 46-patient cohort.** Occult parotid disease was 3.8%, complications were 65.4% versus 5.0%, and survival did not differ; the sample is too small to create a universal omission rule.
- **Neck and reconstructive technique signals are practical but nonrandomized.** Imaging reliably identified strictly positive HPV+ nodes, nerve-preserving neck dissection still caused a 45.2% early accessory-nerve conduction drop, and higher fluid volume—not vasopressor use—tracked with more free-flap and medical complications.
- **The systemic pipeline split by setting:** PDS0101 plus pembrolizumab produced no objective responses in the ICI-resistant cohort, while first-line amivantamab plus pembrolizumab produced a 59% ORR in 39 patients; both require randomized confirmation. LiGeR-HN1 and HN2 have closed accrual but post no results.

**Quiet watchlist:** No new decision-impact ctDNA/MRD study, KEYNOTE-689 or NIVOPOST result, radiation-omission trial, or perioperative/adjuvant cSCC randomized readout appeared in the interval. Newly indexed ctDNA material was commentary or review-level evidence and was not promoted into a finding.

## 1. Early oral cancer and response-adapted local therapy move toward comparative testing

**Bottom line:** The new randomized phase III protocol asks whether neoadjuvant sintilimab plus chemotherapy improves event-free survival over upfront surgery in cT2N0 oral SCC. Two retrospective cohorts suggest response can influence local-treatment selection, but neither establishes a safe off-protocol de-escalation rule.

### Phase III cT2N0 oral SCC trial: neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy versus upfront surgery

**Provenance/access:** ClinicalTrials.gov protocol, first posted September 18; not yet recruiting; **no results**.

**Protocol:** NCT07827872 plans to randomize 300 patients with resectable cT2N0M0 oral SCC to sintilimab 200 mg, nab-paclitaxel 260 mg/m², and carboplatin AUC 5 every three weeks before radical primary resection and neck dissection, or to upfront radical surgery. The primary endpoint is 3-year event-free survival. Pathologic complete response, major pathologic response, overall survival, radiographic response, adverse events, and pathologic nodal positivity are secondary outcomes. The estimated primary completion is October 2032.

**Surgical interpretation:** This is a clinically useful superiority design because it compares the new sequence with the actual standard and retains definitive surgery in both arms.

**Limitations/do not overread:** The registry does not specify a validated response-based reduction in primary resection or neck treatment. There are no efficacy, safety, delay, margin, or functional results. Do not alter cT2N0 surgical management on registration alone.

**Source:** [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07827872](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07827872)

### Neoadjuvant ICI in temporal-bone SCC

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; two-center retrospective cohort.

**Methods/results:** Twenty-three patients with temporal-bone-involving SCC received neoadjuvant immune-checkpoint inhibition and response assessment before local treatment. RECIST outcomes were complete response in 6/23 (26.1%), partial response in 4/23 (17.4%), stable disease in 3/23 (13.0%), and progression in 10/23 (43.5%). Two patients with resectable disease moved to nonsurgical management and two moved from lateral temporal-bone resection to mastoidectomy. Three-year overall survival was 100% among patients with complete/partial response or stable disease versus 78% among those with progression (p=.029; reported HR 13.2, 95% CI 1.3–134).

**Surgical interpretation:** Response assessment may help frame the magnitude of temporal-bone surgery in selected patients, while the high early-progression fraction argues for documenting the pre-treatment operation and preserving a prompt surgical pathway.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Retrospective selection, mixed primary temporal-bone and cutaneous tumors, 23 patients, treatment heterogeneity, and an extremely wide survival confidence interval. The cohort does not prove that surgery or adjuvant radiation can be safely omitted.

**Source:** [Otology & Neurotology; DOI 10.1097/MAO.0000000000005078](https://doi.org/10.1097/MAO.0000000000005078)

### TORS versus radical radiotherapy after neoadjuvant therapy

**Provenance/access:** PubMed abstract; two-center retrospective cohort with overlap weighting.

**Methods/results:** Investigators compared 103 patients treated with TORS and 264 treated with radical radiotherapy after neoadjuvant therapy for oropharyngeal carcinoma. At 41 months' median follow-up in the weighted cohort, 3-year PFS was 75.6% versus 74.4% (p=.834), OS 80.5% versus 84.8% (p=.703), locoregional relapse-free survival 76.1% versus 75.0% (p=.909), and distant-metastasis-free survival 80.0% versus 82.0% (p=.941). Radiotherapy produced more xerostomia, mucositis, and leukopenia; TORS produced more hemorrhage and infection, including one fatal hemorrhage.

**Surgical interpretation:** When either modality remains feasible after induction, the observed disease-control estimates support a multidisciplinary choice centered on anatomy, functional priorities, and the different toxicity profiles.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Treatment was not randomized; overlap weighting cannot remove unmeasured selection, surgical quality, response-based routing, HPV-risk imbalance, or center effects. Comparable observed survival is not proof of equivalence.

**Source:** [Journal of Robotic Surgery; DOI 10.1007/s11701-026-03989-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11701-026-03989-z)

**Section counterpoint:** Response is influencing local-treatment selection in prospective protocols and retrospective practice, but none of these sources validates an operation-reduction algorithm outside a trial.

**Go deeper:** The new cT2N0 phase III registry is the most decision-relevant source because it preserves upfront surgery as the comparator and uses event-free survival rather than pathologic response alone.

## 2. Neck and reconstruction: count nodes carefully, protect function, and avoid fluid excess

**Bottom line:** This cycle supplies four practical planning signals: strict radiographic criteria can identify positive HPV+ nodes without reliably reproducing pathologic burden; nerve preservation does not prevent early accessory-nerve dysfunction; higher intraoperative fluid volume tracks with complications; and multidomain geriatric assessment predicts morbidity better than age alone.

### Radiographic nodal counts before TORS in HPV-positive OPSCC

**Provenance/access:** PubMed abstract; blinded retrospective imaging-pathology cohort.

**Methods/results:** Specialized radiologists, blinded to pathology, re-read preoperative imaging in 292 HPV-positive OPSCC patients treated with TORS from 2016–2024. Correlation between clinical and pathologic nodal count was 0.70. A strict radiographic-positive definition yielded 0.94 sensitivity and 0.98 positive predictive value; adding “suspicious” nodes increased sensitivity to 0.96 but lowered specificity to 0.57. Among patients with three radiographically positive nodes, only 5% had five or more pathologic nodes (OR 1.24; p=.58). Accuracy was highest in level II (0.91) and lowest in level III (0.74).

**Surgical interpretation:** A strictly positive imaging read can support candidacy discussion for upfront TORS, but suspected-node inflation should not be treated as a pathologic count or automatic adjuvant-treatment assignment.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Single-institution surgical cohort, retrospective re-read, selection for TORS, and no external validation. High PPV does not establish that imaging can replace neck dissection pathology.

**Source:** [Oral Oncology; DOI 10.1016/j.oraloncology.2026.108127](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oraloncology.2026.108127)

### Accessory-nerve conduction after nerve-preserving neck dissection

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; prospective physiologic cohort.

**Methods/results:** Forty-three oral-cavity SCC patients underwent nerve-preserving selective neck dissection (n=35) or modified radical neck dissection (n=8). In 41 unilateral cases, compound motor action-potential amplitude fell 45.2%, from 9.15±2.04 mV at baseline to 5.01±2.47 mV at three weeks (p<.0001), with partial recovery to 6.26±2.23 mV at three months (p<.001). The exploratory asymmetry index normalized after selective dissection but remained numerically worse after modified radical dissection; the latter comparison was not significant and included only six patients.

**Surgical interpretation:** “Nerve preserved” should not be equated with “shoulder function preserved.” Early rehabilitation and explicit counseling remain appropriate even after selective dissection.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Small cohort, very small modified-radical subgroup, three-month follow-up, adjuvant therapy not modeled as a covariate, and no validated shoulder-function endpoint. The physiology does not argue against an oncologically indicated dissection.

**Source:** [Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery; DOI 10.1007/s10006-026-01640-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10006-026-01640-7)

### Vasopressors versus fluid volume in oral-cavity free-flap reconstruction

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; retrospective 826-patient cohort with inverse-probability weighting.

**Methods/results:** Among oral-cavity free-flap cases from 2008–2019, vasopressor use rose from 69.7% to 81.7% after 2012 and was not associated with medical complications (OR 1.36; 95% CI 0.76–2.43) or flap complications (OR 2.24; 0.66–7.57). Each additional liter of intraoperative fluid was associated with more medical complications (OR 1.33; 1.17–1.52) and flap complications (OR 1.30; 1.06–1.59).

**Surgical interpretation:** The data support avoiding reflexive fluid loading solely to avoid vasopressors and favor a hemodynamic strategy that limits unnecessary volume.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Retrospective practice evolution, confounding by indication, broad confidence intervals for flap events, and no randomized vasopressor protocol. “No association” does not prove vasopressors are risk-free in every flap.

**Source:** [Head & Neck; DOI 10.1002/hed.70481](https://doi.org/10.1002/hed.70481)

### Multidomain geriatric assessment before curative oral-cancer surgery

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; prospective single-center risk-stratification cohort.

**Methods/results:** Seventy-four patients aged 70 or older underwent assessment across eight domains before curative oral SCC surgery. Thirty-four (46%) had deficits in at least two domains and were classified high risk. Major 30-day morbidity occurred in 17/74 (23%), including three deaths (4.1%). High-risk status independently predicted major morbidity (OR 6.03; 95% CI 1.10–33.04; p=.039), while chronological age did not (OR 0.70; 0.16–3.08; p=.64). No single domain remained independently predictive.

**Surgical interpretation:** Use multidomain assessment to target prehabilitation, consent, and postoperative support; age alone is a poor gatekeeper for curative surgery.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Small single-center cohort, wide confidence interval, no external validation, and no evidence that assessment-guided intervention reduces complications. A high-risk classification is not a contraindication.

**Source:** [European Journal of Surgical Oncology; DOI 10.1016/j.ejso.2026.112153](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejso.2026.112153)

**Section counterpoint:** These studies refine counseling and workflow but are not randomized technique comparisons. Apply them as risk signals, not absolute eligibility or reconstruction rules.

**Go deeper:** The 826-patient free-flap cohort is the most operationally useful source; the prospective accessory-nerve study is the clearest reminder that anatomic preservation and functional preservation are different endpoints.

## 3. Postoperative radiotherapy delay is the modifiable hazard this cycle

**Bottom line:** Two cohorts show that the six-week PORT target is missed in most laryngectomy and oral-cavity pathways. The laryngectomy study links increasing delay with mortality; the oral-cavity study identifies length of stay and readmission as practical coordination targets.

### PORT timing after total laryngectomy for pT4 disease

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; National Cancer Database retrospective cohort.

**Methods/results:** Among 6,079 adults with nonmetastatic pT4 laryngeal SCC treated with total laryngectomy from 2010–2022, 21.2% began PORT within 42 days, 66.2% began 43–180 days after surgery, and 12.6% received no PORT within 180 days. Each additional week of delay was associated with a 3.7% higher adjusted mortality risk. Receiving radiation at a different facility from the operating institution was associated with delay; older age, greater comorbidity, and lower neighborhood income were associated with omission.

**Surgical interpretation:** The operative plan should include a pre-booked adjuvant pathway, early wound and dental readiness, and closed-loop transfer when radiation will occur elsewhere.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Registry data cannot fully adjust for postoperative complications, disease severity, access, or immortal-time and selection effects. The association supports workflow improvement but does not prove that every observed week of delay directly causes mortality.

**Source:** [Head & Neck; DOI 10.1002/hed.70482](https://doi.org/10.1002/hed.70482)

### PORT timing after oral-cavity cancer resection

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; integrated-system retrospective cohort.

**Methods/results:** Of 252 resected oral-cavity cancer patients who received PORT in Kaiser Permanente Southern California from 2015–2025, 225 (89.3%) started later than six weeks. Median time to PORT was 8.57 weeks (IQR 2.86). Random-forest modeling ranked postoperative length of stay, margin status, concurrent systemic therapy, and readmission within 60 days as the largest contributors to timing; postoperative length of stay was the only significant predictor in univariate analysis.

**Surgical interpretation:** Discharge barriers, margin finalization, readmission prevention, and early multidisciplinary handoff are the most concrete leverage points before the patient leaves the surgical service.

**Limitations/do not overread:** One integrated system, retrospective prediction, no external validation, and no survival model in this study. Variable importance does not establish causation or a guaranteed intervention target.

**Source:** [Head & Neck; DOI 10.1002/hed.70480](https://doi.org/10.1002/hed.70480)

**Section counterpoint:** PORT delay is partly a marker of complications and access barriers. The studies support redesigning the pathway while avoiding causal overstatement from observational timing data.

**Go deeper:** The 6,079-patient laryngectomy analysis provides the clearest risk signal; the oral-cavity cohort is more useful for locating modifiable workflow bottlenecks.

## 4. Cutaneous SCC: parotid restraint and more precise perineural-spread imaging

**Bottom line:** A small cN0 cohort argues against routine prophylactic parotidectomy when the morbidity is substantial; MRI neurography closely tracked nerve involvement but was less reliable for detailed skull-base zoning; anti-PD-1 therapy produced durable imaging control in a highly selected perineural-spread series.

### Prophylactic parotidectomy in cN0 face/scalp cSCC

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; retrospective 46-patient cohort.

**Methods/results:** Twenty-six patients underwent prophylactic parotidectomy and 20 were observed. Occult parotid metastasis was found in 1/26 (3.8%), while occult neck metastasis was found in 5/26 (19.2%). Complications occurred in 65.4% versus 5.0% (p<.001), including facial-nerve paralysis in 42.3% of the parotidectomy group. Five-year recurrence-free survival (p=.971) and disease-specific survival (p=.911) did not differ.

**Surgical interpretation:** In a clinically node-negative face/scalp primary, a parotid-sparing strategy with disciplined surveillance is reasonable to discuss rather than treating prophylactic parotidectomy as routine.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Only 46 patients, one occult parotid event, retrospective selection over two decades, undefined risk balance between groups, and no randomized comparison. The data do not justify omission when clinical, imaging, or pathologic risk is high.

**Source:** [Acta Oto-Laryngologica; DOI 10.1080/00016489.2026.2732481](https://doi.org/10.1080/00016489.2026.2732481)

### MRI neurography versus resected-nerve histology

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; retrospective imaging-pathology correlation.

**Methods/results:** Seventy patients with suspected large-nerve perineural spread from head-and-neck cSCC contributed 90 nerves. Two MRI-positive nerves were negative on histology, producing a reported positive predictive value of 98%. Three MRI-negative nerves were positive on histology; the authors report 97% positive agreement. Zonal agreement was 83% using Williams' classification and 69% using the institution's modified system; modified-zone agreement fell to 50% for zone 2a and 40% for zone 2b.

**Surgical interpretation:** MRI neurography is reliable for detecting involved nerves, but operative skull-base planning should retain uncertainty when the decision depends on fine subdivision within zone 2.

**Limitations/do not overread:** The cohort was enriched for suspected disease and resected nerves, so the reported PPV is not population screening performance. Zonal subgroup sizes were limited, and “positive agreement” is not interchangeable with sensitivity.

**Source:** [ANZ Journal of Surgery; DOI 10.1111/ans.70943](https://doi.org/10.1111/ans.70943)

### Imaging response of cSCC perineural spread to anti-PD-1 therapy

**Provenance/access:** PubMed abstract; nine-patient retrospective series plus narrative literature review. Published September 14 and indexed during this cycle.

**Methods/results:** Among nine patients with MRI surveillance of cSCC perineural spread treated with anti-PD-1 therapy, best imaging outcome was complete response in three, partial response in five, and stable disease in one; none had progressive perineural disease at a median 37 months. One patient showed early pseudoprogression. Seven of nine had zone 3 intracranial involvement. The narrative review identified 101 reported patients, with complete response 35%, partial response 43%, stable disease 9%, and progression 10%.

**Surgical interpretation:** Durable radiographic control is possible even with advanced perineural spread, but early MRI worsening requires clinicoradiologic review before declaring failure or abandoning organ-preserving therapy.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Nine selected patients, retrospective imaging interpretation, narrative rather than systematic review, heterogeneous regimens and follow-up, and no surgical comparator. Imaging response does not prove eradication along the nerve or define when surgery can be omitted.

**Source:** [American Journal of Neuroradiology; DOI 10.3174/ajnr.A9637](https://doi.org/10.3174/ajnr.A9637)

**Section counterpoint:** The parotid and perineural-spread data point toward selective treatment, not blanket omission. Clinical burden, named-nerve anatomy, salvageability, and surveillance reliability remain decisive.

**Go deeper:** The MRI-histology correlation is the strongest anatomy study; use the anti-PD-1 imaging series primarily to recognize response lag and pseudoprogression.

## 5. Systemic therapy: CPS <1 caution, setting-specific vaccine activity, and phase III accrual complete

**Bottom line:** The CPS <1 meta-analysis raises a credible harm signal that should sharpen systemic-therapy selection, but it is subgroup-level evidence rather than a new randomized treatment rule. PDS0101 activity was confined to ICI-naïve disease; amivantamab plus pembrolizumab produced a high first-line response rate in a small uncontrolled cohort. Both LiGeR phase III trials now show completed accrual and no posted results.

### Bayesian meta-analysis of ICI-based therapy in PD-L1 CPS <1 HNSCC

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; systematic review and Bayesian/frequentist random-effects meta-analysis.

**Methods/results:** Seven phase III randomized trials contributed 630 CPS <1 patients. In recurrent/metastatic disease (three trials), the Bayesian OS HR was 1.42 (95% credible interval 0.93–2.06), with a 95.6% posterior probability that HR exceeded 1.0; the frequentist HR was 1.46 (95% CI 1.17–1.83; p<.001). In locally advanced disease (four trials), the Bayesian HR for a composite of DFS/PFS/EFS was 1.19 (0.72–2.06), with 76.8% probability that HR exceeded 1.0. Across all settings, the Bayesian OS HR was 1.43 (0.97–2.07), with 96.2% probability that HR exceeded 1.0.

**Surgical interpretation:** For CPS <1 recurrence or perioperative discussion, avoid treating “ICI eligible” as synonymous with “ICI likely to help.” Document the competing non-ICI options, disease setting, and the limits of subgroup evidence.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Aggregate subgroup data, few trials, mixed disease settings and endpoints, possible interaction and reporting limitations, and no patient-level adjustment. This analysis does not prove that every CPS <1 patient is harmed or replace a setting-specific guideline and multidisciplinary decision.

**Source:** [JAMA Oncology; DOI 10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.3626](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.3626)

### PDS0101 plus pembrolizumab in HPV16-positive recurrent/metastatic HNSCC

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; multicenter phase II nonrandomized trial.

**Methods/results:** VERSATILE-002 enrolled 88 patients; 87 received treatment and 75 entered the modified intention-to-treat efficacy analysis. Among 53 ICI-naïve patients with CPS ≥1, central-review ORR was 34.0%, median PFS 5.3 months (95% CI 2.10–9.00), and median OS 39.3 months (23.90–not evaluable). Among 22 ICI-resistant patients, no objective responses were observed and median OS was 14.8 months (8.50–26.00). Grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events occurred in 13.8%.

**Surgical interpretation:** The vaccine combination remains a first-line research strategy in HPV16-positive disease; the absence of responses after ICI resistance argues against extrapolating the naïve-cohort signal to salvage.

**Limitations/do not overread:** No randomized pembrolizumab control, efficacy population smaller than enrollment, predominantly male and White cohort, and separate cohorts with different eligibility. The long observed OS in the naïve cohort cannot be attributed to PDS0101.

**Source:** [JAMA Oncology; DOI 10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.3492](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.3492)

### First-line amivantamab plus pembrolizumab

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; phase Ib/II OrigAMI-4 cohort.

**Methods/results:** Thirty-nine previously untreated recurrent/metastatic HNSCC patients with CPS ≥1 received subcutaneous amivantamab plus intravenous pembrolizumab every three weeks; p16-positive oropharyngeal cancer and prior anti-EGFR or anti-PD-(L)1 therapy were excluded. At 13.6 months' median follow-up, confirmed ORR was 59% (95% CI 42%–74%), including seven complete responses (18%). Clinical-benefit rate was 74% (58%–87%), target lesions shrank in 82%, and median PFS was 7.7 months (5.0–not reached). Median response duration and OS were not reached.

**Surgical interpretation:** The response depth warrants randomized study and explicit protocols for consolidative local therapy; it is not yet a basis for conversion surgery or comparison with standard first-line regimens.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Thirty-nine patients, no control, investigator-assessed primary endpoint, short follow-up, selected CPS-positive population, and exclusion of p16-positive OPSCC. The abstract reports no new safety signal but does not provide detailed event rates.

**Source:** [Clinical Cancer Research; DOI 10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-1372](https://doi.org/10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-1372)

### Update since August 3: LiGeR-HN2 has completed accrual

**Provenance/access:** ClinicalTrials.gov live registry, updated September 25; active, not recruiting; **no results**.

**Protocol/status:** NCT06496178 now lists 621 actual participants in the open-label randomized phase III comparison of petosemtamab with investigator's choice of cetuximab, methotrexate, or docetaxel after prior treatment. Overall survival remains the primary endpoint, and estimated primary completion is February 2028.

**Surgical interpretation:** Accrual completion moves the post-platinum/anti-PD-1 question from recruitment to follow-up; it does not yet create a salvage option.

**Limitations/do not overread:** No efficacy, safety, or subgroup results are posted. The status update should not be interpreted as a positive readout.

**Source:** [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06496178](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06496178)

### Update since August 3: LiGeR-HN1 has completed accrual

**Provenance/access:** ClinicalTrials.gov live registry, updated September 25; active, not recruiting; **no results**.

**Protocol/status:** NCT06525220 now lists 699 actual participants in the open-label randomized phase III comparison of first-line petosemtamab plus pembrolizumab with pembrolizumab alone in PD-L1-positive recurrent/metastatic HNSCC. Overall survival and blinded-central-review ORR remain co-primary endpoints; estimated primary completion is January 2028.

**Surgical interpretation:** The definitive first-line combination question is now in follow-up. Continue to use trial participation or established therapy rather than assuming benefit from early-phase response data.

**Limitations/do not overread:** No results are posted. Closing accrual is a program milestone, not evidence of efficacy or safety.

**Source:** [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06525220](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06525220)

**Section counterpoint:** The strongest completed evidence is a subgroup meta-analysis that raises caution, while the promising response cohorts are uncontrolled and the phase III registries have no outcomes. Setting and comparator matter more than headline response rate.

**Go deeper:** The CPS <1 meta-analysis is the most practice-informing source; read its credible intervals and subgroup design before treating the harm probability as an individual-patient rule.

## Bottom line for tumor board

This cycle changes workflow more than it changes treatment. Build the adjuvant plan before laryngectomy or oral-cavity resection so wound, dental, pathology, and referral bottlenecks do not consume the six-week PORT window. For cN0 face/scalp cSCC, prophylactic parotidectomy should be individualized against its observed morbidity; for named-nerve spread, use MRI neurography confidently for detection but cautiously for fine zone-2 mapping and for early post-ICI worsening. In HPV-positive OPSCC, do not convert radiographic “suspicious” nodes into a pathologic count. In recurrent/metastatic disease, make CPS <1 a deliberate risk-benefit discussion, recognize that PDS0101 activity did not extend to ICI-resistant disease, and wait for randomized confirmation of amivantamab and petosemtamab strategies. The new cT2N0 oral-cancer phase III protocol is a trial opportunity, not a reason to change upfront surgery today.
