# Head & Neck Oncology Digest — August 31, 2026

**For the surgeon · Literature window:** August 7–31, 2026
**Sources:** PubMed-indexed primary literature, official journal pages, open full text where available, and official ClinicalTrials.gov records.

All paper identifiers and numeric claims were checked against PubMed or the journal record. Registry protocols were checked against ClinicalTrials.gov on August 31, 2026. Registry-only findings are explicitly labeled and have no results. All 17 DOI/NCT records are new to this cycle; none appeared in the August 6 digest. This overdue cycle closes that full 25-day gap.

## Highlights

- Response-adapted surgery is ready for prospective testing, not off-trial adoption: indirect pooled survival was similar to baseline-planned surgery, but the evidence is nonrandomized.
- Across 60 HPV-ctDNA studies and 8,234 patients, reported analytic performance was high, while clinical decision-impact evidence remained sparse.
- A 2,008-respondent survey showed broad agreement on elective-neck risk thresholds but surprisingly broad inclusion of low-risk levels.
- ConCERT provides randomized phase III support for weekly cisplatin with definitive RT, with fewer severe adverse events and hospitalizations.
- In neoadjuvant cSCC cohorts, pathology outperformed imaging as a response readout; surgery omission was not tested.
- Danvatirsen added essentially no response signal to pembrolizumab in randomized phase II testing.

## 1. Can treatment response safely change the planned operation?

**Bottom line:** New data strengthen the case for prospectively testing response-adapted surgery after neoadjuvant therapy; they do not establish a safe off-trial resection-omission rule. This extends [the prior cycle's perioperative-IO debate](HN_Onc_Digest_2026-08-06.html#perioperative-io).

### Response-adapted versus baseline-planned surgery after neoadjuvant checkpoint inhibition

**Provenance/access:** Systematic review/meta-analysis; structured abstract available, full text restricted.

**Methods/scope:** PubMed, Embase, Cochrane, and trial-registry searches covering 2020–2025 identified four response-adapted surgery (RAS) studies with 202 patients and 11 baseline-planned surgery (BPS) studies with 403 patients with resectable HNSCC after neoadjuvant checkpoint inhibition.

**Results:** Pooled EFS was 83.3% (95% CI 76.9–88.2) with RAS and 82.0% (75.2–87.2) with BPS (p=.751). OS was 92.3% (87.5–95.3) versus 91.4% (80.3–96.5; p=.839). pCR was 41.7% versus 19.8% (13.3–29.6; p=.001), while MPR was 59.6% versus 48.5% (p=.245). RAS reports described greater organ preservation and fewer mandibulectomies/free-flap reconstructions. The accessible abstract prints a pCR confidence interval that appears internally inconsistent, so it is not reproduced as a validated range here.

**Surgical interpretation:** The synthesis makes response-guided resection trials reasonable and helps define what they must protect: margin control, salvageability, function, and EFS.

**Limitations/do not overread:** This is an indirect study-level comparison with differing selection, regimens, response assessments, and follow-up—not a randomized comparison or noninferiority result. Do not reduce a planned operation outside a protocol on these pooled data.

**Source:** [Oral Oncology; DOI 10.1016/j.oraloncology.2026.108098](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oraloncology.2026.108098)

### Neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy versus immunotherapy alone

**Provenance/access:** Structured abstract; single-center retrospective off-trial cohort.

**Methods/scope:** 86 patients treated July 2024–March 2026 received at least one, typically two, preoperative cycles followed by surgery. Fifty-eight received chemotherapy plus immunotherapy and 28 immunotherapy alone.

**Results:** Overall pCR was 25/86 (29%; 95% CI 21–39) and MPR 15/86 (17%; 95% CI 11–27). Deep response occurred in 39/58 chemoimmunotherapy patients (67%; 95% CI 54–78) versus 1/28 immunotherapy-only patients (3.6%; 95% CI 0.6–17.7). The propensity-adjusted OR was 29.1 (95% CI 6.7–274.7). The abstract reports few adverse-event-related surgical delays but gives no exact denominator.

**Surgical interpretation:** The large response separation supports trial referral and multidisciplinary preoperative review, and may make functional surgery trials feasible if validated.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Nonrandom treatment selection, residual confounding, a very wide CI, and no mature oncologic outcomes. Pathologic response is not survival; these data do not make surgery optional.

**Source:** [JAMA Otolaryngology; DOI 10.1001/jamaoto.2026.2259](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoto.2026.2259)

### NCT07751276: finotonlimab plus platinum/taxane before surgery

**Provenance/access:** ClinicalTrials.gov protocol, first posted August 7; not yet recruiting; **no results**.

**Protocol:** Open-label randomized phase II, planned n=142, stage II resectable nonnasopharyngeal HNSCC. The experimental arm gives two 21-day cycles of finotonlimab 200 mg day 1, nab-paclitaxel 100 mg/m² days 1/8/15, and carboplatin AUC 4 or cisplatin 75 mg/m² day 1, followed by surgery, pathology-directed adjuvant therapy, and six q3-week finotonlimab maintenance cycles. Control is upfront surgery with risk-adapted adjuvant therapy. Primary endpoint: 2-year EFS; secondary endpoints include MPR, pCR, OS, and safety.

**Surgical interpretation:** This is a useful randomized sequencing test. Because the experimental arm includes maintenance, any eventual EFS difference will not isolate the preoperative component.

**Limitations/do not overread:** No participants, response data, surgical outcomes, or survival results are posted. The regimen is neither proven nor standard care.

**Source:** [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07751276](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07751276)

**Section counterpoint:** Better response signals are not a validated response-to-resection algorithm. Preserve baseline surgical planning unless a protocol prospectively defines how response changes the operation.

## 2. HPV ctDNA: strong accuracy, no omission rule

**Bottom line:** HPV ctDNA shows strong reported analytic performance, but this cycle adds no completed trial proving that acting on it improves outcomes. That boundary matters alongside [the prior registry signals](HN_Onc_Digest_2026-08-06.html#ctdna-mrd).

### HPV ctDNA systematic review

**Provenance/access:** Systematic review; structured abstract.

**Methods/scope:** Literature from January 2012–September 2025 yielded 60 studies/8,234 patients; 41 (68.3%) were OPSCC-only, 17 (28.3%) mixed, and median follow-up was 23 months. Thirty-three studies (55%) were prospective, 19 (31.7%) retrospective, and 40 (66.7%) evaluated curative-intent care. Plasma was used in 55 studies; ddPCR in 40, qPCR in 16, and NGS in four. Thirty-eight evaluated prognosis and only four screening/diagnostic applications.

**Results:** Median reported sensitivity was 91.1% and specificity 99.4%; among OPSCC-only studies, 89.4% and 99.4%. Dynamic clearance was associated with outcomes.

**Surgical interpretation:** ctDNA is credible for risk-stratification and surveillance research. The decisive question remains whether a specified assay and time point can safely alter adjuvant therapy, imaging, or salvage.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Heterogeneous populations, platforms, time points, thresholds, and mainly observational associations. Descriptive medians are not pooled performance for an individual assay and do not authorize treatment omission.

**Source:** [Oral Oncology; DOI 10.1016/j.oraloncology.2026.108116](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oraloncology.2026.108116)

### NeckTAR-IN prospective protocol

**Provenance/access:** Open full-text BMJ Open protocol; prospective multicenter ancillary study; **no outcomes**.

**Protocol:** About 59 node-positive, locally advanced HNSCC patients treated with chemoradiation will have blood collected at months 1 and 3. A customized NGS panel covers 188 point-mutation targets, six copy-number targets, and high-risk HPV E7. The primary endpoint is PPV of month-3 ctDNA for incomplete clinical/radiographic response at month 3; accuracy, kinetics, and survival are secondary. Clinicians are blinded. Twelve patients had enrolled by February 2026.

**Surgical interpretation:** Blinding appropriately prevents biomarker-driven intervention during validation. The design may establish whether a result is sufficiently trustworthy to study as a future neck-management gate.

**Limitations/do not overread:** No formal sample-size calculation, small target, complex assay, and no treatment assignment. This protocol cannot show that ctDNA-guided neck dissection or surveillance improves outcomes.

**Source:** [BMJ Open; DOI 10.1136/bmjopen-2026-118532](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2026-118532)

## 3. Neck decisions: thresholds, ENE depth, and neural ALT

**Bottom line:** This cycle adds preference data, ENE granularity, and a small randomized reconstruction trial—not evidence to expand elective levels or de-intensify adjuvant therapy.

### International elective-neck threshold survey

**Provenance/access:** Structured abstract; international web survey.

**Methods/results:** Of 2,077 responses, 2,008 were analyzed. Fifty-five percent of all respondents would offer elective neck dissection at occult risk ≤10% and 86% at 20%; among head-and-neck surgeons, 57% and 92%. Even at predicted level-specific risk ≤5%, surgeons reported including IIB in 78%, IV in 75%, and V in 69%. Ninety percent preferred individualized risk plus patient discussion over a fixed threshold.

**Surgical interpretation:** Level-specific risk estimates can expose practice variation and support shared decisions; many elective fields may be broader than clinicians' stated threshold logic.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Voluntary web participation, uncertain representativeness, and hypothetical choices. This neither validates a threshold nor proves omission of a level safe.

**Source:** [Head & Neck; DOI 10.1002/hed.70444](https://doi.org/10.1002/hed.70444)

### Minor versus major extranodal extension in oral-cavity SCC

**Provenance/access:** Structured abstract; single-center retrospective cohort.

**Methods/results:** Among 349 surgically treated OCSCC patients (2002–2022), 101 were node-positive and 47 had ENE: 16 minor (≤2 mm) and 31 major (>2 mm). DFS was worse for major ENE (p=.016); OS was not significantly different (p=.34). Adjusted major-versus-minor DFS HR was 5.47 (95% CI 1.19–25.08; p=.02).

**Surgical interpretation:** Recording ENE extent may improve recurrence discussion and future risk-stratified trials.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Only 47 ENE cases, wide CI, retrospective single-center design, and two decades of treatment evolution. Do not infer that minor ENE can safely avoid standard adjuvant intensification.

**Source:** [International Journal of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery; DOI 10.1016/j.ijom.2026.08.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijom.2026.08.009)

### Neural ALT flap after hemiglossectomy

**Provenance/access:** Structured abstract; randomized trial, n=40.

**Methods/results:** Patients reconstructed with ALT flaps were randomized to no nerve anastomosis (n=19) or neural ALT (n=21) and evaluated at 2, 6, and 12 months. Neural ALT improved two-point discrimination and touch pressure (p<.05), speech and swallowing at every time point, UW-QOL emotional/swallowing domains, and reduced flap atrophy. The abstract does not provide functional effect magnitudes.

**Surgical interpretation:** Sensory coaptation is a plausible functional refinement when anatomy and operative conditions permit.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Small single-center trial, uncertain blinding, missing effect magnitudes, limited follow-up. Do not promise a specific recovery or generalize to larger composite defects.

**Source:** [Annals of Surgical Oncology; DOI 10.1245/s10434-026-20302-2](https://doi.org/10.1245/s10434-026-20302-2)

## 4. Weekly cisplatin clears phase III; RT omission remains investigational

**Bottom line:** ConCERT supports weekly cisplatin as a less toxic concurrent schedule within its trial context. The remaining additions are a heterogeneous review and two protocols with no results; none establishes reduced postoperative volume or response-adapted dose as standard.

### ConCERT: weekly versus 3-weekly cisplatin

**Provenance/access:** Structured abstract; open-label multicenter randomized phase III.

**Methods:** 278 Indian patients with nonnasopharyngeal locally advanced HNSCC were randomized to cisplatin 40 mg/m² weekly or 100 mg/m² every 3 weeks with definitive 70 Gy RT; 272 were analyzed. Median follow-up was 43.9 months. Noninferiority used a −10-percentage-point margin.

**Results:** Two-year LRC was 61.3% weekly versus 51.1% every 3 weeks, absolute difference 10.2 points with a one-sided 95% lower bound of 0.3. HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.50–1.05; p=.091). Median PFS was 20.3 versus 17.9 months (p=.204); OS 24.9 versus 22.2 months (p=.501). Grade ≥3 adverse events were 44.7% versus 60.7% (p=.018), and hospitalization 20.3% versus 36.8% (p=.004).

**Surgical interpretation:** This adds level-I evidence when concurrent cisplatin schedule, recovery, and treatment completion are discussed in definitive or postoperative pathways.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Open-label; unexpectedly low control-arm LRC; generous margin; accessible abstract lacks HPV/subsite, adherence, and cumulative-dose detail. Do not claim superiority or extrapolate beyond comparable patients and RT.

**Source:** [Journal of the National Cancer Institute; DOI 10.1093/jnci/djag276](https://doi.org/10.1093/jnci/djag276)

### HPV-negative RT de-escalation systematic review

**Provenance/access:** Structured abstract; systematic review.

**Methods/results:** PubMed and Embase screening of 3,156 records found 14 published prospective studies (10 definitive, four postoperative) and 23 ongoing studies. Elective nodal failure was 0–4.6%, with most recurrences in high-dose regions. Two randomized trials preserved control while reducing acute toxicity; one stopped for futility.

**Surgical interpretation:** Postoperative reduction should remain protocol-defined with careful recording of margins, ENE, nodal burden, subsite, and recurrence geography.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Small studies, mixed/incompletely specified HPV status, disparate dose/volume interventions, and conflicting randomized results. A low elective-field failure rate does not prove equal survival or function.

**Source:** [Oral Oncology; DOI 10.1016/j.oraloncology.2026.108095](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oraloncology.2026.108095)

### CLEAR: postoperative low-risk neck-level omission

**Provenance/access:** ClinicalTrials.gov protocol, first posted August 10; recruiting; **no results**.

**Protocol:** Spanish single-arm phase II, n=101. Eligibility requires ≥18 nodes per dissected neck and pN0 or one pN1 node <1 cm without ENE or skip metastasis in pT1–2N1 or pT3–4N0/N1 disease. IMRT/VMAT treats the primary bed to 60 Gy/30 fractions or 66 Gy/33; low-risk dissected cervical levels are omitted. Cisplatin is permitted for positive or close (<5 mm) margins. Primary outcome is 24-month recurrence in unirradiated levels versus historical 6–8%; toxicity, QOL, and lymphopenia are secondary.

**Surgical interpretation:** The protocol directly tests pathology-selected postoperative field reduction and requires high-quality neck dissection/pathology.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Single arm, historical comparator, stringent eligibility, no outcomes. Do not omit levels on this registry outside the study.

**Source:** [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07755449](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07755449)

### IDEAL-RT: response-selected HPV-negative dose reduction

**Provenance/access:** ClinicalTrials.gov protocol, first posted August 28; active, not recruiting; **no results**.

**Protocol:** Open-label multicenter phase II noninferiority study, n=180, HPV/p16-negative nonnasopharyngeal locally advanced HNSCC with ≥50% RECIST response after 2–3 cycles of platinum plus PD-1 therapy. De-escalated RT: GTV 60 Gy, CTV1 50 Gy, CTV2 45 Gy in 25 fractions. Control: 69.96/60.06/50.96 Gy in 33/33/28 fractions. Both groups then receive camrelizumab 200 mg q3 weeks ×8. Primary endpoint: 2-year PFS; secondary OS, LRC, DMFS, toxicity, QOL, and MDADI.

**Surgical interpretation:** Response selection is more biologically discriminating than HPV status alone and merits prospective testing.

**Limitations/do not overread:** No efficacy/safety results. Induction response may enrich prognosis without proving treatment-effect modification.

**Source:** [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07791602](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07791602)

## 5. After neoadjuvant PD-1 in cSCC, pathology outruns imaging

**Bottom line:** Post-treatment pathology may stratify risk better than imaging, but these retrospective cohorts do not validate surgery omission or a gene-expression-directed adjuvant plan.

### Residual nodal-disease model after neoadjuvant immunotherapy

**Provenance/access:** Structured abstract; single-center retrospective cohort.

**Methods/results:** 82 resectable head-and-neck cSCC patients received ≥2 neoadjuvant immunotherapy doses then surgery. Median follow-up was 31.6 months, with 24 DFS events and 14 deaths. Nodal pCR occurred in 27 (32.9%). The iN model used residual positive-node count, residual ENE, and viable tumor percentage, assigning low/intermediate/high risk to 39/22/21 patients. Three-year DFS was 91.2%/70.8%/25.9% (p<.001). High-versus-low DFS HR was 13.42 (95% CI 3.85–46.77); OS HR 20.86 (2.65–164.34).

**Surgical interpretation:** Detailed post-IO nodal pathology may help identify trial candidates for intensified surveillance/adjuvant therapy and should be captured beyond ypN alone.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Few events, very wide CIs, potential overfitting, no external validation or randomized treatment comparison. Do not use iN to select therapy yet.

**Source:** [Annals of Surgical Oncology; DOI 10.1245/s10434-026-20338-4](https://doi.org/10.1245/s10434-026-20338-4)

### Imaging versus pathology after neoadjuvant cemiplimab

**Provenance/access:** Structured abstract; retrospective cohort, n=18.

**Methods/results:** Eighteen head-and-neck cSCC patients treated 2019–2024 received neoadjuvant cemiplimab then curative surgery. Eleven (61%) achieved pCR and two (11%) MPR, combined 72%. Among 16 imaging-evaluable patients, 33% had partial response and none complete response.

**Surgical interpretation:** Residual imaging abnormality after PD-1 therapy may not equal viable tumor; pathology remains critical and response-adapted surgery requires validated multimodal criteria.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Selected, retrospective, single-institution cohort without survival analysis. Every patient underwent surgery; omission was not tested.

**Source:** [Head & Neck; DOI 10.1002/hed.70438](https://doi.org/10.1002/hed.70438)

### Integrated 40-gene expression profile model

**Provenance/access:** Structured abstract; retrospective validation with industry-employed authors.

**Methods/results:** 572 high-risk cSCC cases were classified using 40-GEP plus immunosuppression, location, differentiation, diameter, and perineural invasion into 1A/1B/2A/2B. Classes stratified metastasis and local recurrence (p<.001). Class 1A maintained >95% NPV across NCCN subsets. Class 2B comprised 12.9% and had 3-year MFS 66.2%. The abstract states an association with adjuvant-RT benefit but gives no effect magnitude.

**Surgical interpretation:** The model may complement, not replace, staging and pathology in surveillance and trial discussions.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Retrospective, no randomized RT allocation, no head-and-neck-only breakdown, and company conflicts. Prognostic separation does not prove predictive benefit from RT.

**Source:** [Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology; DOI 10.1016/j.jaad.2026.08.075](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaad.2026.08.075)

## 6. Negative STAT3 trial; a post-checkpoint ADC enters phase III

**Bottom line:** STAT3 antisense did not improve response with pembrolizumab; a newly posted randomized phase III ADC study has no outcomes.

### Danvatirsen plus pembrolizumab

**Provenance/access:** Structured abstract; randomized open-label phase II (NCT05814666).

**Methods/results:** First-line PD-L1 CPS≥1 recurrent/metastatic HNSCC was randomized 2:1 to danvatirsen 3 mg/kg weekly plus pembrolizumab 200 mg q3 weeks or pembrolizumab alone. Of 69 randomized, 66 were treated (45 versus 21). ORR was 15.6% versus 14.3% (risk difference 1.3 points; p=1.0). Treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 97.8% versus 85.7%. Enrollment stopped for lack of response in CPS 1–19 after ≥1 scan/~6 weeks and CPS≥20 after ≥2 scans/~12 weeks.

**Surgical interpretation:** For unresectable recurrence counseling, this is a useful negative trial: danvatirsen did not rescue pembrolizumab response.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Small sample, ORR-focused early stopping, and no mature survival inference. The result does not invalidate other STAT3 strategies.

**Source:** [Clinical Cancer Research; DOI 10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-0819](https://doi.org/10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-0819)

### NCT07768189: CRB-701 versus investigator's choice

**Provenance/access:** ClinicalTrials.gov protocol, first posted August 17; not recruiting; **no results**.

**Protocol:** Planned phase III n=260 with recurrent/metastatic oropharyngeal SCC after platinum and PD-(L)1 therapy. CRB-701 3.6 mg/kg IV q3 weeks is compared with investigator choice of capecitabine, cetuximab, or docetaxel. Co-primary endpoints are blinded-independent-review ORR and OS; PFS, duration of response, safety, and QOL are secondary.

**Surgical interpretation:** If completed, the active-control design should give a clinically interpretable post-checkpoint comparison for unresectable recurrence.

**Limitations/do not overread:** No participant flow, safety, response, or survival data. Anticipated ADC class activity is not CRB-701 efficacy.

**Source:** [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07768189](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07768189)

## Bottom line for tumor board

This was not a cycle for sweeping surgical change. The most actionable randomized signal is ConCERT's support for weekly cisplatin with lower acute treatment burden, while response-adapted surgery, ctDNA-guided decisions, postoperative field omission, and HPV-negative RT de-escalation remain trial questions. Preserve high-quality baseline staging and planned margins; record granular post-treatment pathology, including ENE extent and residual nodal burden; and route plausible de-escalation candidates to protocols rather than improvising an omission rule. The cSCC data reinforce that imaging can undercall pathologic response after PD-1 therapy, but they do not make surgery dispensable.
