1. Can treatment response safely change the planned operation?

3 findings
Hypothesis-generating

New data strengthen the case for prospectively testing response-adapted surgery after neoadjuvant therapy; they do not establish a safe off-trial resection-omission rule. This extends the prior cycle's perioperative-IO debate.

  • A systematic review/meta-analysis compared response-adapted surgery (RAS) with baseline-planned surgery (BPS) after neoadjuvant immune-checkpoint inhibition.▸

    Key conclusion — Pooled EFS and OS were similar, while RAS reports described more organ preservation and fewer mandibulectomies/free-flap reconstructions.

    Counterpoint — This was an indirect, study-level comparison—not a randomized comparison of surgical strategies.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · restricted full textSystematic review/meta-analysis

    Response-adapted surgery: encouraging, not practice-defining

    Methods/scope: PubMed, Embase, Cochrane, and trial-registry searches covering 2020–2025 identified four RAS studies (202 patients) and 11 BPS studies (403 patients) in resectable HNSCC after neoadjuvant checkpoint inhibition.

    Pooled study-level outcomes
    OutcomeRASBPSComparison
    EFS83.3% (76.9–88.2)82.0% (75.2–87.2)p=.751
    OS92.3% (87.5–95.3)91.4% (80.3–96.5)p=.839
    pCR41.7%19.8% (13.3–29.6)p=.001
    MPR59.6%48.5%p=.245

    Surgical interpretation: the synthesis makes prospective response-guided resection trials reasonable and helps define the outcomes they must protect—margin control, salvageability, function, and EFS.

    Limitations/do not overread: selection, regimen, response-assessment, and follow-up differed across cohorts; the abstract reports a pCR CI that appears internally inconsistent. Do not treat pooled survival similarity as noninferiority or use it to reduce a planned operation outside a protocol.

  • An 86-patient off-trial cohort found deeper pathologic response after neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy than after immunotherapy alone.▸

    Key conclusion — Deep response occurred in 39/58 chemoimmunotherapy patients (67%) versus 1/28 immunotherapy-only patients (3.6%).

    Counterpoint — Treatment selection was nonrandom and pathologic response is not survival.

    Evidence briefStructured abstractSingle-center retrospective cohort

    Large response separation, wide causal uncertainty

    Methods/scope: 86 patients treated July 2024–March 2026 received at least one, typically two, preoperative cycles followed by surgery: 58 received chemotherapy plus immunotherapy and 28 immunotherapy alone.

    Results: overall pCR was 25/86 (29%; 95% CI 21–39) and MPR 15/86 (17%; 95% CI 11–27). Deep response was 67% versus 3.6%; the propensity-adjusted odds ratio was 29.1 (95% CI 6.7–274.7). The abstract reports few adverse-event-related surgical delays but no exact denominator.

    Surgical interpretation: if validated, deeper response could make functional surgery studies feasible; today it supports trial referral and careful preoperative multidisciplinary review, not unilateral scope reduction.

    Limitations/do not overread: single-center retrospective allocation, residual confounding, a very wide CI, and no mature oncologic outcomes. Do not infer that chemoimmunotherapy improves survival or makes surgery optional.

    Read the paper →

  • NCT07751276 will randomize 142 patients to upfront surgery or two cycles of finotonlimab plus platinum/taxane before surgery.▸

    Key conclusion — A newly posted phase II protocol will test whether chemoimmunotherapy before surgery improves 2-year EFS.

    Counterpoint — The study was not yet recruiting and has no results.

    Protocol briefClinicalTrials.gov · first posted Aug 7No results

    Randomized sequencing trial; efficacy unknown

    Protocol: open-label phase II, 142 patients with stage II resectable nonnasopharyngeal HNSCC. The experimental arm gives two 21-day cycles of finotonlimab 200 mg day 1, nab-paclitaxel 100 mg/m² days 1/8/15, and carboplatin AUC 4 or cisplatin 75 mg/m² day 1, then surgery, pathology-directed adjuvant therapy, and six q3-week finotonlimab maintenance cycles. Control is upfront surgery with risk-adapted adjuvant therapy.

    Endpoints/interpretation: primary endpoint is 2-year EFS; MPR, pCR, OS, and safety are secondary. It is relevant as a sequencing test, but the maintenance component means any eventual EFS difference will not isolate the preoperative regimen.

    Limitations/do not overread: no participants, response data, surgical outcomes, or survival results were posted. Do not cite the regimen as effective or available standard care.

Section counterpoint

The field has better response signals, not a validated response-to-resection algorithm. Preserve baseline surgical planning unless a protocol prospectively defines how response changes the operation.

2. HPV ctDNA: strong accuracy, no omission rule

2 findings
Background

HPV ctDNA shows strong reported analytic performance, but this cycle adds no completed trial proving that acting on it improves outcomes. That boundary matters alongside the prior registry signals.

  • A 60-study systematic review mapped HPV ctDNA evidence across 8,234 patients and found median reported sensitivity 91.1% and specificity 99.4%.▸

    Key conclusion — Serial HPV ctDNA clearance is consistently prognostic, but study design and assays remain heterogeneous.

    Counterpoint — Descriptive medians are not a pooled, prospectively validated decision threshold.

    Evidence briefStructured abstractSystematic review

    High accuracy reports, thin decision-impact evidence

    Methods/scope: literature from January 2012–September 2025 yielded 60 studies/8,234 patients; 41 (68.3%) were OPSCC-only, 17 (28.3%) mixed, median follow-up 23 months. Thirty-three were prospective and 19 retrospective; 40 evaluated curative-intent care.

    91.1%Median sensitivity
    99.4%Median specificity
    55/60Used plasma
    4/60Screening/diagnostic studies

    ddPCR was used in 40 studies, qPCR in 16, and NGS in four. OPSCC-only median sensitivity/specificity were 89.4%/99.4%; 38 studies evaluated prognosis.

    Surgical interpretation: ctDNA is credible for risk stratification and surveillance research, but the clinically decisive question is whether a specified assay/time point can safely alter adjuvant therapy, imaging, or salvage.

    Limitations/do not overread: heterogeneous populations, platforms, sampling times, thresholds, and mostly observational associations. Do not use these median accuracies as the performance of any individual assay or as permission to omit treatment.

  • NeckTAR-IN is a prospective blinded ancillary protocol testing whether post-CRT ctDNA predicts incomplete response.▸

    Key conclusion — The study specifies a useful validation design but is not treatment-guiding.

    Counterpoint — Only 12 participants had enrolled by February 2026; no results are reported.

    Full-text protocolOpen access · BMJ OpenNo outcomes

    Blinded prospective accuracy study after chemoradiation

    Protocol: multicenter French prospective ancillary study targeting about 59 node-positive, locally advanced HNSCC patients treated with chemoradiation. Blood is collected at months 1 and 3. A customized NGS panel covers 188 point-mutation targets, six copy-number targets, and high-risk HPV E7.

    Endpoint/interpretation: the primary endpoint is the positive predictive value of month-3 ctDNA for incomplete clinical/radiographic response at month 3; accuracy, kinetics, and survival are secondary. Treating clinicians are blinded, appropriately preventing biomarker-driven intervention during validation.

    Limitations/do not overread: no formal sample-size calculation, small planned cohort, assay complexity, and no treatment assignment. This protocol cannot show that ctDNA-guided neck dissection or surveillance improves outcomes.

3. Neck decisions: thresholds, ENE depth, and neural ALT

3 findings
Hypothesis-generating

This cycle adds preference data, ENE granularity, and a small randomized reconstruction trial—not evidence to expand elective levels or de-intensify adjuvant therapy.

  • An international survey of 2,008 clinicians found 55% would perform elective neck dissection at occult risk ≤10% and 86% by 20%.▸

    Key conclusion — Most respondents preferred individualized risk discussion, but low-risk level selection remained broad.

    Counterpoint — Attitudes are not oncologic outcomes.

    Evidence briefStructured abstractInternational web survey

    Threshold consensus, level-selection discordance

    Methods/results: 2,077 responses were received and 2,008 analyzed. Fifty-five percent of all respondents would offer elective neck dissection at ≤10% occult risk and 86% at 20%; among head-and-neck surgeons, 57% and 92%, respectively. Even with predicted level-specific risk ≤5%, surgeons reported including IIB in 78%, IV in 75%, and V in 69%. Ninety percent preferred individualized risk plus patient discussion over a fixed threshold.

    Surgical interpretation: use level-specific risk estimates to expose practice variation and support shared decisions; the results suggest many elective fields may be broader than their stated threshold logic.

    Limitations/do not overread: voluntary web participation, uncertain representativeness, and hypothetical choices. The survey neither validates a 10%/20% cutoff nor shows that omitting any level is safe.

    Read the paper →

  • In 349 oral-cavity SCC resections, major extranodal extension (>2 mm) carried worse DFS than minor ENE.▸

    Key conclusion — ENE extent may contain prognostic information beyond a binary label.

    Counterpoint — The analysis does not establish different adjuvant treatment by ENE depth.

    Evidence briefStructured abstractSingle-center retrospective cohort

    Major ENE predicts DFS, with substantial uncertainty

    Methods/results: among 349 surgically treated OCSCC patients (2002–2022), 101 were node-positive and 47 had ENE: 16 minor (≤2 mm), 31 major (>2 mm). DFS was worse for major ENE (p=.016), while OS was not significantly different (p=.34). Adjusted major-versus-minor ENE DFS HR was 5.47 (95% CI 1.19–25.08; p=.02).

    Surgical interpretation: recording ENE extent may improve pathology-informed recurrence discussion and future risk-stratified trials.

    Limitations/do not overread: only 47 ENE cases, wide CI, retrospective single-center design, and two decades of treatment evolution. Do not infer that minor ENE can safely avoid standard adjuvant intensification.

    Read the paper →

  • A 40-patient randomized trial found sensory nerve coaptation improved functional outcomes after hemiglossectomy ALT reconstruction.▸

    Key conclusion — Neural ALT flaps improved measured sensibility, speech/swallowing scores, and selected quality-of-life domains.

    Counterpoint — The abstract provides p-values but not functional effect sizes.

    Evidence briefStructured abstractRandomized trial · n=40

    Small randomized reconstruction signal

    Methods/results: 40 hemiglossectomy patients reconstructed with ALT flaps were randomized to no nerve anastomosis (n=19) or neural ALT (n=21) and evaluated at 2, 6, and 12 months. Neural ALT improved two-point discrimination and touch pressure (p<.05), speech and swallowing at every time point, UW-QOL emotional/swallowing domains, and reduced flap atrophy.

    Surgical interpretation: sensory coaptation is a plausible functional refinement when anatomy and operative conditions permit, and deserves discussion during reconstruction planning.

    Limitations/do not overread: small single-center study, uncertain blinding, missing effect magnitudes in the accessible abstract, and limited follow-up. Do not promise a specific recovery or generalize to larger composite defects.

4. Weekly cisplatin clears phase III; RT omission remains investigational

4 findings
Practice-informing

ConCERT supports weekly cisplatin as a less toxic concurrent schedule within its trial context. The other additions are a heterogeneous review and two protocols with no results; none yet establish reduced postoperative volume or response-adapted dose as standard.

  • ConCERT randomized 278 patients and met its prespecified noninferiority margin for weekly versus 3-weekly cisplatin with definitive 70-Gy RT.▸

    Key conclusion — Weekly cisplatin produced noninferior 2-year locoregional control and fewer severe adverse events/hospitalizations.

    Counterpoint — The unexpectedly low control-arm LRC and −10-point margin temper certainty.

    Evidence briefStructured abstractOpen-label multicenter phase III

    Randomized support for weekly cisplatin

    Methods: 278 patients in India with nonnasopharyngeal locally advanced HNSCC were randomized to cisplatin 40 mg/m² weekly or 100 mg/m² every 3 weeks with 70 Gy RT; 272 were analyzed. Median follow-up was 43.9 months. Noninferiority used a −10-percentage-point margin.

    Key trial outcomes
    OutcomeWeeklyEvery 3 weeks
    2-y LRC61.3%51.1%
    Grade ≥3 AEs44.7%60.7%
    Hospitalization20.3%36.8%
    Median OS24.9 mo22.2 mo

    LRC absolute difference was 10.2 points; one-sided 95% lower bound 0.3. HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.50–1.05; p=.091). Median PFS was 20.3 versus 17.9 months (p=.204) and OS comparison p=.501. Toxicity p=.018 and hospitalization p=.004.

    Surgical interpretation: when postoperative or definitive concurrent cisplatin scheduling is debated, this adds level-I schedule evidence and a toxicity tradeoff relevant to recovery and completion.

    Limitations/do not overread: open label; the abstract lacks HPV/subsite, adherence, and cumulative-dose detail. Do not infer superiority or generalize beyond comparable patients and RT delivery.

  • A systematic review found prospective HPV-negative RT de-escalation evidence small, heterogeneous, and conflicting.▸

    Key conclusion — Elective-volume failures were uncommon in reported studies, but randomized experience did not consistently preserve outcomes.

    Counterpoint — “De-escalation” combined unlike dose, volume, and treatment approaches.

    Evidence briefStructured abstractSystematic review

    No mature HPV-negative de-escalation standard

    Methods/results: PubMed and Embase screening of 3,156 records found 14 published prospective studies (10 definitive, four postoperative) and 23 ongoing studies. Elective nodal failure ranged 0–4.6%, with most recurrences in high-dose regions. Two randomized trials preserved control while reducing acute toxicity; one stopped for futility.

    Surgical interpretation: postoperative reduction should remain protocol-defined, with careful documentation of margin, ENE, nodal burden, subsite, and recurrence geography.

    Limitations/do not overread: small studies, mixed or incompletely specified HPV status, disparate interventions, and conflicting randomized results. A low elective-field failure rate alone does not prove equal survival or functional benefit.

    Read the paper →

  • CLEAR will omit low-risk postoperative cervical levels after systematic neck dissection in 101 selected patients.▸

    Key conclusion — The phase II study directly tests volume reduction using surgical pathology.

    Counterpoint — Recruiting registry; no results.

    Protocol briefClinicalTrials.gov · first posted Aug 10No results

    Pathology-selected postoperative field omission

    Protocol: single-arm Spanish phase II, planned n=101. Eligibility requires ≥18 nodes per dissected neck and pN0 or one pN1 node <1 cm without ENE/skip metastasis, in pT1–2N1 or pT3–4N0/N1 disease. IMRT/VMAT treats the primary bed to 60 Gy/30 fractions or 66 Gy/33; low-risk dissected cervical levels are omitted. Cisplatin is permitted for positive or close (<5 mm) margins.

    Endpoint/interpretation: primary outcome is 24-month recurrence in unirradiated levels versus a historical 6–8% rate; toxicity, QOL, and lymphopenia are secondary. The trial is directly relevant to postoperative volume selection.

    Limitations/do not overread: single arm, historical comparator, stringent pathology quality, and no posted outcomes. Do not omit levels based on this registry outside the study.

  • IDEAL-RT will randomize deep responders with HPV/p16-negative disease to 25-fraction de-escalated or standard 33-fraction RT after induction chemoimmunotherapy.▸

    Key conclusion — The protocol selects by ≥50% RECIST response rather than HPV status alone.

    Counterpoint — Registry status is active, not recruiting; no results.

    Protocol briefClinicalTrials.gov · first posted Aug 28No results

    Response-selected, randomized dose reduction

    Protocol: open-label multicenter phase II noninferiority study, planned n=180, in HPV/p16-negative nonnasopharyngeal locally advanced HNSCC with ≥50% RECIST response after 2–3 cycles platinum plus PD-1 therapy. De-escalated RT is GTV 60 Gy, CTV1 50 Gy, CTV2 45 Gy in 25 fractions; control is 69.96/60.06/50.96 Gy in 33/33/28 fractions. Both groups then receive camrelizumab 200 mg q3 weeks ×8.

    Endpoints/interpretation: primary endpoint 2-year PFS; OS, LRC, DMFS, toxicity, QOL, and MDADI are secondary. This is a meaningful biomarker/response-selection design.

    Limitations/do not overread: newly posted but active-not-recruiting, with no efficacy or safety results. Induction response may enrich prognosis without proving treatment-effect modification.

5. After neoadjuvant PD-1 in cSCC, pathology outruns imaging

3 findings
Hypothesis-generating

Post-treatment pathology may stratify risk better than imaging, but the available cohorts remain retrospective and do not validate surgery omission or a gene-expression-directed adjuvant plan.

  • A residual nodal-disease model separated 3-year DFS after neoadjuvant immunotherapy for resectable head-and-neck cSCC.▸

    Key conclusion — Residual positive-node number, ENE, and viable tumor percentage identified a very-high-risk group.

    Counterpoint — This is an unvalidated single-center derivation model.

    Evidence briefStructured abstractRetrospective cohort · n=82

    Pathologic residual burden after neoadjuvant IO

    Methods/results: 82 patients received ≥2 neoadjuvant immunotherapy doses then surgery; median follow-up 31.6 months, 24 DFS events, 14 deaths. Nodal pCR occurred in 27 (32.9%). The iN model assigned low/intermediate/high risk to 39/22/21 patients; 3-year DFS was 91.2%/70.8%/25.9% (p<.001). High versus low risk DFS HR was 13.42 (95% CI 3.85–46.77); OS HR 20.86 (2.65–164.34).

    Surgical interpretation: detailed post-IO nodal pathology may identify patients needing trials of intensified surveillance/adjuvant therapy and should be captured beyond ypN alone.

    Limitations/do not overread: very few events, wide CIs, possible overfitting, no external validation, and no randomized treatment comparison. Do not use iN to select therapy yet.

    Read the paper →

  • In 18 head-and-neck cSCC patients given neoadjuvant cemiplimab, pCR/MPR reached 72% while imaging showed no complete responses.▸

    Key conclusion — Standard imaging may markedly underestimate pathologic clearance after PD-1 therapy.

    Counterpoint — Every patient still underwent surgery; omission was not tested.

    Evidence briefStructured abstractRetrospective cohort · n=18

    Radiology-pathology discordance in a very small series

    Methods/results: 18 patients treated 2019–2024 received neoadjuvant cemiplimab followed by curative-intent surgery. Eleven (61%) achieved pCR and two (11%) MPR. Among 16 evaluable by imaging, 33% had partial response and none complete response.

    Surgical interpretation: apparent residual imaging abnormality after PD-1 therapy may not equal viable tumor; pathology remains critical and response-adapted surgery should use validated multimodal criteria.

    Limitations/do not overread: selected, retrospective, single-institution cohort with no survival analysis. Do not infer that surgery can be omitted or imaging safely ignored.

    Read the paper →

  • A 572-patient retrospective validation combined a 40-gene expression profile with clinical factors to stratify high-risk cSCC.▸

    Key conclusion — The integrated model separated metastasis and local-recurrence risk.

    Counterpoint — Prognostic separation does not prove predictive benefit from adjuvant RT.

    Evidence briefStructured abstractRetrospective validation · industry involvement

    Integrated risk model, not a treatment-selection trial

    Methods/results: 572 high-risk cSCC cases were classified using 40-GEP plus immunosuppression, location, differentiation, diameter, and perineural invasion into 1A/1B/2A/2B. Classes stratified metastasis and local recurrence (p<.001). Class 1A maintained >95% NPV across NCCN subsets; class 2B comprised 12.9% and had 3-year MFS 66.2%. The abstract states an association with adjuvant-RT benefit but provides no effect magnitude.

    Surgical interpretation: the tool may complement—not replace—staging and pathology in discussing surveillance or trials.

    Limitations/do not overread: retrospective analysis, no randomized RT allocation, no head-and-neck-only breakdown, and company-employed authors. Do not claim the test predicts who benefits from RT on this evidence.

6. Negative STAT3 trial; a post-checkpoint ADC enters phase III

2 findings
Pipeline

STAT3 antisense did not improve response with pembrolizumab; a newly posted randomized phase III ADC study has no outcomes yet.

  • Randomized phase II danvatirsen plus pembrolizumab produced no meaningful ORR improvement over pembrolizumab alone.▸

    Key conclusion — Enrollment stopped early for lack of response signal.

    Counterpoint — The small study was not designed to settle survival.

    Evidence briefStructured abstractRandomized open-label phase II

    Negative response result for STAT3 antisense

    Methods/results: first-line PD-L1 CPS≥1 recurrent/metastatic HNSCC was randomized 2:1 to danvatirsen 3 mg/kg weekly plus pembrolizumab 200 mg q3 weeks or pembrolizumab alone. Of 69 randomized, 66 were treated (45 versus 21). ORR was 15.6% versus 14.3% (risk difference 1.3 points; p=1.0). Treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 97.8% versus 85.7%. Enrollment stopped after lack of response in CPS 1–19 after ≥1 scan/~6 weeks and CPS≥20 after ≥2 scans/~12 weeks.

    Surgical interpretation: for unresectable recurrence counseling, this is a useful negative trial: adding danvatirsen did not rescue pembrolizumab response.

    Limitations/do not overread: small sample, ORR-focused early stopping, and no mature survival inference. The result does not invalidate other STAT3 strategies or combinations.

    Read the paper →

  • NCT07768189 will compare the targeted ADC CRB-701 with investigator-choice single-agent therapy after platinum and PD-(L)1 treatment.▸

    Key conclusion — A 260-patient phase III program is entering the post-platinum/post-checkpoint space.

    Counterpoint — Not recruiting; no results.

    Protocol briefClinicalTrials.gov · first posted Aug 17No results

    Randomized ADC phase III; efficacy unknown

    Protocol: planned n=260 with recurrent/metastatic oropharyngeal SCC after platinum and PD-(L)1 therapy. CRB-701 3.6 mg/kg IV q3 weeks is compared with investigator choice of capecitabine, cetuximab, or docetaxel.

    Endpoints/interpretation: co-primary outcomes are blinded-independent-review ORR and OS; PFS, duration of response, safety, and quality of life are secondary. The active-control design should produce a clinically interpretable post-checkpoint comparison if completed.

    Limitations/do not overread: the study is not recruiting and no participant flow, safety, response, or survival data exist. Do not cite anticipated class activity as CRB-701 efficacy.

Bottom line for tumor board

This was not a cycle for sweeping surgical change. The most actionable randomized signal is ConCERT's support for weekly cisplatin with lower acute treatment burden, while response-adapted surgery, ctDNA-guided decisions, postoperative field omission, and HPV-negative RT de-escalation remain trial questions. For the surgeon, the practical move is to preserve high-quality baseline staging and planned margins, record granular post-treatment pathology—including ENE extent and residual nodal burden—and route plausible de-escalation candidates to protocols rather than improvising an omission rule. The cSCC data reinforce that imaging can undercall pathologic response after PD-1 therapy, but they do not make surgery dispensable.
