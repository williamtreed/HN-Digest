**For the surgeon · Literature window: September 29–October 12, 2026**

**12 new DOI/NCT sources · 1 material registry update · 0 unchanged repeats.** This is a strict delta from the September 28 cycle: 11 newly published papers, one newly posted randomized radiotherapy protocol, and one previously covered ctHPV-DNA–guided trial whose registry now shows withdrawal before enrollment.

According to PubMed, paper identifiers, publication dates, and numeric claims were checked against indexed abstracts; registry protocols and statuses were checked against ClinicalTrials.gov on October 12, 2026. Each finding includes provenance, methods or protocol, exact results where available, surgical interpretation, limitations, and a do-not-overread boundary. Registry-only entries are labeled and contain no efficacy claims.

## Highlights — key takeaways

- **The ctDNA signal is trajectory, not a single early positive.** In 332 HPV-associated OPSCC patients, 6/7 with persistent early TTMV-HPV DNA positivity recurred, while none of six whose positive result cleared recurred; this is retrospective and does not validate treatment escalation.
- **Neoadjuvant response remains clinically provocative but not operation-sparing evidence.** A 39-patient oral-cancer cohort reported 50% pCR among the 20 who underwent surgery, while a 21-patient anlotinib/TP trial sent only eight patients to surgery.
- **Baseline swallowing deserves preoperative attention.** In an E3311 subset, baseline DIGEST ≥1 was associated with post-TOS DIGEST ≥2 (adjusted OR 4.69), supporting targeted prehabilitation rather than excluding surgery.
- **A post-treatment PET-negative neck remains reassuring, not infallible.** In a low-HPV cN+ cohort, FDG-PET/CT had 89.2% NPV but only 40.0% sensitivity after RT-based treatment.
- **COMPASS-SCC is a credible staging challenger.** The 20,972-tumor system weights diameter, depth, differentiation, large-caliber PNI, and LVI and outperformed AJCC8 and BWH in the retrospective development dataset; external implementation work is still needed.
- **Setanaxib missed its randomized primary endpoint.** Exploratory PFS and OS hazard ratios favored the combination using 80% confidence intervals and a permissive α threshold, so the trial is a research signal—not a treatment-changing result.

**Quiet watchlist:** No new KEYNOTE-689, NIVOPOST, perioperative/adjuvant cSCC randomized result, validated surgical-omission rule, or prospective ctDNA-triggered intervention readout appeared in the interval. ReACT 2.0 was withdrawn before its first participant, narrowing rather than expanding the ctDNA-guided interventional landscape.

## 1. Perioperative therapy and residual pathology: strong signals, weak permission to de-escalate

**Bottom line:** Two small neoadjuvant studies show activity, and two retrospective pathology models refine risk after surgery. None establishes that a planned operation, neck dissection, or adjuvant treatment can safely be reduced outside a trial.

### Neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy in locally advanced oral SCC

**Provenance/access:** PubMed abstract; two-center retrospective real-world cohort.

**Methods/results:** Thirty-nine consecutive patients received a PD-1 inhibitor plus paclitaxel and cisplatin; 35 were radiographically evaluable and 20 proceeded to surgery. Nine complete and 14 partial responses produced an ORR of 65.7% (95% CI 45.8–79.4). Ten of 20 surgical patients achieved pCR (50.0%; 95% CI 29.9–70.1). At 16 months' median follow-up, six DFS events and no deaths had occurred; estimated 2-year DFS was 84.4% (95% CI 60.9–89.3).

**Surgical interpretation:** Record the pre-treatment operation and scrutinize why nearly half the cohort did not proceed to surgery; response should inform trial design, not an improvised omission rule.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Retrospective cohort, 39 patients, only 20 operations, heterogeneous PD-1 inhibitors, short follow-up, and low event count. The exploratory comparison between surgical and nonsurgical management cannot establish equivalence.

**Source:** [Journal of Cranio-Maxillo-Facial Surgery; DOI 10.1016/j.jcms.2026.109906](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcms.2026.109906)

### Neoadjuvant anlotinib plus paclitaxel-cisplatin in technically resectable HNSCC

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; prospective single-arm phase II study; ChiCTR2100046440.

**Methods/results:** Twenty-one patients received two 21-day cycles. ORR was 66.7% (14/21; 95% CI 43.0–85.4), meeting the prespecified activity criterion. Eight underwent surgery, all achieved R0 resection, 5/8 had major pathologic response, and 4/8 underwent a less extensive resection than initially planned. Reported adverse events were mainly grade 1; one grade 2 primary-tumor bleed occurred, and proteinuria was not systematically captured.

**Surgical interpretation:** The regimen may create resection-planning opportunities, but the denominator that reached surgery is the critical number for consent and trial design.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Twenty-one patients, no control arm, only eight operations, exploratory surgical endpoints, and incomplete toxicity capture. The study does not establish anlotinib benefit beyond chemotherapy or prove functional preservation.

**Source:** [Head & Neck; DOI 10.1002/hed.70501](https://doi.org/10.1002/hed.70501)

### Composite residual viable tumor score after neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy

**Provenance/access:** PubMed abstract; single-center retrospective derivation study.

**Methods/results:** Among 353 stage III–IV oral-SCC patients treated with at least two neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy cycles followed by surgery, the score used the higher residual viable tumor percentage in the primary bed or any involved node. Compared with ≤10% RVT, 11–40% and >40% RVT were associated with worse EFS (adjusted HR 4.87, 95% CI 3.12–7.60; and 9.25, 5.41–15.80) and OS (adjusted HR 5.12, 3.07–8.54; and 9.87, 5.38–18.11). Optimism-corrected C-index was 0.76 versus 0.70 for ypTNM; NRI was 0.42 (95% CI 0.29–0.55).

**Surgical interpretation:** Standardized primary-and-nodal viable-tumor reporting may be more informative than ypTNM alone when postoperative trials stratify risk.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Retrospective single-center derivation, data-driven cut points, internal bootstrap correction only, and no evidence that score-directed adjuvant therapy improves outcome. External validation is required.

**Source:** [Journal of Cranio-Maxillo-Facial Surgery; DOI 10.1016/j.jcms.2026.109919](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcms.2026.109919)

### Cumulative pathology risk within pENE-positive HNSCC

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; retrospective cohort treated with surgery and adjuvant chemoradiotherapy.

**Methods/results:** Investigators studied 281 pENE-positive patients treated from 2008–2016. Positive margins, poor differentiation, and more than three metastatic nodes were independently associated with worse OS and DFS. Counting these “P” factors as 0, 1, or 2 yielded 5-year OS of 55.0%, 37.5%, and 6.7% and 5-year DFS of 50.5%, 34.5%, and 0%, respectively (both p<.001); only 15 patients had two factors.

**Surgical interpretation:** ENE is not a uniform risk state; margin status, differentiation, and nodal burden can sharpen postoperative trial stratification and counseling.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Retrospective single-era cohort, treatment from 2008–2016, no external validation, and a 15-patient highest-risk group. The score does not define an evidence-based intensification regimen.

**Source:** [Journal of the Formosan Medical Association; DOI 10.1016/j.jfma.2026.10.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfma.2026.10.008)

**Section counterpoint:** Radiographic and pathologic response are prognostic signals, not automatic permission to shrink surgery. Attrition before operation and unmeasured response-based selection are central uncertainties.

**Go deeper:** The composite RVT study has the largest surgical cohort and the clearest pathology framework; the 39-patient chemoimmunotherapy cohort is the most important caution about denominator selection.

## 2. ctDNA/MRD: persistence matters, while an intervention trial disappears

**Bottom line:** Early post-treatment TTMV-HPV DNA trajectories strongly separated recurrence risk in a retrospective cohort, but ReACT 2.0 was withdrawn before enrollment. The biomarker remains useful for risk discussion, not a stand-alone trigger for adjuvant treatment.

### Early post-treatment TTMV-HPV DNA in HPV-associated OPSCC

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; retrospective eight-site cohort.

**Methods/results:** Among 332 patients tested 1–89 days after definitive treatment, the first result was negative in 310 (93%), indeterminate in three (1%), and positive in 19 (6%). Of 59 retested after an initial negative, two converted to positive and both recurred. Of 13 initially positive patients with adequate trajectory follow-up, six cleared and none recurred; seven remained positive and six recurred. The last early result was associated with subsequent event-free survival in a 90-day landmark analysis.

**Surgical interpretation:** Repeat an unexpectedly positive early result before treating it as molecular persistence; the clearance-versus-persistence pattern is more informative than a single draw.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Retrospective testing and follow-up, small positive subgroups, selected retesting, no randomized intervention, and commercial-assay context. The study does not establish when to biopsy, operate, irradiate, or add systemic therapy.

**Source:** [Head & Neck; DOI 10.1002/hed.70483](https://doi.org/10.1002/hed.70483)

### ReACT 2.0 withdrawn before enrollment

**Provenance/access:** ClinicalTrials.gov registry update posted October 8; **no results**.

**Protocol/status:** NCT07513324 had planned a randomized phase II comparison of ctHPV-DNA–risk-adapted adjuvant pembrolizumab versus observation in HPV-positive OPSCC. The registry now lists the study as withdrawn, with 0 actual participants and the reason “study halted prematurely prior to enrollment of first participant.”

**Surgical interpretation:** Remove this trial from referral options and avoid implying that its biomarker-guided adjuvant strategy generated evidence.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Withdrawal supplies no efficacy, safety, feasibility, or biomarker-validity result and does not invalidate other ctDNA trials.

**Source:** [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07513324](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07513324)

**Section counterpoint:** Strong prognostic enrichment is not the same as demonstrated clinical utility. The missing link remains proof that acting on an early ctDNA result improves outcomes without avoidable treatment.

**Go deeper:** Read the TTMV-HPV DNA paper for trajectory denominators—especially the 13 initially positive patients—rather than extrapolating from the overall cohort of 332.

## 3. Neck and function: use imaging to stratify, not to declare certainty

**Bottom line:** Baseline swallow impairment predicted early post-TOS dysphagia, response-assessment PET was best at ruling out rather than finding persistent neck disease, and imaging-detected nodal count carried prognostic information in a small HPV-positive cohort.

### Baseline modified barium swallow before TOS

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; exploratory secondary analysis of ECOG-ACRIN E3311.

**Methods/results:** Eighty-nine patients had centrally reviewed baseline and post-TOS modified barium swallow studies before adjuvant therapy. Baseline DIGEST was 0 in 80%, 1 in 17%, and ≥2 in 3%. After surgery, 51% worsened by at least one grade and 16% had DIGEST ≥2. Baseline DIGEST ≥1 was independently associated with post-TOS DIGEST ≥2 (adjusted OR 4.69; 95% CI 1.32–16.64), with a 23.8% absolute risk difference (95% CI 2.6–48.8%) and optimism-corrected AUC 0.75 (95% CI 0.59–0.88).

**Surgical interpretation:** Mild preoperative impairment should trigger targeted swallowing prehabilitation, nutrition planning, and early postoperative reassessment—not automatic exclusion from TOS.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Selected 89-patient subset, exploratory retrospective analysis, rare baseline high-grade impairment, wide intervals, and only acute pre-adjuvant outcomes. Predictive value for long-term function is unknown.

**Source:** [Oral Oncology; DOI 10.1016/j.oraloncology.2026.108162](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oraloncology.2026.108162)

### FDG-PET/CT for persistent nodal disease after RT-based treatment

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; retrospective cN+ cohort with low HPV prevalence.

**Methods/results:** Among 125 patients with complete primary-site response and PET/CT 2–4 months after RT, CRT, or bio-RT, 10 scans were initially positive, 99 negative, and 16 indeterminate; review reclassified 12 indeterminate scans as positive and four as negative. Sensitivity was 40.0%, specificity 94.3%, PPV 57.1%, and NPV 89.2%. Oropharyngeal NPV was 95.8% in HPV-positive and 85.7% in HPV-negative tumors.

**Surgical interpretation:** A negative response-assessment PET can support neck-dissection avoidance within a complete clinical pathway, while positive or indeterminate findings need confirmation rather than reflex surgery.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Retrospective cohort, treatment and HPV heterogeneity, low sensitivity, local reclassification process, and no randomized neck-management comparison. The 89.2% NPV still leaves residual risk.

**Source:** [Acta Otorrinolaringológica Española; DOI 10.1016/j.otoeng.2026.512388](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.otoeng.2026.512388)

### Imaging-detected nodal count in HPV-associated OPSCC

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; retrospective consecutive cohort using FDG-PET/CT and Node-RADS.

**Methods/results:** Sixty-eight patients treated from 2014–2024 were evaluated by two readers; an imaging-positive node had SUVmax ≥3.5 or Node-RADS ≥3. Five-year OS and EFS were 80% and 77%. At least five imaging-detected nodal metastases were associated with worse OS (HR 11.4; 95% CI 3.1–44.5) and EFS (HR 6.5; 2.0–20.1); estimates remained large after adjustment for clinical stage III.

**Surgical interpretation:** Explicitly counting imaging-positive nodes may improve pre-treatment risk discussion and multidisciplinary planning when pathologic nodal burden is unavailable.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Sixty-eight patients, retrospective imaging thresholds, few events, very wide confidence intervals, and no external validation. Imaging count cannot substitute for pathologic staging after neck dissection.

**Source:** [European Radiology; DOI 10.1007/s00330-026-12916-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-026-12916-7)

**Section counterpoint:** These tools refine probabilities. None converts imaging or a baseline swallow study into a definitive operative eligibility, neck-dissection, or adjuvant-treatment rule.

**Go deeper:** The E3311 secondary analysis is the most immediately actionable for prehabilitation; the PET persistence study is the clearest reminder to separate rule-out value from sensitivity.

## 4. Radiation pathways: a distant-relapse model and a new older-adult trial

**Bottom line:** A small postoperative cohort proposes four features for distant-relapse enrichment, while a newly posted 60-patient trial will compare three-week hypofractionation with conventional treatment in adults 65 and older. Neither changes current treatment standards.

### Distant-metastasis risk after postoperative (chemo)radiotherapy

**Provenance/access:** PubMed abstract; retrospective 78-patient postoperative cohort.

**Methods/results:** Four prespecified factors—hypopharyngeal primary, contralateral nodal metastasis, level IV-or-lower nodal disease, and ENE—defined low-, intermediate-, and high-risk groups. At 50 months' median follow-up, 39 patients had recurred and distant metastasis was the most common first failure. Twelve-month DMFS was 85.8%, 79.3%, and 33.3% for 0, 1, and ≥2 factors. High-risk status was associated with worse DMFS after T-category adjustment (HR 5.43; 95% CI 2.24–13.15).

**Surgical interpretation:** The four features can identify a cohort for prospective postoperative intensification or surveillance studies, not a ready-made treatment algorithm.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Seventy-eight patients, single retrospective cohort, many events relative to model size, broad confidence intervals, and no external validation. Do not intensify therapy solely from this score.

**Source:** [Journal of Radiation Research; DOI 10.1093/jrr/rrag087](https://doi.org/10.1093/jrr/rrag087)

### Three-week radiotherapy trial for older adults

**Provenance/access:** ClinicalTrials.gov protocol first posted September 29; not yet recruiting; **no results**.

**Protocol:** NCT07847060 plans to randomize 60 adults aged 65 or older with nonmetastatic HNSCC to image-guided hypofractionated RT or standard fractionation. The experimental schedule is 45 Gy in 15 fractions for definitive treatment or 40.5 Gy in 15 fractions postoperatively; the comparator is conventional 1.8–2.0 Gy fractions over 6–7 weeks. One-year locoregional control is the primary endpoint; estimated primary completion is October 2027.

**Surgical interpretation:** This is a relevant access-and-tolerance question for older postoperative patients, but the registry currently lists no study locations and has not begun recruitment.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Small open-label single-sponsor protocol, mixed definitive and postoperative settings, no posted locations, and no outcomes. Registration is not evidence of noninferiority or safety.

**Source:** [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07847060](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07847060)

**Section counterpoint:** One study derives risk and the other proposes a solution in a different population. Neither demonstrates that systemic intensification or abbreviated RT improves survival or function.

**Go deeper:** The postoperative model is useful for trial enrichment; the new registry matters mainly as a future option once recruitment and sites are confirmed.

## 5. Cutaneous SCC: COMPASS proposes a more discriminating staging system

**Bottom line:** COMPASS-SCC uses five familiar pathologic features and outperformed AJCC8 and BWH in a very large multicenter retrospective dataset. It is promising for risk communication but is not yet a replacement staging standard.

### COMPASS-SCC development and internal performance

**Provenance/access:** PubMed structured abstract; retrospective database from 12 international centers.

**Methods/results:** The model included 20,972 primary cSCC tumors and defined the endpoint as metastasis or disease-specific death. Points were assigned for diameter 2–<5 cm (1) or ≥5 cm (2), invasion beyond fat or depth >6 mm (1), moderate (1) or poor differentiation (2), large-caliber PNI (1), and LVI (1). Total scores map from T1 (0) to T4 (≥5). Sensitivity, NPV, AUROC, and Uno's c-index were significantly higher than AJCC8 and BWH; the abstract does not report the absolute metric values.

**Surgical interpretation:** The point system could standardize multidisciplinary risk language and selection for nodal staging, surveillance, or adjuvant trials after independent validation.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Retrospective development dataset, no absolute comparative performance values in the abstract, and no prospective evidence that COMPASS-directed management improves outcomes. Do not replace current guideline staging from this report alone.

**Source:** [Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology; DOI 10.1016/j.jaad.2026.09.104](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaad.2026.09.104)

**Section counterpoint:** Better retrospective discrimination does not automatically improve decisions. Calibration, external validation, treatment interactions, and head-and-neck-specific implementation still matter.

**Go deeper:** The value is the transparent point assignment and 20,972-tumor scale; the missing absolute performance estimates should temper claims of superiority.

## 6. Systemic therapy: setanaxib misses the tumor-shrinkage endpoint

**Bottom line:** Adding setanaxib to pembrolizumab did not improve the randomized primary endpoint in CAF-rich recurrent/metastatic HNSCC. Exploratory survival and immune signals justify research, not clinical adoption.

### Setanaxib plus pembrolizumab versus placebo plus pembrolizumab

**Provenance/access:** PubMed abstract; randomized double-blind placebo-controlled phase II trial, NCT05323656.

**Methods/results:** Fifty-five patients with CAF levels ≥5% were treated: 27 with setanaxib 800 mg twice daily plus pembrolizumab and 28 with placebo plus pembrolizumab. Least-squares mean best tumor-size change was −7.9% versus −12.9%; difference 5.1 percentage points (80% CI −11.9 to 22.0; p=.7008). Exploratory PFS HR was 0.58 (80% CI 0.38–0.89; p=.1023) and OS HR 0.45 (0.24–0.85; p=.1057), evaluated against a prespecified p<.20 threshold. CD8+ TILs increased with setanaxib (p=.037). Thirty-three serious adverse events occurred (16 versus 17); four deaths occurred, all in the placebo arm.

**Surgical interpretation:** Do not use the exploratory survival estimates to justify off-trial treatment or conversion surgery; the primary tumor-shrinkage endpoint favored neither setanaxib nor the hypothesized mechanism.

**Limitations/do not overread:** Fifty-five patients, 80% confidence intervals, permissive α, multiple secondary and biomarker analyses, and a negative primary endpoint. The apparent survival hazard ratios are hypothesis-generating.

**Source:** [Cancer Research Communications; DOI 10.1158/2767-9764.CRC-26-0422](https://doi.org/10.1158/2767-9764.CRC-26-0422)

**Section counterpoint:** Randomization strengthens the comparison, but the small trial and negative primary endpoint outweigh favorable exploratory hazard ratios.

**Go deeper:** Focus on the endpoint hierarchy and 80% confidence intervals before interpreting the PFS, OS, CD8, or death imbalance.

## Bottom line for tumor board

This cycle sharpens boundaries more than it changes standards. For neoadjuvant therapy, keep the original surgical plan visible and treat response-based reduction as a trial question; if pathology follows immunochemotherapy, report viable tumor across both primary and nodal sites. In HPV-positive OPSCC surveillance, confirm whether an early positive TTMV-HPV DNA result persists before escalation, while recognizing that no randomized intervention result exists and ReACT 2.0 will not supply one. Use baseline swallow imaging to target prehabilitation, and interpret response-assessment PET by its rule-out performance rather than its sensitivity. COMPASS-SCC deserves validation, not immediate replacement of AJCC8/BWH. The new older-adult RT trial has no results, and setanaxib's exploratory survival signals do not reverse its negative primary endpoint.
