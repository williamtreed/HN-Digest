# Head & Neck Oncology Digest — For the Surgeon

Window covered: ~last 12 months (through August 2026)

Sources: JCO, NEJM, Lancet, Lancet Oncology, JAMA Oncology, JAMA Otolaryngology, Laryngoscope, Head & Neck, Oral Oncology, IJROBP, Clin Cancer Res, Ann Surg Oncol, Annals of Oncology, and ClinicalTrials.gov.

According to PubMed, all literature findings below are drawn from indexed sources; DOI links are provided per PubMed's attribution requirements. Trial protocols are linked to their live ClinicalTrials.gov records.

This is the portable edition of the baseline cycle. Every item includes an article-specific digest covering scope, methods or protocol, exact results where available, interpretation, limitations, and source provenance. Registry items are explicitly labeled as protocols with no results.

## Highlights — key takeaways

- Perioperative pembrolizumab improved event-free survival in KEYNOTE-689, with the clearest absolute separation at CPS ≥10; the trial tested a whole pre- and postoperative program, not neoadjuvant dosing alone.
- NIVOPOST-OP improved disease-free survival after surgery in a pathologically high-risk population without introducing a presurgical treatment interval.
- ctDNA/MRD is moving from prognostic validation into trials that use a blood result to assign observation, systemic treatment, or radiation duration—but most utility questions remain unanswered.
- Selective omission of level IIB and response-adapted omission of neck dissection are testable hypotheses, not established standards.
- Lower-dose RT showed better swallowing outcomes in a small cross-trial comparison; cetuximab failed to improve quality of life over cisplatin in RTOG 1016.
- Petosemtamab has entered phase III development, while ficerafusp alfa remains an early, small-cohort signal.

## 1. Who gets a medical oncology consult before you book the OR

**[Practice-changing, with caveats] Bottom line:** For CPS ≥1 resectable stage III–IV disease, perioperative pembrolizumab has randomized EFS evidence, but benefit magnitude, added toxicity, and the inability to isolate the neoadjuvant contribution make selection and preoperative coordination central.

### KEYNOTE-689

**Key conclusion:** Adding neoadjuvant and adjuvant pembrolizumab to surgery-based curative therapy improved EFS across the trial hierarchy, with the clearest absolute separation at CPS ≥10. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2415434) · [NCT03765918](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03765918)

**Scope and methods:** This open-label phase III trial randomized 714 patients with resectable, locally advanced HNSCC. The experimental pathway was two preoperative pembrolizumab cycles → surgery → postoperative pembrolizumab with risk-adapted RT ± cisplatin → pembrolizumab maintenance. The control pathway was surgery → standard postoperative RT ± cisplatin. The primary endpoint was EFS in a prespecified CPS hierarchy. Median follow-up was 38.3 months.

| Population | 36-month EFS with pembrolizumab | Control | Hazard ratio (95% CI) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| CPS ≥10 | 59.8% | 45.9% | 0.66 (0.49–0.88) |
| CPS ≥1 | 58.2% | 44.9% | 0.70 (0.55–0.89) |
| All patients | 57.6% | 46.4% | 0.73 (0.58–0.92) |

Surgery was completed in about 88% of both groups. Overall major pathologic response was 9.4% with pembrolizumab and 0% in control. Grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events occurred in 44.6% vs 42.9%.

**Interpretation:** The trial supports consulting medical oncology before committing to the operative calendar because systemic therapy begins before surgery and continues through postoperative treatment. CPS informs expected benefit, but response did not license a smaller operation.

**Limitations:** Overall survival was immature; the regimen-level design cannot identify whether benefit came from the preoperative doses, postoperative therapy, or both; major pathologic response was modest; and Merck funded the study.

### NIVOPOST-OP / GORTEC 2018-01

**Key conclusion:** Postoperative nivolumab plus chemoradiotherapy improved DFS after complete resection in a defined high-risk pathology population. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(25)01850-1) · [NCT03576417](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03576417)

**Scope and methods:** This open-label phase III trial enrolled 680 patients across 82 sites in six countries; 666 entered the efficacy analysis. Eligibility required complete macroscopic resection plus extranodal extension, a positive margin, at least four involved cervical nodes without extranodal extension, or multiple foci of perineural invasion.

**Protocol flow:** complete resection → confirm high-risk pathology → either 66 Gy RT plus cisplatin 100 mg/m² every three weeks for three cycles, or nivolumab lead-in followed by nivolumab with chemoradiotherapy and six maintenance doses.

At 30.3 months' median follow-up, 3-year DFS was 63.1% with nivolumab versus 52.5% with control (HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.60–0.98), reported irrespective of PD-L1. Grade 4 treatment-related events occurred in 10% vs 5%; two treatment-related deaths occurred in each group.

**Interpretation:** This is the easier surgical pathway to operationalize because high-risk pathology, not a preoperative biomarker, triggers the systemic discussion. It does not delay the initial operation.

**Limitations:** The study was open-label, overall survival remains immature, toxicity increased, and the result does not apply to presurgical selection or lower-risk postoperative disease.

### KEYNOTE-689 presurgical progression signal

**Key conclusion:** Forty-five clinical progressions were recorded by surgery in the pembrolizumab group versus nine in control, while roughly 88% in each group still completed surgery. The operation therefore needs a fixed date and an explicit escalation pathway during neoadjuvant treatment. [Trial paper and supplement](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2415434)

| Implementation measure | Perioperative pembrolizumab | Surgery-first control | Boundary |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Clinical progression by surgery | 45 | 9 | Timing-window event; not synonymous with cancelled surgery |
| Completed surgery | ~88% | ~88% | Similar aggregate completion despite the imbalance |

**Interpretation:** Clinical progression before or at surgery and failure to undergo surgery are distinct endpoints. The protocol preserved the planned surgical footprint rather than allowing response to shrink the operation.

**Limitations:** This is an exploratory supplemental signal from the same randomized population, not an independent study. Without patient-level timing, reasons, adjudication, and surgical-morbidity detail, it does not prove pembrolizumab caused progression or prevented surgery.

### Ben Kridis and Khanfir meta-analysis

**Key conclusion:** Pooling KEYNOTE-689 and NIVOPOST-OP favored checkpoint inhibition, but the synthesis is most useful for placing benefit and treatment burden together. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/jop.70153)

**Scope and methods:** The meta-analysis contained only two phase III trials with 1,394 high-risk patients. One program was perioperative and one postoperative.

| Outcome | Estimate | Reading boundary |
|---|---:|---|
| Disease-free survival | HR 0.75 (0.65–0.87) | Program-level relative benefit |
| Overall survival | HR 0.74 (0.65–0.85) | Maturity follows the constituent reports |
| CPS ≥1 subgroup | HR 0.66 (0.49–0.88) | Not a universal regimen cutoff |
| Grade ≥3 adverse events | 42.9%–44.6% | Substantial treatment burden |
| Discontinuation for adverse events | 12.4%–17.7% | Material attrition during curative therapy |

**Interpretation:** Checkpoint intensification has a consistent favorable direction across the two pivotal programs, but counseling needs to include a long pathway and substantial toxicity.

**Limitations:** Two heterogeneous studies are too few to explore heterogeneity robustly. Pooling cannot isolate treatment timing, resolve immature trial-level OS, or tell whether preoperative therapy is preferable to postoperative-only intensification.

**Section counterpoint:** Reviews defending KEYNOTE-689 note that the EFS benefit came with similar surgical-completion rates. The dispute is the clinical threshold for benefit, not whether the randomized trial occurred. [Review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.critrevonc.2026.105410)

## 2. Cutaneous SCC: the same argument, one step behind

**[Moving into practice] Bottom line:** Adjuvant cemiplimab now has phase III evidence after surgery and postoperative RT in rigorously defined high-risk cSCC; this does not establish neoadjuvant use or surgery avoidance.

### C-POST

**Key conclusion:** In high-risk cSCC already treated with surgery and postoperative RT, cemiplimab for up to 48 weeks produced a large early DFS benefit. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2502449) · [NCT03969004](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03969004)

**Scope and methods:** This double-blind phase III trial randomized 415 patients to cemiplimab or placebo after complete surgery and postoperative RT. High-risk criteria included nodal extranodal extension with a node ≥2 cm, at least three involved nodes, or selected nonnodal features such as in-transit metastasis, T4 disease, perineural invasion, or locally recurrent disease with another risk factor.

**Protocol flow:** complete surgery → postoperative RT → cemiplimab or placebo for up to 48 weeks → disease-free survival assessment.

| 24-month outcome | Cemiplimab | Placebo |
|---|---:|---:|
| Disease-free survival | 87.1% | 64.1% |

The DFS hazard ratio was 0.32 (95% CI 0.20–0.51) at 24 months' median follow-up.

**Interpretation:** Pathology should trigger a deliberate post-RT multidisciplinary review. The evidence fits the C-POST risk phenotype, not any intuitive use of the phrase “high risk.”

**Limitations:** Overall survival was not different at the interim analysis. The trial does not address therapy before surgery, omission of surgery, or patients who did not receive postoperative RT.

### Updated European cSCC treatment guideline

**Key conclusion:** The updated guideline retains margin-controlled surgery as the foundation of resectable disease and adds formal MDT consideration of adjuvant cemiplimab after surgery plus RT in selected high-risk patients. [Guideline](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejca.2026.116764)

**Scope:** This is a peer-reviewed European interdisciplinary consensus update, not a new patient cohort.

**Decision flow:**

- Resectable common cSCC → surgery with postoperative margin assessment.
- Macroscopic nodal disease → individualized multidisciplinary treatment.
- High-risk disease after surgery and postoperative RT → discuss adjuvant cemiplimab.
- Advanced disease not amenable to curative surgery or RT → anti-PD-1 first-line.

**Interpretation:** The update creates a structured post-pathology handoff and integrates checkpoint therapy without displacing surgery for common resectable disease.

**Limitations:** Consensus recommendations do not prove comparative effectiveness. European regulatory context, frailty, comorbidity, immunosuppression, access, life expectancy, and patient preference all limit one-size-fits-all application.

## 3. What's going to replace your 3-month post-op CT/PET

**[Practice-changing within trials] Bottom line:** ctDNA assays now have strong prognostic validation and are entering treatment-assignment trials, but only randomized utility studies can show whether acting on a result improves outcomes.

### Tissue-agnostic methylome MRD validation

**Key conclusion:** A tissue-agnostic methylation classifier produced a strong recurrence-risk signal across HPV-positive and HPV-negative disease and often detected recurrence earlier than standard recognition. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2024.08.2348)

**Scope and methods:** Investigators analyzed 1,155 serial plasma samples from 325 patients using genome-wide methylome enrichment and a tissue-agnostic classifier. Training and validation sets were distinct, validation was blinded, and median follow-up was 60 months.

| Validation measure | Result | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| MRD-positive recurrence-free survival | HR 35.7 (10.8–117.8) | Very strong prognostic association; wide CI |
| HPV-negative subgroup | HR 42.3 | Signal not confined to viral disease |
| HPV-positive OPC subgroup | HR 24.1 | Large subgroup association |
| Surveillance accuracy | 91% sensitivity; 88% specificity | False negatives and positives remain |
| Lead time | Mean 4.1; maximum 14.9 months | Time to act, but no validated action |

**Interpretation:** The assay is mature enough to justify trials assigning imaging, adjuvant therapy, or salvage evaluation according to molecular risk.

**Limitations:** Observational validation proves prognosis, not clinical utility. It does not show that acting on a positive result improves survival or that the assay can replace imaging.

### SAVAL — NCT06088381

**Status:** Recruiting single-arm phase II; estimated n=61. [Live registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06088381)

**Protocol flow:** preoperative HPV ctDNA positive → transoral robotic surgery → pathology plus postoperative ctDNA at days 2–14 → if intermediate-risk pathology and ctDNA clears, observe without RT; if higher risk or ctDNA does not clear, give standard RT ± chemotherapy.

Intermediate risk requires pT1–3, N1–2, negative margins, and absent or microscopic extranodal extension up to 1 mm. The primary endpoint is 2-year PFS including salvage.

**Interpretation:** This is the clearest surgical utility trial in the group because pathology and molecular clearance must agree before postoperative treatment is withheld.

**Limitations:** There are no results. Small single-arm enrollment and a salvage-inclusive endpoint require careful reading of initial recurrence, regional failure, salvage burden, and functional outcomes.

### ReACT 2.0 — NCT07513324

**Status:** Not yet recruiting; randomized open-label phase II; estimated n=116. [Live registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07513324)

**Protocol flow:** molecular-risk population identified with ctHPVDNA → randomize → pembrolizumab every six weeks for up to nine cycles or observation → assess 2-year PFS and grade 3–4 adverse events.

**Interpretation:** The observation arm can separate a marker of recurrence risk from a marker that predicts benefit from pembrolizumab.

**Limitations:** There are no results. A phase II signal will depend on assay threshold, adherence, crossover, event count, and toxicity and would not by itself establish universal practice.

### Ohio State ctDNA-adaptive RT — NCT06323460

**Status:** Recruiting nonrandomized phase II; estimated n=45. [Live registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06323460)

**Protocol flow:** four weeks of RT plus systemic therapy for everyone → week-4 tumor-tissue-modified viral HPV DNA → if decline is >95%, complete five total weeks; if decline is ≤95%, complete seven total weeks → primary endpoint is 3-month PET complete metabolic response by Hopkins criteria.

**Interpretation:** This tests real-time molecular adaptation before conventional post-treatment imaging exists.

**Limitations:** There are no results. The small, nonrandomized design and surrogate PET endpoint cannot establish long-term oncologic noninferiority.

### University of Chicago response-adaptive RT — NCT06005324

**Status:** Recruiting nonrandomized phase I; estimated n=36. [Live registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06005324)

**Protocol flow:** three cycles over nine weeks of paclitaxel, carboplatin, and cetuximab → RECIST imaging response assigns lower-dose versus standard-dose chemoradiation → serial ctDNA is collected for feasibility and correlation.

**Interpretation:** The study may show whether serial ctDNA is practical and concordant with imaging in HPV-negative disease.

**Limitations:** Imaging, not ctDNA, assigns treatment. The study has no efficacy result and cannot validate a ctDNA threshold or establish noninferior control after lower-dose treatment.

### UCSF MRD surveillance cohort — NCT06606028

**Status:** Recruiting prospective observational cohort; estimated n=250. [Live registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06606028)

**Scope and methods:** Curative-intent surgery and radiation cohorts provide tumor tissue and serial blood samples of approximately 20 mL for up to two years. The primary analysis estimates the cumulative incidence of ctDNA positivity while treating death as a competing risk.

**Interpretation:** The cohort can define sampling intervals, positivity patterns, and lead time needed for an interventional surveillance trial.

**Limitations:** No treatment is assigned. Observation cannot demonstrate clinical utility, improved survival, or safe replacement of standard surveillance.

## 4. Where the extent of your dissection is actually being questioned

**[Hypothesis-generating] Bottom line:** Low pooled level IIB involvement supports careful selection for omission, while response-adapted omission of a whole neck dissection remains far more preliminary.

### Level IIB in clinically node-negative necks

**Key conclusion:** A 35-study meta-analysis found uncommon occult level IIB metastasis across four subsites, making selective omission plausible when expected oncologic yield is low and SAN morbidity matters. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1002/hed.70393)

**Scope and methods:** Following PRISMA 2020, investigators searched Embase, PubMed, Scopus, and Cochrane through April 30, 2026. Studies included adults with cN0 laryngeal, oral cavity, oropharyngeal, or hypopharyngeal SCC who underwent elective neck dissection with level-specific pathology. Random-effects models pooled metastasis proportions.

| Primary subsite | Pooled occult level IIB metastasis |
|---|---:|
| Oral cavity | 1.2% |
| Larynx | 1.7% |
| Hypopharynx | 2.5% |
| Oropharynx | 2.6% |

**Interpretation:** Subsite, nodal mapping, and intraoperative findings should select candidates; the pooled rates do not justify automatic omission.

**Limitations:** Included studies were heterogeneous and largely retrospective, randomized regional-control data are absent, and SAN morbidity and shoulder impairment were sparsely reported.

### Omission after imaging-negative neoadjuvant response

**Key conclusion:** A 58-patient retrospective series supplies a prospective-trial hypothesis, not proof that neck dissection can be safely omitted. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oraloncology.2026.108076)

**Scope and methods:** At Sun Yat-sen University, cT3–4N0M0 oral SCC patients treated from October 2019 through July 2023 received three to four cycles of PD-1 blockade plus paclitaxel and platinum, then extended primary-tumor resection. Imaging-negative necks were managed with dissection in 30 patients and without dissection in 28. Every patient received concurrent postoperative chemoradiotherapy. Median follow-up was 38 months.

| 36-month outcome | Neck dissection | No dissection | Hazard ratio (95% CI) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Neck control | 93.21% | 85.43% | 2.14 (0.43–10.61) |
| Disease-free survival | 82.11% | 70.59% | 1.26 (0.42–3.76) |
| Overall survival | 86.67% | 87.05% | 0.61 (0.15–2.48) |

**Interpretation:** The design suggests how to test response-adapted omission prospectively.

**Limitations:** “Not significant” is not equivalence. Selection was retrospective, confidence intervals were extremely wide, imaging-negative nodes lacked pathologic confirmation in the omission group, and postoperative chemoradiotherapy may have sterilized residual nodal disease.

## 5. Radiation de-escalation — changes your referral conversations

**[Useful for counseling now] Bottom line:** Lower-dose RT may improve swallowing in selected HPV-positive disease, but the comparison is not yet randomized by dose; cetuximab substitution did not offer a quality-of-life advantage over cisplatin.

### ORATOR/ORATOR2 post hoc comparison

**Key conclusion:** The 60 Gy cohort had a coherent functional advantage at one year across swallowing, symptom, and toxicity measures. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.06.3054)

**Scope and methods:** Investigators compared the RT arms of ORATOR2 (60 Gy, n=29) and ORATOR (70 Gy, n=30) in T1–2 p16-positive oropharynx cancer. Outcomes included 1-year MD Anderson Dysphagia Inventory change, EORTC domains, and grade 2–3 toxicity.

| One-year MDADI change | 60 Gy | 70 Gy | P value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Total score | +1.9 ± 12.2 | −5.8 ± 11.3 | .035 |
| Functional score | +5.5 ± 12.7 | −3.3 ± 12.2 | .023 |

**Interpretation:** The patient-facing reason to study lower dose is measurable swallowing function, not just fewer fractions.

**Limitations:** This was a 59-patient post hoc comparison across separate trials with different cisplatin schedules, eras, and potentially supportive care. It cannot establish oncologic noninferiority or justify 60 Gy outside an appropriate protocol.

### RTOG 1016 quality-of-life substudy

**Key conclusion:** Cetuximab did not produce a quality-of-life advantage that could offset its efficacy disadvantage relative to cisplatin. [QOL substudy](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.02.242) · [Parent efficacy trial](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)32779-X)

**Scope and methods:** The first 400 RTOG 1016 participants completed EORTC QLQ-C30 and H&N35 questionnaires at baseline, treatment end, and 3, 6, and 12 months; 375 were analyzable. A change >10 points was clinically meaningful. The parent randomized trial compared accelerated 70 Gy RT with cisplatin versus cetuximab.

| Outcome | Finding |
|---|---|
| 6-month QOL change | No significant difference in any domain |
| Persistent burdens | Senses, social eating, mouth opening, dry mouth, sticky saliva |
| 5-year overall survival | 77.9% cetuximab vs 84.6% cisplatin; HR 1.45 |
| 5-year progression-free survival | 67.3% vs 78.4%; HR 1.72 |
| Locoregional failure | 17.3% vs 9.9%; HR 2.05 |

**Interpretation:** For a cisplatin-eligible patient, cetuximab loses efficacy without a demonstrated patient-reported advantage.

**Limitations:** The QOL analysis was a subset with follow-up attrition. It does not identify the best regimen for a truly cisplatin-ineligible patient or compare newer alternatives.

## 6. Systemic options once someone is out of surgical/RT salvage

**[Background / pipeline] Bottom line:** Petosemtamab has entered pivotal randomized testing; ficerafusp alfa has an interesting but very early durable-response signal.

### Petosemtamab

**Pipeline conclusion:** The EGFR × LGR5 bispecific is being tested in both first-line PD-L1-positive disease and after platinum plus anti-PD-1, with survival as a phase III endpoint. [Review](https://doi.org/10.1080/14796694.2025.2511470)

| Trial | Population and comparison | Planned enrollment | Primary endpoint |
|---|---|---:|---|
| [LiGeR-HN1 / NCT06525220](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06525220) | First-line PD-L1-positive R/M HNSCC: petosemtamab + pembrolizumab vs pembrolizumab | 700 | OS and ORR |
| [LiGeR-HN2 / NCT06496178](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06496178) | After anti-PD-1 + platinum: petosemtamab vs investigator's choice | 600 | OS |

**Interpretation:** The program could create a new EGFR-directed option after salvage is exhausted and could alter first-line therapy if the combination beats pembrolizumab alone.

**Limitations:** Both open-label phase III trials remain recruiting. Their enrollment, endpoints, and biologic rationale are not efficacy results, and cross-trial comparisons should wait for randomized readouts.

### Ficerafusp alfa

**Key conclusion:** The EGFR/TGF-β fusion plus pembrolizumab produced durable responses in a small phase I mixed-solid-tumor population, including two HNSCC responses. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-25-0100) · [NCT04429542](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04429542)

**Scope and methods:** This first-in-human study evaluated pharmacokinetics in 46 monotherapy and 15 combination-treated patients with advanced solid tumors. In the pembrolizumab-combination efficacy set, four partial responses occurred—two HNSCC and two squamous anal cancer—and five additional patients had stable disease.

| Early efficacy measure | Result |
|---|---:|
| Partial responses | 4 |
| HNSCC responses | 2 |
| Overall response rate | 31% (95% CI 9–61) |
| Clinical benefit rate | 69% (95% CI 39–91) |
| Durable responses | 3 of 4 lasted >2 years; range 10.1–36.7 months |

One hypopharynx case responded after prior pembrolizumab-directed therapy and cetuximab.

**Interpretation:** The signal justifies disease-specific trials and makes the agent relevant to referral conversations after standard options are exhausted.

**Limitations:** This was a heterogeneous, uncontrolled, very small phase I cohort. The wide CI and only two HNSCC responses cannot define an HNSCC-specific response rate or prove a preferred sequence after cetuximab.

## Bottom line for tumor board

The baseline evidence shifts the resectable-HNSCC discussion from “add immunotherapy or not” to patient selection, timing, and what the entire treatment package costs in toxicity and delay. Surgery remains the fixed point in the perioperative pathway. ctDNA is the most plausible near-term mechanism for personalizing postoperative therapy and surveillance, but current trials should not be mistaken for validated utility. De-escalation signals in the neck and radiation dose are clinically meaningful research questions; neither supports casual off-protocol omission.
