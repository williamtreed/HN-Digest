1. Perioperative therapy: pathologic response is prognostic; a randomized comparator is coming

2 findings
Hypothesis-generating

A retrospective PCE cohort links broader pathologic response—not pCR alone—to RFS, while TRACE will compare a new antibody with pembrolizumab before surgery. Neither supports response-adapted reduction of a planned operation today.

  • Favorable pathologic response after neoadjuvant PCE occurred in 42.4% and was associated with 80.1% versus 62.7% 2-year RFS.▸

    Key conclusion — Response broader than pCR carried the prognostic signal.

    Counterpoint — Retrospective, 59 patients, one institution, and no untreated or alternative-regimen control.

    Evidence briefPubMed structured abstract · retrospective cohort

    Describe residual viability beyond a binary pCR

    Methods/scope: Fifty-nine HNSCC patients received neoadjuvant paclitaxel, carboplatin, and cetuximab followed by surgery. Favorable response was grade ≥2, with less than one-third viable cancer.

    Results: Favorable response occurred in 42.4% and was associated with 2-year RFS of 80.1% versus 62.7% (p=.032). Neither pCR nor radiologic complete/partial response was significantly associated with prognosis. Younger age and female sex were multivariable predictors of favorable response.

    Surgical interpretation

    If PCE is used in a protocol or selected multidisciplinary setting, capture residual viability beyond pCR and keep the original operative plan visible.

    Limitations · do not overread

    Response groups were defined after therapy. Association with RFS does not prove PCE improves survival or identify who can safely receive less surgery.

    Read the paper →

  • TRACE will randomize 78 resectable HNSCC patients to INCA33890 or pembrolizumab before surgery; it is not yet recruiting and has no results.▸

    Key conclusion — The comparator and endpoint are surgical-specimen based.

    Counterpoint — No recruiting sites, outcomes, EFS, OS, function, or validated de-escalation rule.

    Evidence briefClinicalTrials.gov · phase II · no results

    Randomized neoadjuvant comparison with surgery retained

    Protocol: NCT07876414 plans 78 patients with resectable locally advanced HNSCC. INCA33890 900 mg IV every two weeks for three cycles is compared with pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every three weeks for two cycles.

    Surgery + MPR≤10% viable primary + nodes

    Endpoint/status: Major pathologic response is ≤10% residual viable tumor across the resected primary and cervical nodes, assessed at nine versus six weeks. The trial was first posted October 15, updated October 22, is not yet recruiting, has an estimated February 2027 start, and lists no sites.

    Surgical interpretation

    TRACE preserves surgery and uses primary-plus-node pathology as the comparison endpoint. It is not yet a referral option.

    Limitations · do not overread

    Enrollment and dates are estimates. MPR is not demonstrated EFS, OS, function, or safe surgical de-escalation.

    View the registry →

Section counterpoint

Pathologic response can stratify prognosis and make a useful trial endpoint. It does not by itself establish that an operation, neck dissection, or adjuvant treatment can be reduced.

Go deeper

The PCE paper is a signal about how to describe the resection specimen; TRACE is the more consequential future test because it randomizes the neoadjuvant comparator.

2. Molecular and conventional surveillance: feasibility is ahead of clinical utility

2 findings
Hypothesis-generating

Pretreatment ctHPV-DNA was detectable in most—but not all—rare HPV-positive sinonasal/nasopharyngeal cancers, while a retrospective-only review found no survival advantage for routine radiologic follow-up. Neither validates a surveillance action rule.

  • Pretreatment ctHPV-DNA was detected in 80% of 15 HPV-positive sinonasal or nasopharyngeal cancers.▸

    Key conclusion — Baseline detection is feasible across several HPV genotypes.

    Counterpoint — Baseline-only, 15-patient feasibility work supplies no recurrence-detection or treatment-action performance.

    Evidence briefOpen-access full text · single-center feasibility cohort

    A baseline-negative fraction remains clinically important

    Methods/scope: Fifteen patients treated from 2017–2025 had pretreatment Streck-tube blood analyzed by droplet digital PCR. Thirteen tumors were SCC and two HPV-related multiphenotypic carcinoma.

    15Patients
    80%Baseline sensitivity
    6HPV genotypes represented

    Results: HPV16 accounted for 6/15 (40%); HPV18 for two, HPV33 for one, HPV35 for three, HPV45 for one, and HPV56 for two. Pretreatment sensitivity was 80%, and ctHPV-DNA level increased with T category.

    Surgical interpretation

    A positive baseline assay may enable longitudinal research in rare HPV-associated sinonasal/nasopharyngeal disease; the 20% baseline-negative fraction must remain visible.

    Limitations · do not overread

    Mixed subsites and histologies, no specificity estimate, no recurrence-detection performance, and no evidence that acting on the assay improves outcome.

    Read the open-access paper →

  • A review of 18 retrospective studies found no significant OS or DFS advantage for routine radiological versus non-radiological follow-up.▸

    Key conclusion — The comparative surveillance evidence remains weak.

    Counterpoint — Detection-mode proportions are confounded and do not prove symptom-led follow-up is superior.

    Evidence briefPubMed structured abstract · systematic review

    Routine imaging has not demonstrated a survival advantage

    Methods/scope: PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, and CENTRAL were searched through April 2025; 18 retrospective HNSCC follow-up studies met inclusion criteria.

    Reported recurrence-detection mode

    Symptoms61.5%
    Clinical24%
    Imaging14.5%

    Descriptive retrospective proportions—not comparative treatment effects.

    Results: Recurrences were reported as symptom-detected in 61.5%, clinically detected in 24%, and imaging-detected in 14.5%. Reported salvageable recurrence proportions were 60%, 45%, and 30%. Eight studies with survival data found no significant OS or DFS difference between radiological and non-radiological strategies.

    Surgical interpretation

    Build follow-up around rapid symptom access, examination, and site-specific imaging rather than assuming scheduled scans improve survival.

    Limitations · do not overread

    Heterogeneous sites, stages, eras, schedules, and definitions; selection, lead-time, and ascertainment bias. Do not abandon imaging where anatomy, symptoms, or post-treatment assessment justify it.

    Read the review →

Section counterpoint

Detectability and earlier detection are surrogate properties. The missing evidence is a prospectively tested action pathway that improves salvage, survival, or quality of life.

Go deeper

The ctHPV-DNA full text is useful for assay feasibility and genotype breadth; the surveillance review is most useful for showing how weak the comparative evidence remains.

3. Surgery, margins, neck pathology, imaging, and recovery

4 findings
Practice-informing

Margin status dominated one salvage-surgery validation; quantitative nodal burden added retrospective prognostic information; CT and MRI remained the practical jaw-invasion modalities; and ERAS improved efficiency outcomes with low-certainty evidence.

  • PATH outperformed rpTNM after salvage surgery, but its four-stage survival gradient was not ordered and margin status dominated.▸

    Key conclusion — The model discriminated better than rpTNM in this cohort.

    Counterpoint — Eighty-seven selected surgical patients and post hoc dichotomization limit use as an eligibility rule.

    Evidence briefPubMed structured abstract · external validation cohort

    Margin status carried the clearest independent signal

    Methods/scope: Eighty-seven patients with recurrent HNSCC underwent radical-intent salvage surgery from 2014–2024; PATH and rpTNM were compared by disease-specific survival, C-index, and AIC.

    0.670PATH C-index
    0.572rpTNM C-index
    3.57Post hoc adjusted HR

    Results: Five-year disease-specific survival was 65.9%, 92.3%, 37.4%, and 13.5% for PATH I–IV (p=.003), without an ordered gradient. rpTNM did not discriminate (p=.674). PATH C-index was 0.670 versus 0.572 and AIC 291.0 versus 304.1. Margin status was the only independent variable (70.0% vs 17.8%; p<.001); post hoc PATH I–II versus III–IV yielded adjusted HR 3.57.

    Surgical interpretation

    PATH may communicate prognosis better than recurrent-stage TNM; the operative priority reinforced here is a negative margin when anatomically and functionally reasonable.

    Limitations · do not overread

    Retrospective selection for radical surgery and non-ordered stages. Do not use PATH alone to determine candidacy or assume a negative margin is achievable.

    Read the paper →

  • Weighted lymph-node ratio had the strongest of three quantitative nodal associations with OS in 343 HPV-unrelated cancers.▸

    Key conclusion — Side-specific quantitative pathology may add prognostic information.

    Counterpoint — Data-derived cutoffs and modest discrimination do not create a treatment rule or pure surgical-quality metric.

    Evidence briefPubMed structured abstract · retrospective cohort

    Quantitative nodal measures complement rather than replace anatomy

    Methods/scope: A single center analyzed 410 surgical patients from 2010–2020, separating 343 HPV-unrelated cancers from 67 HPV-associated oropharyngeal cancers.

    Results: In HPV-unrelated disease, lymph-node yield, ratio, and weighted ratio were independently associated with OS: HR 1.58 (p=.012), 1.77 (p=.026), and 2.30 (p<.001), with C-indices 0.55, 0.59, and 0.61. Associations were ipsilateral. In no-adjuvant-recommendation patients, yield HR was 1.92 and weighted ratio HR 2.31; in pN0 disease, yield HR was 1.88. Measures were not prognostic in HPV-associated OPSCC, where ENE dominated.

    Surgical interpretation

    Adequate retrieval and side-specific reporting remain valuable; weighted ratio is a research stratifier, not a replacement for anatomic pathology.

    Limitations · do not overread

    Retrospective single center, data-derived cutoffs, multiple subgroups, modest discrimination, and no external validation. Yield also reflects anatomy, specimen handling, and case complexity.

    Read the paper →

  • CT and MRI showed similar pooled performance for jaw invasion; SPECT was highly sensitive but poorly specific.▸

    Key conclusion — Use complementary imaging rather than one pooled winner.

    Counterpoint — Indirect heterogeneous comparisons do not define marginal-versus-segmental resection.

    Evidence briefPubMed structured abstract · 38-study meta-analysis

    Translate pooled accuracy into operative uncertainty

    Methods/scope: Thirty-eight studies contributed 78 datasets evaluating imaging for jaw-bone invasion in oral SCC.

    Pooled diagnostic performance

    MRI84.3%81.6%
    SPECT96.7%57.5%
    CBCT88.6%77.1%

    Results: In OSCC studies using histopathology as reference, CT sensitivity/specificity was 78.9%/81.7% and MRI 87.2%/75.4%. Across all included datasets, CT was 78.7%/84.2%, MRI 84.3%/81.6%, SPECT 96.7%/57.5%, cone-beam CT 88.6%/77.1%, PET/CT 80.2%/81.5%, and panoramic radiography 65.7%/90.0%.

    Surgical interpretation

    CT and MRI remain complementary workhorses. A high-sensitivity modality such as SPECT may create many false positives and should not independently dictate segmental resection.

    Limitations · do not overread

    Heterogeneous anatomy, thresholds, prevalence, and reference standards. Pooled indirect comparisons do not prove superiority or a safe resection boundary.

  • ERAS pathways shortened stay by 2.75 days and 72-hour opioid exposure by 55.98 MME, with low-certainty evidence.▸

    Key conclusion — Efficiency outcomes improved; rare-event safety equivalence remains unproven.

    Counterpoint — Mostly nonrandomized studies, heterogeneity, and low or very-low GRADE certainty.

    Evidence briefPubMed structured abstract · updated meta-analysis

    Standardize recovery while keeping certainty visible

    Methods/scope: Fifteen comparative studies with 2,787 free-flap patients were pooled using random effects after searches through July 22, 2026; certainty was graded.

    −2.75 dLength of stay
    −55.9872-hour MME
    LowGRADE certainty

    Results: ERAS reduced length of stay by 2.75 days (13 studies; 95% CI 1.35–4.16 shorter) and 72-hour opioid exposure by 55.98 MME (three studies; 95% CI 41.07–70.89 lower). No clear difference appeared for 30-day readmission, total flap failure, reoperation, or 30-day mortality. The abstract's enteral-feeding estimate has an internally inconsistent direction/interval, so it is not reproduced.

    Surgical interpretation

    ERAS is reasonable for standardizing mobilization, analgesia, and discharge planning; the strongest signal is efficiency.

    Limitations · do not overread

    Low certainty for stay/opioid outcomes and very low for most safety outcomes. Shorter stay does not prove better recovery or equivalent rare-event safety.

    Read the meta-analysis →

Section counterpoint

Retrospective prognostic models and pooled diagnostic accuracy refine probabilities. They do not replace resectability judgment, pathology review, or patient-specific functional tradeoffs.

Go deeper

PATH is the clearest caution about relying on a staging label; the jaw-imaging meta-analysis is the most directly useful for operative planning; ERAS supports pathway work while keeping certainty visible.

4. Postoperative radiation and treatment delivery: discordant selection evidence, clearer process signals

4 findings
Practice-informing

Two small pN1 oral-cancer cohorts point in different directions, so PORT remains an individualized decision. Navigation improved time to radiation, and protons reduced selected acute toxicities without improving disease control or survival.

  • In 85 pT1–2 pN1 oral cancers, PORT was not associated with adjusted OS or regional RFS.▸

    Key conclusion — Observation remained plausible after adequate neck management.

    Counterpoint — The null result has wide intervals and cannot exclude benefit in every pN1 subgroup.

    Evidence briefPubMed structured abstract · retrospective cohort

    No adjusted PORT association in the larger cohort

    Methods/scope: Eighty-five pT1–2 OSCC patients had one ipsilateral node ≤3 cm and no ENE; 43 received PORT. Poor differentiation, high-risk margins, and bilateral neck dissection predicted PORT use.

    Results: At 60 months, OS was 63% with surgery alone and 65% with PORT; regional-recurrence probability was 30% and 25%. Adjusted PORT HR was 0.94 for OS (95% CI 0.46–1.92; p=.9) and 0.81 for regional RFS (0.30–2.17; p=.7). Apparent pT2, age-under-70, and yield-under-18 signals were exploratory.

    Surgical interpretation

    Observation remains defensible for selected pT1–2 pN1 patients with adequate neck management and no other adverse feature.

    Limitations · do not overread

    Retrospective treatment selection, 85 patients, wide intervals, and subgroup multiplicity. A null estimate is not proof of no individual benefit.

    Read the paper →

  • A second pN1 cohort found improved RFS only after propensity matching reduced 58 patients to 30.▸

    Key conclusion — The positive estimate is fragile and conflicts with the full cohort.

    Counterpoint — Incomplete adverse-pathology capture, only 30 matched patients, wide CI, and overfitting risk.

    Evidence briefOpen-access full text · retrospective cohort

    Matched RFS signal does not settle the PORT question

    Methods/scope: Fifty-eight pT1–2 pN1 OSCC patients without extracapsular spread were studied; propensity matching retained 14 surgery-only and 16 PORT patients and excluded 28 without a match.

    Same study, different analytic populations
    AnalysisOSRFS
    Full cohort · n=58p=.628p=.244
    Matched · n=30p=.893HR 0.21; p=.029

    Results: In the full cohort, PORT was not associated with OS (p=.628) or RFS (p=.244). In the 30 matched patients, PORT was associated with RFS (multivariable HR 0.21; 95% CI 0.02–0.85; p=.029) but not OS (p=.893).

    Surgical interpretation

    Use the result to support individualized discussion when other risk features cluster, not to make pN1 alone automatic.

    Limitations · do not overread

    Retrospective data back to 2009, residual confounding, incomplete adverse pathology, and a very small matched model. It does not overturn the null full cohort or companion study.

    Read the open-access paper →

  • A free-flap navigation pathway shortened median surgery-to-radiation time from 56 to 45 days without a DFS difference.▸

    Key conclusion — Ownership and coordination produced an actionable process signal.

    Counterpoint — Before/after design cannot prove causality or survival benefit.

    Evidence briefPubMed structured abstract · retrospective before/after cohort

    Navigation moved the postoperative clock

    Methods/scope: A standardized navigation pathway was evaluated in 404 head-and-neck free-flap patients.

    Median surgery-to-radiation interval

    Before56 d
    Navigation45 d

    Bar scale normalized to 60 days; observational before/after comparison.

    Results: Median surgery-to-RT time fell from 56 days (IQR 39–83) to 45 days (IQR 31–64; p=.004). DFS did not differ (p=.7695). This extends the September 28 radiation-timing signal.

    Surgical interpretation

    Assign ownership of dental clearance, pathology review, referral, simulation, and wound readiness.

    Limitations · do not overread

    Nonrandomized eras, possible secular and case-mix changes. An 11-day median reduction does not prove every patient met ≤42 days or that navigation caused it.

    Read the paper →

  • IMPT reduced grade 3 acute dysphagia and dryness versus IMRT without an efficacy advantage.▸

    Key conclusion — Frame proton benefit around selected acute toxicity.

    Counterpoint — Only three comparative sources and inconclusive heterogeneous nutritional endpoints.

    Evidence briefPubMed structured abstract · comparative evidence synthesis

    Acute toxicity—not tumor control—is the current advantage

    Methods/scope: Two phase III randomized trials and one matched cohort comparing IMPT with IMRT for oropharyngeal cancer were synthesized.

    Selected comparative outcomes
    OutcomeIMPTIMRTEffect
    Grade 3 acute dysphagia24.0%40.9%RR 0.65
    Grade 3 dryness19.9%32.5%RR 0.72
    Locoregional recurrenceNo significant differenceHR 1.37

    Results: LRR HR was 1.37 (95% CI 0.56–3.36), PFS HR 0.90 (0.61–1.33), and OS HR 0.79 (0.32–1.98). Grade 3 acute dysphagia was 24.0% versus 40.9% (RR 0.65; 0.51–0.84); grade 3 xerostomia/oral-pharyngeal dryness was 19.9% versus 32.5% (RR 0.72; 0.54–0.95). Twelve-month gastrostomy RR was 0.32 (0.08–1.28); weight-loss and composite nutrition estimates were highly heterogeneous.

    Surgical interpretation

    Refer around reduction of selected acute toxicities and dose distribution, not superior disease control or proven late feeding-tube benefit.

    Limitations · do not overread

    Three sources, one nonrandomized, broad efficacy intervals, heterogeneous nutrition outcomes, and limited late-toxicity evidence.

Section counterpoint

PORT selection remains confounded in small observational cohorts. Process improvement and toxicity reduction are more credible signals this cycle than any new oncologic indication.

Go deeper

Read the two pN1 studies together—the 30-patient matched analysis should not be isolated from its null full cohort or from the separate adjusted null result.

5. Cutaneous SCC: durable neoadjuvant follow-up, expert ART boundaries, and a changing trial landscape

5 findings
Practice-informing

Final phase II cemiplimab follow-up is reassuring for patients with deep pathologic response, but remains single-arm. ART consensus is expert opinion, exenteration evidence lacks an eye-preservation comparator, C-PRE is suspended, and the Quad Shot study is feasibility-only.

  • At 54 months after neoadjuvant cemiplimab, no new recurrences appeared and none of 40 patients with pCR had recurred.▸

    Key conclusion — Deep pathologic response remained durable in final phase II follow-up.

    Counterpoint — Single-arm, post-treatment response groups cannot establish omission or comparative efficacy.

    Evidence briefPubMed/JCO abstract · final phase II analysis · NCT04154943

    Durability strengthens response as a trial endpoint

    Methods/scope: Final follow-up of the multicenter resectable stage II–IV cSCC trial that previously reported 51% pCR and 89% estimated 12-month EFS.

    Estimated 54-month EFS by pathologic response

    pCR81.5%
    MPR88.9%
    Partial100%
    No/NE45.7%

    Post-treatment groups; not randomized comparisons.

    Results: At 54 months' median follow-up, 16 EFS events had occurred; no new recurrence occurred, and five new events were comorbidity deaths without cSCC recurrence. Estimated 54-month EFS was 76.2% overall, 81.5% after pCR, 88.9% after MPR, 100% after partial pathologic response, and 45.7% in nonresponders/not evaluable. None of 40 pCR patients recurred; OS was 79.4%, with no new grade ≥3 treatment-emergent event.

    Surgical interpretation

    Use the durability for counseling and pathologic-response trial stratification after neoadjuvant therapy plus surgery.

    Limitations · do not overread

    Single arm, response defined after treatment, few later events, and competing deaths. Do not compare response groups causally or omit surgery/RT for a clinical response.

    Read the final analysis →

  • A 35-expert Delphi panel defined node-negative cSCC ART scenarios and favored omission after neoadjuvant pCR.▸

    Key conclusion — The clearest consensus was ART for an unresectable positive margin.

    Counterpoint — Expert agreement—especially pCR-based omission—is not comparative outcomes evidence.

    Evidence briefPubMed structured abstract · multidisciplinary Delphi consensus

    A framework for tumor board, not a trial result

    Methods/scope: Thirty-five experts—15 radiation oncologists, nine dermatologic/Mohs surgeons, seven medical oncologists, three head-and-neck surgeons, and one surgical oncologist—used ≥80% concordance for consensus.

    Selected Delphi agreements
    ScenarioAgreement
    Unresectable positive margin → ART35/35
    Gross bone or skull-base invasion → ART32/35
    Clinical nerve invasion → ART31/35
    pCR after neoadjuvant IO → omit ART34/35

    Results: All 35 recommended ART when further surgery is impossible and gross/microscopic margin remains positive. After negative margins, consensus included gross cortical/marrow or skull-base invasion, four risk factors, three risk factors, PNI ≥0.1 mm plus another factor, or clinical nerve invasion. Thirty-four of 35 favored omitting ART after neoadjuvant pCR. Radiation oncologists favored 4–8 weeks, 60–66 Gy at 2 Gy/fraction or 50–55 Gy at 2.5 Gy/fraction. No consensus emerged for elective nodal-basin RT.

    Surgical interpretation

    Re-excise when feasible; ART for an unresectable positive margin is the firmest consensus. Use the other scenarios as a discussion framework.

    Limitations · do not overread

    Only three head-and-neck surgeons; overlapping risk factors; no comparative outcomes. pCR-based ART omission has not been randomized.

    Read the consensus →

  • Orbital-exenteration series pooled 70.8% five-year OS, 51.6% five-year DFS, and 39.2% complications.▸

    Key conclusion — The review quantifies consent but cannot compare eye-preserving care.

    Counterpoint — Retrospective heterogeneity, ecological meta-regression, and no direct comparator.

    Evidence briefPubMed structured abstract · systematic review and meta-analysis

    Outcomes after exenteration, not proof of superiority

    Methods/scope: Ten studies included 217 patients with advanced head-and-neck cSCC undergoing orbital exenteration.

    70.8%5-year OS
    51.6%5-year DFS
    39.2%Complications

    Results: Pooled OS was 73.7% at two years (95% CI 63.4–81.9) and 70.8% at five years (56.0–82.2); DFS was 66.1% (42.2–83.9) and 51.6% (37.8–65.2). Local recurrence was 23.7%, regional recurrence 14.0%, and complications 39.2%. Margin-positive proportion was the only study-level covariate associated with local recurrence (β 0.076 per percentage point; p=.015; R²=100%).

    Surgical interpretation

    Use for consent and margin planning alongside orbital-apex extent, cranial-nerve involvement, clearance, function, and systemic options.

    Limitations · do not overread

    Selected operable patients, wide intervals, ecological regression, and no exenteration-versus-orbit-preservation comparison. Pooled survival cannot establish superiority.

Go deeper

Bottom line for tumor board

This cycle changes confidence more than standards. Neoadjuvant pathologic response is increasingly durable and prognostic, but no finding permits surgery omission. For pT1–2 pN1 oral SCC, preserve equipoise: two new cohorts disagree, and the positive estimate rests on 30 matched patients. In salvage surgery and orbital-invasion cSCC, margin status remains central, while new staging and imaging summaries should refine—not replace—operative judgment. Invest in delivery systems: navigation shortened surgery-to-RT time, ERAS reduced stay and opioid exposure, and protons reduced selected acute toxicities without an efficacy advantage. For cSCC trials, distinguish mature single-arm cemiplimab follow-up from expert ART consensus and result-free registry events; C-PRE suspension has no posted clinical explanation, and the Quad Shot study is a small feasibility protocol.
