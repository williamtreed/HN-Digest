1. Perioperative therapy and residual pathology: strong signals, weak permission to de-escalate

4 findings
Hypothesis-generating

Two small neoadjuvant studies show activity, and two retrospective pathology models refine risk after surgery. None establishes that a planned operation, neck dissection, or adjuvant treatment can safely be reduced outside a trial.

  • Neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy produced a 65.7% ORR in 35 evaluable oral-SCC patients and 50% pCR among the 20 who underwent surgery.▸

    Key conclusion — Activity was substantial, but only 20/39 patients proceeded to surgery.

    Counterpoint — Retrospective, heterogeneous PD-1 therapy, short follow-up, and no credible surgery-versus-no-surgery comparison.

    Evidence briefPubMed abstract · two-center retrospective cohort

    Keep the pre-treatment operation visible

    Methods/scope: Thirty-nine consecutive patients received a PD-1 inhibitor plus paclitaxel and cisplatin; 35 were radiographically evaluable and 20 proceeded to surgery.

    65.7%ORR; 95% CI 45.8–79.4
    10/20Surgical pCR
    84.4%Estimated 2-year DFS

    Results: Nine complete and 14 partial responses were observed. At 16 months' median follow-up, six DFS events and no deaths had occurred.

    Surgical interpretation: Document the planned operation and scrutinize why nearly half the cohort did not proceed to surgery.

    Limitations/do not overread: Thirty-nine patients, only 20 operations, short follow-up, and low event count. The exploratory management comparison cannot establish equivalence.

    Read the paper →

  • Two cycles of anlotinib plus paclitaxel-cisplatin produced a 66.7% ORR, but only eight of 21 patients underwent surgery.▸

    Key conclusion — All eight surgical patients had R0 resection; five had major pathologic response.

    Counterpoint — Single-arm phase II with exploratory surgical endpoints and incomplete toxicity capture.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · prospective single-arm phase II · ChiCTR2100046440

    Activity signal, not proof of surgical preservation

    Methods/scope: Twenty-one technically resectable patients received two 21-day cycles of anlotinib plus paclitaxel-cisplatin.

    Results: ORR was 66.7% (14/21; 95% CI 43.0–85.4). Eight underwent surgery; all achieved R0, 5/8 had major pathologic response, and 4/8 underwent less extensive resection than initially planned. One grade 2 primary-tumor bleed occurred; proteinuria was not systematically captured.

    Surgical interpretation: The denominator reaching surgery is the essential number for consent and future trials.

    Limitations/do not overread: No control, only eight operations, and exploratory functional claims. Benefit beyond TP is unproven.

    Read the paper →

  • A composite primary-or-nodal residual viable tumor score separated outcomes after neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy better than ypTNM.▸

    Key conclusion — The higher RVT percentage at either site defined three sharply separated prognostic groups.

    Counterpoint — Single-center retrospective derivation with internal correction only.

    Evidence briefPubMed abstract · retrospective derivation cohort

    Residual disease across both compartments matters

    Methods/scope: The score used the higher RVT percentage in the primary bed or any involved node among 353 stage III–IV oral-SCC patients treated with immunochemotherapy and surgery.

    Results: Versus ≤10% RVT, 11–40% and >40% strata had worse EFS (adjusted HR 4.87 and 9.25) and OS (5.12 and 9.87; all p<.001). Optimism-corrected C-index was 0.76 versus 0.70 for ypTNM; NRI was 0.42 (95% CI 0.29–0.55).

    Surgical interpretation: Standardized primary-and-nodal viable-tumor reporting may improve postoperative trial stratification.

    Limitations/do not overread: Data-driven cut points, internal bootstrap only, and no evidence that score-directed therapy improves outcome.

    Read the paper →

  • Within pENE-positive HNSCC, positive margins, poor differentiation, and more than three metastatic nodes accumulated into progressively worse survival.▸

    Key conclusion — Five-year OS fell from 55.0% with no additional factors to 6.7% with two.

    Counterpoint — Retrospective 2008–2016 cohort; the two-factor group contained only 15 patients.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · retrospective surgery + adjuvant CRT cohort

    ENE is not a uniform postoperative risk state

    Methods/scope: Investigators studied 281 pENE-positive patients and counted positive margins, poor differentiation, and >3 metastatic nodes as additional “P” factors.

    Results: Five-year OS was 55.0%, 37.5%, and 6.7% for 0, 1, and 2 factors; DFS was 50.5%, 34.5%, and 0% (both p<.001).

    Surgical interpretation: Use the individual features for postoperative trial stratification and counseling.

    Limitations/do not overread: No external validation and no evidence-based intensification regimen follows from the score.

    Read the paper →

Section counterpoint

Radiographic and pathologic response are prognostic signals, not automatic permission to shrink surgery. Attrition before operation and unmeasured response-based selection are central uncertainties.

Go deeper

The composite RVT study has the largest surgical cohort and clearest pathology framework; the 39-patient chemoimmunotherapy cohort is the key caution about denominator selection.

2. ctDNA/MRD: persistence matters, while an intervention trial disappears

2 findings
Practice-informing

Early post-treatment TTMV-HPV DNA trajectories strongly separated recurrence risk in a retrospective cohort, but ReACT 2.0 was withdrawn before enrollment. The biomarker remains useful for risk discussion, not a stand-alone trigger for adjuvant treatment.

  • Persistent—but not transient—TTMV-HPV DNA positivity 1–89 days after treatment identified high recurrence risk.▸

    Key conclusion — Six of seven persistently positive patients recurred; none of six whose positive result cleared recurred.

    Counterpoint — Small positive subgroups, selected repeat testing, and no intervention trial.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · retrospective eight-site cohort

    Trajectory is more informative than one early draw

    Methods/scope: Among 332 HPV-associated OPSCC patients tested 1–89 days after definitive therapy, 310 were initially negative, three indeterminate, and 19 positive.

    Results: Of 59 retested after an initial negative, two converted and both recurred. Of 13 initially positive patients with trajectory follow-up, six cleared and none recurred; seven remained positive and six recurred. The last early result was associated with EFS in a 90-day landmark analysis.

    Surgical interpretation: Repeat an unexpectedly positive early result before treating it as molecular persistence.

    Limitations/do not overread: Retrospective testing, small positive strata, selected retesting, and no randomized action rule.

    Read the paper →

  • Update since August 3: ReACT 2.0 was withdrawn before enrolling its first participant.▸

    Key conclusion — The ctHPV-DNA–guided adjuvant pembrolizumab-versus-observation trial will not answer its randomized question.

    Counterpoint — Withdrawal supplies no efficacy, safety, feasibility, or biomarker-validity result.

    Evidence briefClinicalTrials.gov · updated Oct 8 · no results

    Zero enrolled; no inference possible

    Protocol/status: NCT07513324 had planned a randomized phase II comparison of ctHPV-DNA–risk-adapted adjuvant pembrolizumab versus observation in HPV-positive OPSCC.

    Update: The registry lists withdrawn status, 0 actual participants, and “study halted prematurely prior to enrollment of first participant.”

    Surgical interpretation: Remove the study from referral options and avoid implying the strategy generated evidence.

    Limitations/do not overread: Withdrawal does not invalidate other ctDNA trials.

    View the registry update →

Section counterpoint

Strong prognostic enrichment is not demonstrated clinical utility. The missing link remains proof that acting on an early ctDNA result improves outcome without avoidable treatment.

Go deeper

Read the TTMV-HPV DNA paper for trajectory denominators—especially the 13 initially positive patients—rather than extrapolating from the full cohort.

3. Neck and function: use imaging to stratify, not to declare certainty

3 findings
Practice-informing

Baseline swallow impairment predicted early post-TOS dysphagia, response-assessment PET was best at ruling out rather than finding persistent neck disease, and imaging-detected nodal count carried prognostic information in a small HPV-positive cohort.

  • Baseline DIGEST ≥1 was associated with moderate-to-severe dysphagia after TOS in an E3311 secondary analysis.▸

    Key conclusion — Adjusted OR for post-TOS DIGEST ≥2 was 4.69 (95% CI 1.32–16.64).

    Counterpoint — Selected 89-patient subset, wide intervals, and acute pre-adjuvant outcomes only.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · exploratory E3311 secondary analysis

    Use mild baseline impairment to target support

    Methods/scope: Eighty-nine patients had centrally reviewed baseline and post-TOS modified barium swallows before adjuvant therapy.

    Results: Baseline DIGEST was 0 in 80%, 1 in 17%, and ≥2 in 3%. After surgery, 51% worsened by at least one grade and 16% had DIGEST ≥2. Baseline DIGEST ≥1 yielded a 23.8% absolute risk difference (95% CI 2.6–48.8%) and optimism-corrected AUC 0.75.

    Surgical interpretation: Trigger swallowing prehabilitation, nutrition planning, and early reassessment—not automatic TOS exclusion.

    Limitations/do not overread: Exploratory subset and no long-term functional endpoint.

    Read the paper →

  • Post-treatment FDG-PET/CT had 89.2% NPV but 40.0% sensitivity for persistent nodal disease in a low-HPV cN+ cohort.▸

    Key conclusion — Rule-out performance was clinically useful; positive and indeterminate scans were less definitive.

    Counterpoint — Retrospective, heterogeneous RT regimens, local reclassification, and no randomized neck-management comparison.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · retrospective response-assessment cohort

    A negative scan reduces risk; it does not erase it

    Methods/scope: One hundred twenty-five cN+ patients with complete primary response underwent PET/CT 2–4 months after RT, CRT, or bio-RT.

    40.0%Sensitivity
    94.3%Specificity
    57.1%PPV
    89.2%NPV

    Results: Oropharyngeal NPV was 95.8% in HPV-positive and 85.7% in HPV-negative tumors.

    Surgical interpretation: Use a negative scan within a complete surveillance pathway; confirm positive or indeterminate findings rather than reflexively operating.

    Limitations/do not overread: Low sensitivity and residual false-negative risk.

    Read the paper →

  • At least five imaging-detected nodal metastases were associated with worse survival in 68 HPV-associated OPSCC patients.▸

    Key conclusion — Imaging nodal count may add clinical prognostic information when pathology is unavailable.

    Counterpoint — Very small retrospective cohort, few events, and wide confidence intervals.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · FDG-PET/CT + Node-RADS cohort

    Count positive nodes explicitly—but preserve uncertainty

    Methods/scope: Two readers evaluated 68 patients; a positive node had SUVmax ≥3.5 or Node-RADS ≥3.

    Results: Five-year OS and EFS were 80% and 77%. At least five imaging-positive nodes were associated with worse OS (HR 11.4; 95% CI 3.1–44.5) and EFS (HR 6.5; 2.0–20.1); estimates remained large after clinical-stage adjustment.

    Surgical interpretation: Use explicit imaging counts for multidisciplinary risk discussion.

    Limitations/do not overread: The count is not externally validated and cannot replace neck-dissection pathology.

    Read the paper →

Section counterpoint

These tools refine probabilities. None converts imaging or a baseline swallow study into a definitive operative eligibility, neck-dissection, or adjuvant-treatment rule.

Go deeper

The E3311 analysis is most actionable for prehabilitation; the PET study is the clearest reminder to separate rule-out value from sensitivity.

4. Radiation pathways: a distant-relapse model and a new older-adult trial

2 findings
Background-changing

A small postoperative cohort proposes four features for distant-relapse enrichment, while a newly posted 60-patient trial will compare three-week hypofractionation with conventional treatment in adults 65 and older. Neither changes current standards.

  • Four pathology/anatomy features identified a small postoperative group with 33.3% 12-month distant-metastasis-free survival.▸

    Key conclusion — Hypopharyngeal site, contralateral nodes, level IV-or-lower nodes, and ENE may enrich postoperative trials.

    Counterpoint — Seventy-eight patients, single retrospective cohort, and no external validation.

    Evidence briefPubMed abstract · retrospective PORT/POCRT cohort

    A trial-enrichment model, not a treatment algorithm

    Methods/scope: Four prespecified factors defined low-, intermediate-, and high-risk groups among 78 postoperative patients.

    Results: At 50 months' median follow-up, 39 had recurred and distant metastasis was the commonest first failure. Twelve-month DMFS was 85.8%, 79.3%, and 33.3% for 0, 1, and ≥2 factors. High-risk status remained associated with DMFS after T-category adjustment (HR 5.43; 95% CI 2.24–13.15).

    Surgical interpretation: Use the features to design intensification or surveillance studies.

    Limitations/do not overread: Do not intensify therapy solely from this score.

    Read the paper →

  • A new randomized 60-patient protocol will compare three-week hypofractionated RT with conventional RT in adults aged 65 or older.▸

    Key conclusion — The trial asks a relevant access-and-tolerance question across definitive and postoperative settings.

    Counterpoint — Not yet recruiting, no posted locations, and no results.

    Evidence briefClinicalTrials.gov · first posted Sep 29 · no results

    Shorter treatment is a hypothesis, not established equivalence

    Protocol: NCT07847060 plans to randomize nonmetastatic HNSCC patients aged ≥65 to image-guided hypofractionation or conventional RT.

    Schedules/outcome: Experimental treatment is 45 Gy in 15 fractions definitively or 40.5 Gy in 15 fractions postoperatively; the comparator uses 1.8–2.0 Gy fractions over 6–7 weeks. One-year locoregional control is primary; estimated primary completion is October 2027.

    Surgical interpretation: Consider as a future referral option only after recruitment and sites are confirmed.

    Limitations/do not overread: Small open-label protocol mixing treatment settings; registration is not evidence of noninferiority or safety.

    View the registry record →

Section counterpoint

One study derives risk and the other proposes a solution in a different population. Neither demonstrates that intensification or abbreviated RT improves survival or function.

Go deeper

The postoperative model is useful for trial enrichment; the new registry matters mainly as a future option once recruitment and sites are confirmed.

5. Cutaneous SCC: COMPASS proposes a more discriminating staging system

1 finding
Practice-informing

COMPASS-SCC uses five familiar pathologic features and outperformed AJCC8 and BWH in a very large multicenter retrospective dataset. It is promising for risk communication but is not yet a replacement standard.

  • COMPASS-SCC outperformed AJCC8 and BWH across multiple discrimination metrics in a 20,972-tumor development dataset.▸

    Key conclusion — A transparent five-feature point system maps tumors from T1 (0 points) to T4 (≥5).

    Counterpoint — Retrospective development data; absolute comparative performance values were not reported in the abstract.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · 12-center retrospective database

    A credible challenger that still needs implementation evidence

    Methods/scope: The endpoint was metastasis or disease-specific death among 20,972 primary cSCC tumors.

    Score: Diameter 2–<5 cm earns 1 point and ≥5 cm earns 2; invasion beyond fat or depth >6 mm earns 1; moderate and poor differentiation earn 1 and 2; large-caliber PNI and LVI each earn 1.

    Results: Sensitivity, NPV, AUROC, and Uno's c-index were significantly higher than AJCC8 and BWH.

    Surgical interpretation: The system could standardize risk language and trial selection after validation.

    Limitations/do not overread: No prospective evidence that COMPASS-directed nodal staging, surveillance, or adjuvant treatment improves outcomes.

    Read the paper →

Section counterpoint

Better retrospective discrimination does not automatically improve decisions. Calibration, external validation, treatment interactions, and head-and-neck-specific implementation still matter.

Go deeper

The value is the transparent point assignment and 20,972-tumor scale; the missing absolute performance estimates should temper claims of superiority.

6. Systemic therapy: setanaxib misses the tumor-shrinkage endpoint

1 finding
Hypothesis-generating

Adding setanaxib to pembrolizumab did not improve the randomized primary endpoint in CAF-rich recurrent/metastatic HNSCC. Exploratory survival and immune signals justify research, not clinical adoption.

  • Setanaxib plus pembrolizumab missed its primary tumor-size endpoint; exploratory PFS and OS estimates favored the combination.▸

    Key conclusion — Best tumor-size change was −7.9% versus −12.9% with placebo plus pembrolizumab (p=.7008).

    Counterpoint — Fifty-five patients, 80% confidence intervals, permissive α, multiple secondary analyses, and a negative primary endpoint.

    Evidence briefRandomized double-blind phase II · NCT05323656

    Endpoint hierarchy matters more than attractive hazard ratios

    Methods/scope: Twenty-seven CAF-rich patients received setanaxib 800 mg twice daily plus pembrolizumab and 28 received placebo plus pembrolizumab.

    Results: Least-squares mean tumor-size difference was 5.1 points (80% CI −11.9 to 22.0). Exploratory PFS HR was 0.58 (80% CI 0.38–0.89; p=.1023) and OS HR 0.45 (0.24–0.85; p=.1057) against a p<.20 threshold. CD8+ TILs increased with setanaxib (p=.037). Thirty-three serious adverse events occurred (16 versus 17); four deaths occurred, all in the placebo arm.

    Surgical interpretation: Do not use the exploratory estimates to justify off-trial treatment or conversion surgery.

    Limitations/do not overread: Small trial and negative primary endpoint; PFS, OS, immune, and death imbalances are hypothesis-generating.

    Read the paper →

Section counterpoint

Randomization strengthens the comparison, but the small trial and negative primary endpoint outweigh favorable exploratory hazard ratios.

Go deeper

Focus on the endpoint hierarchy and 80% confidence intervals before interpreting the PFS, OS, CD8, or death imbalance.

Bottom line for tumor board

This cycle sharpens boundaries more than it changes standards. For neoadjuvant therapy, keep the original surgical plan visible and treat response-based reduction as a trial question; if pathology follows immunochemotherapy, report viable tumor across both primary and nodal sites. In HPV-positive OPSCC surveillance, confirm whether an early positive TTMV-HPV DNA result persists before escalation, while recognizing that no randomized intervention result exists and ReACT 2.0 will not supply one. Use baseline swallow imaging to target prehabilitation, and interpret response-assessment PET by its rule-out performance rather than its sensitivity. COMPASS-SCC deserves validation, not immediate replacement of AJCC8/BWH. The new older-adult RT trial has no results, and setanaxib's exploratory survival signals do not reverse its negative primary endpoint.
