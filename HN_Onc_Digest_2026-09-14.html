1. Response-adapted surgery moves into prospective testing

3 findings
Hypothesis-generating

Three phase II studies deepen the perioperative signal, but only REMATCH prospectively changed the operation according to response. None establishes safe off-protocol resection reduction, surgery omission, or adjuvant de-intensification. This advances the prior response-adapted-surgery question.

  • REMATCH prospectively assigned reduced-volume surgery after ≥50% primary-tumor regression following neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy.▸

    Key conclusion — Among 42 selected deep responders, 2-year DFS was 90%; laryngeal preservation was 100% in laryngeal/hypopharyngeal cancers.

    Counterpoint — Single-arm selection and only five poor responders prevent a noninferiority conclusion.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · prospective single-arm phase II

    A real response rule, but no randomized surgical comparison

    Methods/scope: Fifty patients with resectable HPV-negative stage III–IVA HNSCC completed three cycles of penpulimab, nab-paclitaxel, and cisplatin. Imaging and laryngoscopy before cycle 3 assigned patients with ≥50% primary-tumor regression to reduced-volume surgery (RVS); lesser responders received standard-volume surgery. Everyone received risk-adapted postoperative RT.

    94%ORR
    90%2-year DFS in RVS
    42RVS patients
    0Grade 4–5 events

    Results: ORR was 94% (95% CI 84%–99%). At 34.0 months' median follow-up, per-protocol 2-year DFS was 83% (69%–91%). RVS patients (n=42) had 90% 2-year DFS and 100% laryngeal preservation; standard-volume patients (n=5) had 20% 2-year DFS. Grade 3 events occurred in 16% during neoadjuvant therapy and 15% around surgery, with no grade 4–5 events.

    Surgical interpretation: This is the most direct prospective evidence yet that a prespecified clinical-response rule can operationalize reduced-volume surgery while preserving near-term disease control in selected responders.

    Limitations/do not overread: Single center, no randomized surgical comparator, only five poor responders, post-treatment selection, and universal postoperative RT. Do not change the planned operation outside a protocol.

  • neoCHANCE-2 combined afatinib with tislelizumab and platinum/taxane before surgery.▸

    Key conclusion — Among 32 resected patients, pCR was 40.6%; no treatment-related surgical delay was reported.

    Counterpoint — Eight treated patients did not reach surgery, and the regimen has no comparator.

    Evidence briefPubMed/open journal record · single-arm phase II

    Substantial pathologic clearance; afatinib contribution unknown

    Methods/scope: Forty patients received two cycles of tislelizumab plus nab-paclitaxel/cisplatin, with afatinib between cycles, before resection; 32 proceeded to surgery.

    Results: Resected-patient pCR was 40.6% (95% CI 23.7%–59.4%); ORR in the treated cohort was 82.5% (67.2%–92.7%). One-year OS was 97.3% (92.2%–100%). Grade 3–4 lymphopenia occurred in 5/40 (12.5%); no grade 5 event or treatment-related surgical delay was reported.

    Surgical interpretation: The regimen supports comparative development with prospective measurement of resection extent, morbidity, and function.

    Limitations/do not overread: The pCR denominator is 32, follow-up is short, and there is no control isolating afatinib. Do not infer survival benefit or improved operability.

    Read the paper →

  • Chemotherapy-free camrelizumab plus apatinib produced MPR in 12/41 resectable HNSCC patients.▸

    Key conclusion — All 41 patients reached R0 resection without delay; grade ≥3 treatment-related events occurred in 2.4%.

    Counterpoint — The unequal one- versus two-cycle comparison was small and not statistically significant.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · exploratory phase II

    Pathologic activity with a low reported severe-toxicity rate

    Methods/scope: Forty-one stage II–IVA patients received one cycle (n=26) or two cycles (n=15) of camrelizumab plus apatinib before surgery.

    Results: Overall MPR was 29.3% (12/41), numerically 40.0% after two cycles versus 23.1% after one (p=.300). Grade ≥3 treatment-related events occurred in 1/41. MPR was associated with PD-L1 CPS ≥20 (50.0% versus 13.0%; adjusted p=.023) and circulating-tumor-cell clearance (87.5% versus 36.8%; adjusted p=.033).

    Surgical interpretation: A non-cytotoxic regimen produced measurable pathologic activity with all patients reaching surgery; CPS and CTC observations can inform prospective response-stratification work.

    Limitations/do not overread: Small exploratory study, unequal exposures, no standard-treatment control, biomarker multiplicity, and no mature survival. MPR associations are not validated selection rules.

    Read the paper →

Section counterpoint

Response is now being linked to surgical scope prospectively, but baseline extent, imaging/laryngoscopy, pathology, postoperative treatment, and salvageability are inseparable parts of the observed outcome.

2. ctDNA: high surveillance performance, unresolved decision impact

3 findings
Evidence boundary

Two cohorts and a meta-analysis sharpen expected test performance. They do not show that replacing standard surveillance, accelerating salvage, or omitting therapy based on ctDNA improves outcomes. The decision-impact gap remains the same one highlighted in the August 31 cycle.

  • A tissue-informed assay showed high surveillance performance across HPV-positive and HPV-negative HNSCC.▸

    Key conclusion — Per-test sensitivity was 95.2%, specificity 100%, PPV 100%, and NPV 99.4%.

    Counterpoint — Only 21 surveillance tests were recurrence-positive; pretreatment assay failure was 7.8%.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · retrospective tertiary-center cohort

    Promising extension beyond virally driven disease

    Methods/scope: A commercial tumor-informed assay was evaluated in 77 pretreatment and 107 surveillance patients. A personalized assay was detected pretreatment in 71/77 (92.2%); median time since treatment in the surveillance cohort was 18 months.

    92.2%Pretreatment detection
    95.2%Per-test sensitivity
    100%Observed specificity
    99.4%Per-test NPV

    Results: Per-test sensitivity was 95.2% (20/21; 95% CI 83.3%–100%), specificity 100% (157/157), PPV 100% (20/20), and NPV 99.4% (157/158). Per-patient sensitivity was 94.4% (17/18), specificity 100% (89/89), and NPV 98.9% (89/90).

    Surgical interpretation: Patient-specific assays may support prospective salvage-timing and postoperative-risk studies outside HPV-driven disease.

    Limitations/do not overread: Retrospective ordering/follow-up, commercial-assay selection, few recurrences, and 7.8% pretreatment failure. Perfect observed specificity does not establish a treatment rule.

  • Serial ctHPV DNA surveillance included 2,192 post-treatment tests in 232 baseline-positive patients.▸

    Key conclusion — Reappearance had 90.3% sensitivity and 97.9% specificity and preceded clinical recurrence in 58.1% of recurrent cases.

    Counterpoint — Only baseline-detectable patients were eligible; management benefit was not tested.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · prospective serial surveillance cohort

    One-month timing appears more informative than immediate testing

    Methods/scope: Investigators performed 2,928 assessments in 232 HPV-associated OPSCC/unknown-primary patients. Among 220 patients with radiologic and molecular response, 2,192 tests were performed in surveillance.

    Results: Reappearance showed 90.3% sensitivity, 97.9% specificity, 87.5% PPV, and 98.4% NPV. It preceded clinical recurrence in 58.1% of recurrent cases. Positivity one month after RT was associated with worse OS (HR 19.1; 95% CI 7.5–48.8) and DFS (HR 15.4; 6.7–35.4); the immediate post-treatment value was less informative.

    Surgical interpretation: A one-month value may be more useful than an immediate post-RT sample; persistent or returning signal should prompt protocolized evaluation, not automatic surgery.

    Limitations/do not overread: Baseline-positive selection, one-institution qPCR, no randomized management effect, and possible lead-time bias. High NPV partly reflects recurrence prevalence.

    Read the paper →

  • A 20-study meta-analysis produced more conservative ctHPV DNA accuracy than several single-center cohorts.▸

    Key conclusion — Across 3,505 tests, pooled sensitivity was 80.3% and specificity 95.5%.

    Counterpoint — Predictive values were modeled at 9.70% recurrence prevalence and will shift across practices.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · PRISMA systematic review/meta-analysis

    A useful calibration against best-case cohort estimates

    Methods/scope: Twenty studies met prespecified criteria, contributing 3,505 clinically correlated tests after curative treatment for HPV-positive OPSCC.

    Results: Pooled sensitivity was 80.3% (95% CI 70.9%–87.2%) and specificity 95.5% (90.9%–97.8%). At 9.70% recurrence prevalence, modeled NPV was 97.7% and PPV 66.0%. A positive test carried 130-fold higher odds of recurrence, with a wide 95% CI (56.6–298.7).

    Surgical interpretation: The pooled estimate reinforces confirmatory testing and imaging/pathologic correlation before salvage decisions.

    Limitations/do not overread: Heterogeneous assays, intervals, thresholds, and reference standards. Predictive values are prevalence-sensitive and do not prove outcome benefit.

    Read the paper →

Section counterpoint

Accuracy is not utility. A negative result can lower short-term recurrence probability, but a positive result still needs confirmation and neither result has a validated surgery-omission rule.

3. Surgery and reconstruction: measure function, not age alone

3 findings
Practice-relevant

This cycle adds a technical warning for optical margins and useful counseling evidence for free-flap and glossectomy recovery. None supports denying reconstruction or curative surgery on age or a single frailty score.

  • Surgical resection altered fluorescence-lifetime signatures differently in oral tongue, base of tongue, and tonsil.▸

    Key conclusion — Cancer/noncancer separability improved ex vivo at base of tongue but worsened after excision at oral tongue and tonsil.

    Counterpoint — This measured optical separation, not positive-margin or recurrence reduction.

    Evidence briefOpen full text · prospective intraoperative optical study

    In-vivo and ex-vivo algorithms are not interchangeable

    Methods/scope: A fiber-based fluorescence lifetime imaging system measured in-vivo and ex-vivo oral tongue, base-of-tongue, and tonsil tissue in 75 surgical patients.

    Results: Separability improved ex vivo at base of tongue (index 0.43 versus 0.15 in vivo) but fell after excision in oral tongue (0.18 versus 0.27) and tonsil (0.28 versus 0.46).

    Surgical interpretation: Margin algorithms require subsite- and workflow-specific validation for in-vivo guidance versus ex-vivo specimen assessment.

    Limitations/do not overread: Diagnostic-separability study, not a randomized margin trial; no evidence yet for fewer positive margins, re-resections, recurrences, or shorter operations.

    Read the open paper →

  • A systematic review linked frailty with recovery burden after head-and-neck free-flap reconstruction.▸

    Key conclusion — Frailty consistently predicted medical morbidity, longer stay, non-home discharge, and worse swallowing recovery.

    Counterpoint — Associations with surgical complications and short-term mortality were inconsistent.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · PRISMA systematic review

    Frailty is a planning signal, not a reconstruction veto

    Methods/scope: Eleven studies using validated frailty measures were included; eight were rated good quality by Newcastle–Ottawa criteria.

    Results: Frailty was consistently associated with medical complications, longer hospitalization, non-home discharge, impaired swallowing recovery, and longer enteral-nutrition dependence. Tools incorporating nutrition or sarcopenia outperformed frailty indices alone for postoperative complications.

    Surgical interpretation: Frailty screening can guide prehabilitation, discharge planning, and expectation setting before microvascular reconstruction.

    Limitations/do not overread: Heterogeneous instruments and outcomes, observational source studies, and no pooled effect magnitudes in the abstract. A positive screen should trigger fuller assessment, not automatic de-escalation.

    Read the paper →

  • A 206-patient glossectomy cohort found slower early recovery with advanced age but few durable group differences.▸

    Key conclusion — Most age-group functional differences resolved by one to two years; tube and tracheostomy dependence did not differ.

    Counterpoint — Comparable outcomes may partly reflect selection of fitter older adults.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · retrospective cohort

    Age changes the trajectory more than the destination

    Methods/scope: Patients treated for oral-tongue cancer from 2016–2024 were grouped as younger than 60 (n=92), 60–70 (n=61), or older than 70 (n=53); 109 (53%) had free-flap reconstruction.

    Results: Older patients had more moderate-to-severe labial range-of-motion and strength deficits at one month. Most differences resolved by one to two years, though labial deficits persisted at two years (p=.048). Gastrostomy/tracheostomy dependence, stay, readmission, reoperation, revision, and FACE-Q scores did not differ.

    Surgical interpretation: Counsel older patients about a potentially slower early trajectory without assuming age predicts feeding-tube or tracheostomy dependence.

    Limitations/do not overread: Retrospective selection, single-center rehabilitation, multiple comparisons, and limited defect/reconstruction matching. Age alone should not determine candidacy.

    Read the paper →

Section counterpoint

Frailty and age are related but not interchangeable. Use a positive frailty screen to plan optimization, rehabilitation, and discharge support—not as an automatic exclusion from reconstruction.

4. Radiation: less toxicity is plausible; omission still needs trials

4 findings
Randomized toxicity signal

Randomized postoperative data favor protons for acute oral toxicity in unilateral fields. RT avoidance after induction therapy and low-neck omission remain promising single-arm or retrospective strategies; a new phase III asks whether hyperfractionation can replace cisplatin.

  • Neoadjuvant TPF followed by transoral surgery allowed 91% of selected HPV-associated OPSCC patients to avoid postoperative RT/CRT.▸

    Key conclusion — Centrally reviewed pCR was 65.6%, but the study missed its primary endpoint.

    Counterpoint — Thirty-two eligible patients, no comparator, and no mature recurrence estimates.

    Evidence briefOpen full text · multicenter prospective phase II

    High pCR and RT avoidance, without practice-defining control data

    Methods/scope: Thirty-two eligible patients received three cycles of docetaxel/cisplatin/5-FU followed by transoral surgery. The abstract says 30 underwent transoral surgery but reports R0 resection in 31, an unresolved internal denominator inconsistency.

    Results: Centrally reviewed pCR was 65.6%, virologic complete response 64.5%, and 91% avoided postoperative RT/chemoradiation. QOL returned to baseline or improved within one year; 23 patients had undetectable ctHPV DNA after chemotherapy.

    Surgical interpretation: A preplanned induction-plus-surgery pathway can spare most selected patients postoperative radiation, making mature control and swallowing data decisive.

    Limitations/do not overread: Primary endpoint not met, small cohort, no control arm, conflicting surgery/R0 denominators, and no mature survival/recurrence counts. Do not adopt TPF-driven RT omission outside a trial.

    Read the open paper →

  • Randomized phase II data compared protons with IMRT for unilateral postoperative head-and-neck treatment.▸

    Key conclusion — Grade ≥2 mucositis was 7.4% versus 22.7%; grade ≥2 dysgeusia was 9.3% versus 31.8%.

    Counterpoint — The study was not powered to prove equivalent disease control.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · multicenter randomized phase II

    Less acute oral toxicity in a selected unilateral setting

    Methods/scope: Ninety-eight analyzable patients with resected, well-lateralized cancers received unilateral adjuvant proton therapy (n=54) or IMRT (n=44), 60 Gy/30 fractions with 54 Gy elective treatment.

    7.4%Mucositis with protons
    22.7%Mucositis with IMRT
    9.3%Dysgeusia with protons

    Results: Acute grade ≥2 mucositis was 7.4% versus 22.7% (p=.03); grade ≥2 dysgeusia 9.3% versus 31.8% (p=.005). Grade ≥3 dermatitis and grade ≥2 dysphagia were similar. At 27.2 months, no late grade 3 events occurred and three-year local control, PFS, and OS did not differ.

    Surgical interpretation: For unilateral postoperative fields, protons can reduce specific acute oral toxicities; weigh benefit against access, plan quality, travel, and cost.

    Limitations/do not overread: Phase II sample, unequal analyzable groups, wide patient-reported-outcome CIs, and limited power for disease-control equivalence.

    Read the paper →

  • Contralateral low-neck sparing was associated with lower esophageal-inlet dose and less feeding-tube placement.▸

    Key conclusion — Feeding-tube placement was 22% with upper-neck-only versus 57% with whole-neck treatment.

    Counterpoint — Retrospective cohort of 52 patients with few oncologic events.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · single-institution retrospective cohort

    Dose–function association needs prospective validation

    Methods/scope: Fifty-two p16-positive OPSCC patients with pre/post-treatment swallow studies received contralateral elective upper-neck irradiation (n=28) or whole-neck irradiation (n=24).

    Results: Esophageal-inlet muscle dose was 29.8 versus 36.1 Gy (p=.011). Feeding-tube placement during/after chemoradiation was 22% versus 57% (p=.006); adjusted one-year HR was 0.25 (p=.006). Pneumonia, dilation, and recurrence did not differ, and no relapse occurred in the omitted contralateral low neck.

    Surgical interpretation: The anatomic dose–function link merits prospective validation and planning discussion in carefully selected lateralized disease.

    Limitations/do not overread: Retrospective allocation, 52 patients, few failures, and no HR confidence interval in the abstract. No observed omitted-neck relapse does not prove safety.

    Read the paper →

  • HYTC will test radiation alone at 81.6 Gy in twice-daily fractions against standard-dose RT plus high-dose cisplatin.▸

    Key conclusion — The international randomized noninferiority trial plans 604 patients and seven-year OS follow-up.

    Counterpoint — Recruiting registry with no efficacy or safety results.

    Evidence briefClinicalTrials.gov · first posted Sep 3 · no results

    A phase III cisplatin-substitution question

    Methods/scope: The open-label trial compares 70 Gy/35 fractions over seven weeks plus high-dose cisplatin with 81.6 Gy/68 twice-daily fractions over seven weeks.

    Results: The primary endpoint is seven-year OS. Locoregional failure, DFS, distant metastasis, clinician- and patient-reported toxicity, hearing, swallowing-related QOL, and speech recognition are secondary.

    Surgical interpretation: If completed, HYTC could answer whether intensified fractionation preserves survival while avoiding cisplatin morbidity in locally advanced disease.

    Limitations/do not overread: No outcomes, long completion horizon, open-label design, and limited registry detail for generalizing eligibility. Do not substitute hyperfractionation based on registration alone.

    View the registry record →

Section counterpoint

The only randomized completed evidence here concerns modality and acute toxicity—not omission. The avoidance, volume-sparing, and hyperfractionation strategies should remain protocol-bound.

5. Cutaneous SCC: selective Level IA surgery and late PD-1 toxicity

2 findings
Selective application

Level IA metastasis was uncommon in a parotid-region cSCC cohort, supporting selective rather than routine inclusion in a clinically negative neck. Longer cemiplimab follow-up found late toxicity uncommon, but the cohort was not head-and-neck-specific.

  • Level IA involvement was uncommon among 131 patients undergoing parotidectomy and neck dissection for advanced cSCC.▸

    Key conclusion — Four patients had Level IA disease (3.6%); the occult rate was 0% (95% CI 0%–3.3%).

    Counterpoint — Only four events occurred across a 25-year retrospective cohort.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · retrospective surgical cohort

    Selective omission appears plausible in a clinically negative neck

    Methods/scope: The cohort included 110 patients with parotid metastases and 21 with preauricular cSCC treated from 2000–2025.

    Results: Level IA disease occurred in 4/131 (3.6%; 95% CI 1%–9%); occult involvement was 0% (95% CI 0%–3.3%). Involvement tracked with pathologic nodal status, nodal burden, and extent of dissection.

    Surgical interpretation: Consider Level IA omission in a clinically negative, lower-burden neck; the findings also support the oncologic plausibility of a submental-island flap in selected patients.

    Limitations/do not overread: Retrospective network cohort, only four events, dissection-selection bias, and wide CIs. High nodal burden should temper omission; flap strategies were not compared.

    Read the paper →

  • Longer follow-up of 134 cemiplimab-treated advanced cSCC patients focused on toxicities beginning after six months.▸

    Key conclusion — Late treatment-related events occurred in 11.2%; all resolved after discontinuation and none became chronic.

    Counterpoint — The abstract provides no head-and-neck-specific subgroup.

    Evidence briefStructured abstract · Italian expanded-access cohort

    Late immune toxicity was uncommon but not absent

    Methods/scope: Late toxicities were defined as treatment-related events beginning at least six months after cemiplimab initiation in 134 advanced cSCC patients.

    Results: ORR was 58.9%, disease-control rate 72.4%, median PFS nine months, and median OS 21 months. Fifteen patients (11.2%) had a late event; median onset was 12 months (range 6–23), two stopped therapy, all events resolved, and none became chronic.

    Surgical interpretation: Delayed toxicity remains relevant during prolonged response and when timing salvage or reconstruction; surveillance should extend beyond the early treatment window.

    Limitations/do not overread: Nonrandomized expanded-access population, no head-and-neck subgroup, incomplete toxicity granularity, and possible undercapture. Response is not comparative efficacy.

    Read the paper →

Section counterpoint

The surgical result is anatomy- and burden-dependent, while the systemic result lacks a head-and-neck subset. Neither should be generalized beyond its observed population.

6. Systemic pipeline: PD-1/EGFR activity and a ficerafusp dosing trial

2 findings
Pipeline

Dual PD-1/EGFR blockade produced encouraging response in a small phase Ib cohort. FORTIFI-FLEX is not a registrational efficacy comparison; it randomizes only nonprogressors after shared loading. It extends the baseline ficerafusp watch.

  • Finotonlimab plus the EGFR antibody SCT200 showed activity in 41 recurrent/metastatic HNSCC patients.▸

    Key conclusion — ORR was 56.7% in 30 systemic-therapy-naïve patients and 27.3% in 11 previously treated patients.

    Counterpoint — Small, uncontrolled phase Ib cohorts cannot support cross-trial efficacy comparisons.

    Evidence briefOpen full text · prospective multicenter phase Ib

    Encouraging PD-1/EGFR signal with familiar EGFR toxicity

    Methods/scope: Patients received finotonlimab 200 mg every three weeks plus SCT200 weekly for 12 weeks, then every two weeks.

    Results: In 11 patients previously treated with platinum and checkpoint inhibition, ORR was 27.3%, median PFS 5.8 months, and OS 10.6 months. In 30 systemic-therapy-naïve patients, ORR was 56.7%, median PFS 9.6 months, and estimated median OS 13.6 months. Common treatment-related events were hypomagnesemia (63.4%), rash (41.5%), and acneiform dermatitis (36.6%).

    Surgical interpretation: The signal may matter for unresectable recurrence or conversion-to-local-therapy research, but needs a randomized comparator and defined post-response local management.

    Limitations/do not overread: Phase Ib, small heterogeneous cohorts, no control, limited CIs, and immature survival. Do not compare directly with pembrolizumab/cetuximab trials.

    Read the open paper →

  • FORTIFI-FLEX will compare less-frequent with continuous-weekly ficerafusp maintenance after shared combination loading.▸

    Key conclusion — All 160 planned patients first receive 12 weeks of ficerafusp plus pembrolizumab; only nonprogressors randomize.

    Counterpoint — No pembrolizumab-alone control and no posted outcomes.

    Evidence briefClinicalTrials.gov · first posted Sep 8 · no results

    A maintenance-frequency study, not an efficacy comparison

    Methods/scope: First-line PD-L1 CPS ≥1 recurrent/metastatic HNSCC receives ficerafusp 1,500 mg weekly plus pembrolizumab 200 mg every three weeks for 12 weeks. Nonprogressors then randomize 2:1 to ficerafusp 2,250 mg every three weeks or continued 1,500 mg weekly, both with pembrolizumab.

    Results: The primary outcome is the proportion alive and progression-free about nine months into maintenance. ORR, PFS, OS, safety, exposure, and response depth are secondary.

    Surgical interpretation: The study may clarify whether the EGFR/TGF-β bifunctional agent can be delivered less frequently after initial control, reducing prolonged treatment burden.

    Limitations/do not overread: Shared loading, responder enrichment, and no pembrolizumab-alone arm mean the design cannot establish incremental combination efficacy.

    View the registry record →

Section counterpoint

Both signals require design-aware reading: the completed study is uncontrolled, while the new protocol cannot isolate the combination's incremental efficacy.

Bottom line for tumor board

This cycle strengthens three conversations without changing standard surgery: prospectively defined response-adapted resection, assay- and time-specific ctDNA surveillance, and treatment planning that measures functional burden. REMATCH is the most surgically provocative result, but its single-arm selection and universal risk-adapted postoperative RT make off-trial resection reduction premature. The most immediately actionable randomized signal is reduced acute mucositis and dysgeusia with protons in unilateral postoperative fields; access and cost still matter. Capture baseline surgical intent before neoadjuvant treatment, document why operative scope changes, measure swallowing and discharge risk rather than using age alone, and treat every registry entry here as a trial opportunity—not evidence of efficacy.
